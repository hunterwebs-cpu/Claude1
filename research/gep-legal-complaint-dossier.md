## 3. The firm's own published complaints procedure

Published at: **https://www.geplegal.co.uk/help/complaints/**

**Who to complain to:** The firm names **Fiona Griffiths** (SRA register: Fiona Caroline Griffiths, solicitor, also listed under both SRA 534834 and 535234) as the **Complaints Manager**.

**Stated process:**
1. **Informal first step (optional):** raise the concern directly with the person handling the matter (here, Natasja Jones) to try to resolve it informally.
2. **Formal complaint:** put the complaint in writing to Fiona Griffiths. Verbal complaints are also accepted, in which case the firm states "a full note specifying the complaint will be taken." A Complaints Resolution form may be offered.
3. **Acknowledgement:** Fiona Griffiths will write to acknowledge receipt, and — if the matter cannot be dealt with immediately — will confirm that a substantive response will be given **within 14 days** of that acknowledgement letter. If circumstances prevent that, the firm says it will send an explanation of the delay.
4. **Investigation:** the case file is passed to Fiona Griffiths; the fee earner concerned (Natasja Jones, on this matter) may be asked for a synopsis/chronology and their own account of events.
5. **Outcome:** Fiona Griffiths considers everything and issues written findings — potentially including an apology, a reduction in fees, or other redress if the complaint is upheld.
6. **External escalation (as stated on the firm's own page):** the firm's own complaints page itself signposts the Legal Ombudsman (within 6 months of the firm's final response, or within 1 year of the issue) and the SRA (for dishonesty, mishandling of funds, or unfair treatment) — i.e., the firm's published procedure already anticipates and points toward exactly the escalation ladder set out in the general memo.

**Practical implication for this matter:** because the beneficiary is not the firm's client (the executor is), the firm may take the position that it owes the beneficiary less than it owes the executor — but the SRA Code of Conduct's complaints-handling obligation (para 8.2, per the general memo) is not limited to clients, and the Legal Ombudsman has explicitly said it will consider probate complaints from beneficiaries even though they are not the client. A complaint from the beneficiary to Fiona Griffiths, referencing prolonged non-response from Natasja Jones and citing the firm's own 14-day acknowledgement standard, is squarely within what the firm's own published procedure contemplates.

**Time limits (as set out in the general memo, reconfirmed here):**
- The firm gets **8 weeks** from a formal complaint to give its own final response before LeO will normally open a case (if 8 weeks pass with nothing at all, the beneficiary can go to LeO without waiting further).
- After the firm's final response (or after the 8-week point), the complaint must reach LeO within **6 months** of that final response.
- In any event, no more than **1 year** from the act/omission complained of, or from when the beneficiary reasonably ought to have known there was cause for complaint.

**Updated 2026 processing-time reality (worth knowing going in, not a reason to delay filing):** the Ombudsman's own site currently states initial checks are taking **up to 12 weeks**, and that it is currently taking **9–12 months** for most cases to be accepted and an investigation to begin, with investigations themselves then taking a further 3–12 months. This is materially slower than older published guidance and should be factored into expectations — filing promptly (once the 8-week/final-response trigger is met) matters precisely because the queue itself is now long, not because there's any reason to wait.

### Step 3 — SRA (only if a genuine conduct issue emerges, not simply for slow service)

- Reporting page: https://www.sra.org.uk/consumers/problems/report-solicitor/ (a downloadable report form; no dedicated online complaint portal found)
- General contact centre: https://www.sra.org.uk/contact-us/ ; phone 0370 606 2555 (UK) / +44 (0)121 820 2250 (international)
- Postal address: Solicitors Regulation Authority, The Cube, 199 Wharfside Street, Birmingham, B1 1RN

The SRA's own site is explicit that it does **not** handle poor-service complaints (delay, communication failures, billing disputes) and cannot order an apology or compensation — that is exactly what the Legal Ombudsman is for. The SRA is the right channel only if something more than delay is found (e.g. dishonesty, client money mishandling, or a firm ignoring its regulatory obligations altogether) — nothing found in this research suggests that is the case here; it is listed for completeness per the escalation ladder in the general memo.
