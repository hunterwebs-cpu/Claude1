**ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT — PRIVILEGED AND CONFIDENTIAL**
**Internal assessment — not for filing**

**Matter:** In re Steven Bradley Mell, Ch. 7, D.N.J. No. 26-16834 (EOH) — fee dispute re MS&B Invoice 264503
**Date:** July 29, 2026

---

## SHORT ANSWER

**Yes — and the documents put the client in a stronger position than the "core vs. ancillary" framing suggested.** The client's reading (flat $5,000 covers the direct Chapter 7 filing work; only genuinely ancillary matters are hourly) is not merely a defensible interpretation — it tracks the retainer's **own plain "in sum" sentence**, and the invoice bills hourly for work that sentence says the flat fee already bought. Two independent grounds each shrink the $2,658.40 balance dramatically:

1. **Scope:** the invoice charges hourly for petition/schedules/SOFA/§341 work that the flat fee expressly covers; and
2. **Timing:** ~68% of the fees predate the retainer entirely.

New Jersey law then loads the dice in the client's favor: retainers are construed **against the drafting firm** and read as a **reasonable client** would read them (*Cohen*), the client's understanding of scope is **admissible even if the firm calls its retainer "clear"** (*Conway*), and pre-agreement work is at most recoverable in **quantum meruit at reasonable value — not the firm's hourly rate** (*Starkey*).

| Bucket | Covers | Paid how |

| **1 — Flat $5,000** | prepare + file **petition, schedules, SOFA**; **§341 prep + attendance** | flat, regardless of hours |
| **2 — "Hourly Services"** | **meetings / settlement discussions with an adversary re: claims** | hourly |
| **3 — Separate retainer** | **motions / adversary proceedings** | separate agreement (this is the $20K MTD retainer) |

The client's reading maps onto Bucket 1 almost exactly. The only soft spot: "trustee conferences" are not separately enumerated (the list is petition/schedules/SOFA/§341). But trustee document requests and §341 logistics are inherent in "preparing and filing" and "preparing for the §341 meeting," and any residual ambiguity is construed against the firm.

## III. NJ LAW APPLIED

**1. Construe against the firm; read as a reasonable client would.**
*Cohen v. Radio-Electronics Officers Union, Dist. Council 3*, 146 N.J. 140, 156 (1996) (binding): "A court should construe an agreement between a lawyer and a client 'as a reasonable person in the circumstances of the client would have construed it.' ... Those principles apply as readily to retainer agreements ...." Reaffirmed flatly in *Starkey v. Estate of Nicolaysen*, 172 N.J. 60, 67 (2002): "fee agreements between lawyers and clients will be construed against the lawyers." **The firm also bears the burden** of proving its reading is fair and reasonable (*Cohen*, 146 N.J. at 155–56).

**2. The client's understanding is admissible even if the firm calls the retainer "clear."**
*Conway v. 287 Corporate Center Assocs.*, 187 N.J. 259, 269 (2006), quoting *Atlantic N. Airlines v. Schwimmer*, 12 N.J. 293, 301–02 (1953): extrinsic evidence of circumstances "is always admissible in aid of the interpretation ... even when the contract on its face is free from ambiguity." So the client's understanding of what the flat fee covered — and industry custom that consumer Ch. 7 flat fees cover petition/schedules/§341 — comes in.

**3. Flat-fee scope ambiguity is the drafter's risk.** *Cohen* reasons two and three: lawyers "are more able than most clients to detect and repair omissions" and have a fiduciary duty to disclose scope and fee basis. A firm that wanted the filing work billed hourly had to say so; it didn't.

**4. Pre-agreement work → quantum meruit, not the contract hourly rate.**
*Starkey*, 172 N.J. at 67–68: a writing executed after work has begun is not "within a reasonable time" under **RPC 1.5(b)**; the firm "cannot benefit from failure to comply with ... RPC 1.5 by enforcing an agreement that violates that rule" (quoting *Vaccaro*, 303 N.J. Super. 201, 207 (App. Div. 1997)). Recovery for pre-signing work, if any, is limited to **reasonable value** (four-part quantum meruit test; firm's burden), not $775/$550/hr.

**5. RPC 1.5(a) reasonableness / good faith.** Charging hourly for flat-fee-covered work, billing to draft the retainer, and the face-of-invoice discrepancies (below) are all reasonableness concerns; the implied covenant of good faith bars using any billing discretion to swallow the benefit the flat fee bought.

## IV. FACE-OF-INVOICE RED FLAGS (credibility / leverage)

1. **$5,000 discrepancy on the bill itself:** page 1 Balance Due **$2,658.40** (after retainer credit) vs. page 4 Remittance **"BALANCE DUE THIS INVOICE $7,658.40"** (no credit shown).
2. **Charging to draft the fee agreement:** 5/21 entry, $37.50, "Prepare retainer agreement for Ch. 7."
3. **Unsigned/undated client signature block** ("Dated: May ___, 2026," signature line blank). If never signed, there is *no* written hourly agreement at all → everything is quantum meruit (worse for the firm). If accepted by paying the $5,000, the firm is bound by the flat-fee terms it wrote. Either way, no path to "hourly for filing work."
4. **Rate mismatch:** letter says paralegal $275/hr; invoice bills paralegal $125/hr (favorable to client, but shows the bill doesn't track the agreement).
5. **Matter caption "RE: Workout"** — not "Chapter 7"; the billing system treated this as a general hourly workout, consistent with billing everything hourly.

## V. THE FIRM'S BEST COUNTER (and why it's weak here)

The firm will argue this was a *contested, litigation-heavy* Chapter 7 (adversary MTD, state-court proceedings, nondischargeability exposure), so the extra work is properly hourly/separate. That argument has some force for the **nondischargeability and state-court/adversary slices** — but those items are (a) mostly **pre-agreement** (billed before any retainer existed), or (b) exactly the "motions/adversary proceedings" the retainer says require a **separate retainer** (the $20K MTD retainer). It does **not** reach the petition/schedules/SOFA/§341 drafting-and-filing work, which the flat fee covers no matter how complex the case became.
