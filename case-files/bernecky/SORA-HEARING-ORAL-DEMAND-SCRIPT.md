# Hearing Preparation Script — Oral Mingo Foundation Demand

**Matter:** People (NY SORA Board of Examiners) v. Jeffrey Bernecky — Risk-Level Classification Hearing, Monroe County Court (Hon. Karen Bailey Turner), September 11, 2026

**Prepared by:** Writer / drafting counsel

**Purpose of this document.** This is an internal preparation aid for whoever appears on Respondent's behalf at the September 11, 2026 hearing. It is not a court filing, is not to be filed or served, and should not be quoted to the Court as if it were the memorandum itself. Its purpose is narrow: to make sure the specific, fact-specific oral demand Point II of the Memorandum of Law previews is actually made on the record, in the right words, at the right moment, and that the record is properly preserved if the Court rules against Respondent. Everything in this script tracks Point II, Sections E and F, of the Memorandum of Law in Support of Respondent's Application for a Downward Departure ("Memorandum of Law"); it does not introduce any new argument.

**Why this matters.** A pre-hearing written demand, standing alone, is not reliable preservation. New York's preservation rule — which governs SORA appeals through CPLR 4017 and 5501(a)(3)-(4), because Correction Law § 168-n(3) routes SORA appeals through the CPLR's civil appellate articles — requires a specific, fact-by-fact objection made at the point the challenged evidence is actually offered or relied on, followed by a ruling. A general objection that never specifies its theory has been held unpreserved even in this exact proceeding type. The written memorandum sets up the argument; this script is what actually preserves it.

---

## 1. Before the Hearing

- Re-read Point II, Sections E and F, of the Memorandum of Law immediately before the hearing. The two challenged sentences and the *Mingo* standard should be fresh, not something counsel is reading cold off this script in the room.
- Confirm whether the Board or the District Attorney has provided any pre-hearing affirmation, offer of proof, or witness identification addressing either challenged sentence. If they have, the script below needs to be adapted on the spot to respond to whatever foundation was actually offered, rather than to the absence of any foundation at all.
- Have a copy of the Case Summary (Ex. A) at hand, opened to page 3, so the two challenged sentences can be pointed to directly rather than paraphrased from memory.

## 2. The Trigger Point

Make this demand when the Case Summary is offered, referenced, or relied upon by the Board or the District Attorney — whether that happens formally ("the People offer the Board's recommendation package") or informally (the Court or the District Attorney simply begins discussing the Case Summary's contents as if they were established). Do not wait for a formal offer-into-evidence moment that may never come in a SORA hearing's ordinary, paper-based practice. If the Case Summary is treated as part of the record without any formal offer, that is the trigger — speak up at that point.

## 3. The Demand — Suggested Language

Adapt to the actual flow of the hearing, but the substance below should be said, on the record, in counsel's own words:

> "Your Honor, before the Court relies on the Case Summary, Respondent specifically objects to two sentences in it and asks the Court to require foundation for each before giving either any weight.
>
> The first is the sentence stating that Respondent's prior conviction provides 'compelling evidence that he poses a serious risk to public safety that is not captured by scoring alone.' The second is the sentence stating that 'prior detection, sanction and treatment have not deterred his conduct.' Both appear at page 3 of the Case Summary. Neither is attributed to any page of either presentence report, to any probation officer's finding, or to any other source.
>
> Under *People v. Mingo*, 12 N.Y.3d 563, foundation for a challenged hearsay assertion can be established 'either through a pre-hearing affirmation, an offer of proof, or by testimony from a witness with knowledge of the internal procedures.' As to these two specific sentences, Respondent requests that the Court require the People to satisfy that standard before relying on either one, and Respondent objects to the Court relying on either sentence in the absence of that showing.
>
> This is not a request for a broader evidentiary hearing, and Respondent is not asking the Court to go beyond what SORA's existing statutory package provides. Respondent is asking the Court to apply *Mingo*'s own foundation framework to these two specific, identified assertions."

## 4. Requesting an Express Ruling

Do not let this demand pass without the Court actually ruling on it. If the Court does not respond directly, follow up:

> "Your Honor, I want to make sure the record reflects a ruling on this specific request — is the Court requiring foundation as to these two sentences, or declining to?"

Preservation under CPLR 4017 attaches "[a]t the time a ruling or order of the court is requested or made." A demand that is made but never actually ruled on is a materially weaker record than one where the Court's response — favorable or not — is captured on the transcript.

## 5. If the Court Grants the Demand

Note what method the People choose to satisfy foundation (affirmation, offer of proof, or testimony) and note it for the file. The choice of method belongs to the People, not to Respondent; do not object to the method chosen unless the specific showing offered is itself inadequate under *Mingo* on its own terms (for example, an affirmation from someone with no actual knowledge of how the Case Summary's characterizations were reached). If the showing offered is inadequate, renew the objection specifically as to that inadequacy, on the record, before the Court proceeds.

## 6. If the Court Denies the Demand — Preserving It for Appeal

If the Court declines to require foundation, state the following on the record, in counsel's own words, before moving on:

> "Your Honor, Respondent is preserving this objection for appellate review under CPLR 4017 and 5501. To be clear for the record: Respondent objected specifically to the Court's reliance on the two Case Summary sentences identified — the 'compelling evidence... not captured by scoring alone' sentence and the 'prior detection, sanction and treatment have not deterred his conduct' sentence — requested that the Court require foundation under *Mingo* before relying on either, and the Court has declined that request. This objection and this ruling are set out in full in Point II, Sections E and F, of Respondent's Memorandum of Law, filed with the Court in advance of this hearing, for the purpose of connecting the trial record and the appellate record on this point."

This does three things: it names the specific ruling being preserved, it ties the oral record back to the written Point II argument so an appellate court reviewing a cold transcript can find the fuller argument without guessing, and it uses the word "preserving" on the record, which removes any ambiguity about whether the objection was made for preservation purposes.

## 7. What Not to Do

- Do not make this demand as, or let it be characterized as, a request for "a full evidentiary hearing." That framing is directly foreclosed by *People v. Watts*, 2024 NY Slip Op 00926 (Ct. App.), which held that SORA's existing statutory package already provides constitutionally adequate process. Keep the demand narrow and tied to *Mingo*, exactly as scripted above.
- Do not let a general objection substitute for this specific one. If time is short, the two challenged sentences must still be named individually; a general objection that "the Case Summary is unreliable" risks the same fate as the unpreserved general objection in *People v. Gillotti* itself.
- Do not treat the pre-hearing written demand (Point II of the Memorandum of Law, and the separately filed Demand for Disclosure and Subpoena Duces Tecum) as self-executing. Both need to be renewed live, at the hearing, at the moment described in Section 2 above.
