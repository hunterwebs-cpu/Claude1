## ARGUMENT

### A. Correction Law § 168-n(3)'s Own Adjournment Clause Requires Adjournment Where There Is a Genuine Dispute Requiring Materials to Be Obtained

Correction Law § 168-n(3) provides: "Where there is a dispute between the parties concerning the determinations, the court shall adjourn the hearing as necessary to permit the sex offender or the district attorney to obtain materials relevant to the determinations from the state board of examiners of sex offenders or any state or local facility, hospital, institution, office, agency, department or division. Such materials may be obtained by subpoena if not voluntarily provided to the requesting party."

This clause is mandatory — "the court shall adjourn," not "may adjourn" — once a genuine dispute concerning the Board's determinations exists and materials relevant to that dispute must be obtained. Respondent's accompanying Demand for Disclosure and Subpoena Duces Tecum identifies exactly that dispute and demands exactly those materials. Adjournment is necessary here for the reason the statute itself contemplates: the demanded materials will not be in hand, let alone reviewed, by September 11, 2026, particularly to the extent production requires a subpoena rather than voluntary compliance.

### B. The General Adjournment-Discretion Standard Also Supports Adjournment

Independent of § 168-n(3)'s own mandatory clause, New York applies its ordinary adjournment-discretion standard to requests of this kind — whether the evidence sought is material, whether the request is made in good faith rather than for purposes of delay, and whether the need for adjournment results from the movant's own lack of diligence. New York applies this same, non-heightened standard in the SORA context specifically. See People v. Eldridge, 2024 NY Slip Op 05117 (App. Div., 2d Dept.) (citation to be confirmed) (reversing denial of an adjournment sought so newly consulted counsel could review documents that could affect the respondent's classification, and holding that an imminent release date was not a sufficient reason to deny the request).

The materials demanded here bear directly on the reliability of the Case Summary's factual assertions and, through them, on the risk-level determination itself; the request is made to permit counsel and Respondent a genuine opportunity to test the Board's Recommendation Package, not to delay the proceeding; and the need for the adjournment arises from the compressed timeline created by the recent passage of the counsel-status deadline and the volume of material that must still be obtained, not from any lack of diligence on Respondent's part.

### C. Newly Assigned Counsel Needs Both Time and the Demanded Records to Prepare

If the Court grants Respondent's Application for Assignment of Counsel, newly assigned counsel will need adequate time to become familiar with the record and to review whatever materials are produced or subpoenaed in response to the accompanying Demand for Disclosure before counsel can meaningfully represent Respondent at a contested classification hearing. An adjournment is necessary to make that preparation possible, rather than assigning counsel and demanding materials on a timeline that affords no realistic opportunity to use either before September 11, 2026.

### D. A Short Adjournment Causes No Identified Prejudice to the People's Notification Interest

The People's interest that ordinarily counsels against adjournment in a SORA proceeding is the public-safety and community-notification interest reflected in the statute's own hearing notice, which warns that failure to appear "may result in a longer period of registration or a higher level of community notification." That interest is not implicated in the same way by a short adjournment of an appearance Respondent is prepared to make, where — subject to the confirmation flagged above — Respondent's registration status and whereabouts would remain current and a matter of record throughout any adjournment period. Respondent offers this point for the Court's consideration and reiterates that it should not be treated as established until independently confirmed.
