## Format requirement (this is the point of the rewrite)

No essay per condition. For each objectionable term: a short label
identifying it, then a few tight, numbered reasons it exceeds the Judgment
or is otherwise defective. Concise. The document as a whole should read as
a clear, persuasive list-driven argument, not a law-review treatment of
every clause. Cut anything that restates a point already made.

## Standing tone rules (apply throughout, independent of format)

- No "Mr. Bernecky does not ask/challenge/dispute X... he asks/challenges Y"
  passive construction anywhere. State affirmatively what he IS doing and
  why, never lead with what he isn't doing.
- No conceding that some part of the federal government (short of an
  Article III court) could validly impose these restrictions if it were
  the one doing it. Only a court may create a restriction; who else holds
  or lacks the paper is not the point.
- No "the government's authority/best authority" framing — no motion has
  been filed and no response received. Frame *Jimenez* and any other
  adverse authority as authority the government is likely to rely on, not
  authority already advanced.
- Meet the government's best likely case (*Jimenez*) head-on, early, and
  affirmatively — not as an apology or a concession.
- Where the document's own inadequacy as professional/legal practice is
  relevant (e.g., a provider in this very district ignoring controlling
  Second Circuit precedent while purporting to keep Bernecky law-abiding),
  make the point pointedly. Forceful, not weak — but professional, not
  sarcastic.
- Do not hand a treating clinician (or "treatment staff") discretion over
  any standard the motion itself is arguing shouldn't be left to their
  discretion (e.g., what counts as an excused absence). Use ordinary,
  objective standards instead (illness, hospitalization, family emergency,
  car trouble, etc.).
- Never call the document a "contract" or "agreement" as the motion's own
  description. It may be scare-quoted once, early, to acknowledge that's
  the label the document/provider uses — then state plainly the motion
  refers to it as [Writer's choice of term — document/form/instrument/etc.]
  because it is not an agreement in the legally understood sense: no
  negotiation, no meeting of the minds, no opportunity to object, and no
  consideration flowing to Bernecky in exchange for anything.
- Do not isolate the challenge to the document's own "SPECIAL CONDITIONS"
  numbered section — pull objectionable terms from every section of the
  document (Requirements, Special Conditions, Code of Conduct,
  Confidentiality, Consequences).
- Note the *Browder* genealogy carefully: the Judgment's Special
  Conditions were found to be a near-verbatim copy of what Judge Vilardo
  entered on remand in *Browder* (W.D.N.Y., Mar. 14, 2018 remand order,
  three months before Bernecky's Judgment issued June 12, 2018 — this
  chronology has been client-confirmed as accurate). Use *Browder* to
  establish what the Judgment's template actually authorizes as a
  baseline — never frame this as a challenge to the Judgment or the
  sentencing judge's own condition. The challenge throughout is to the
  provider's document diverging from that baseline. Also note: Bernecky's
  posture differs from Browder's — Browder had a treatment agreement to
  object to at/near sentencing; this document didn't exist until Bernecky
  actually entered treatment, so this is necessarily an as-applied
  challenge under § 3583(e)(2), not a *Browder*-style facial/direct-appeal
  posture. Make that distinction explicit somewhere early.

## Objectionable terms (facts and authority — label/structure is the Writer's call)

**SC ¶ 8 / ¶ 4 — material ban.** "I will not purchase, possess, view,
produce, or use sexually stimulating material(s) of any kind as defined by
my treatment staff." Companion ¶ 4 requires pre-approval before receiving
"sexually explicit images or narratives." Key point: don't frame this as
fundamentally a pornography argument — "material(s) of any kind" is not
confined to expressive/pornographic content on its face. It plausibly
reaches ordinary legal adult items (sex toys, condoms, lubricant) with no
Judgment restriction and no connection to the conduct of conviction. The
Judgment restricts only child pornography (Special Condition (j)) — never
"legal adult" material of any kind. Authority: *Eaglin*, 913 F.3d 88 (2d
Cir. 2019) — struck a legal-adult-pornography ban that was tethered to a
statute and judge-imposed with findings; this term is untethered,
provider-imposed, no findings — a fortiori, and *Eaglin* at 100 answers any
"gateway" clinical theory directly. *Carlineo*, 998 F.3d 533, 536 (2d Cir.
2021), for vagueness ("reasonable person... speculate as to its meaning" —
this is a *Carlineo* quote, NOT *Reeves* — a prior misattribution was
already corrected in the current v5 draft, keep that correction). Adverse
authority to meet, not avoid: *Simmons*, 343 F.3d at 82, approvingly cites
*Phipps* and *Bee* upholding conditions using the exact phrase "sexually
stimulating" — these two cases are unread/unverified; either verify and
meet them or flag the gap honestly. Illustrative overbreadth examples
available if useful (use sparingly, not as a list to exhaust): a memoir
Bernecky may write describing the abuse he suffered; the PSR's own graphic
description of the images in his possession; Genesis's account of Lot and
his daughters; the film *The Prince of Tides*.

**SC ¶ 5 — platform pre-approval.** Requires treatment-staff approval
before using "any platforms including but not limited to (social media,
dating sites, email...)" — expressly includes email. The Judgment's
Special Condition (a) authorizes only notice and monitoring by the U.S.
Probation Office — no treatment-provider gatekeeper role over any platform,
let alone email. *Browder*, 866 F.3d at 514, found the identical defect
("required the approval not only of the Probation Office, but also of the
treatment team").

**SC ¶ 2 — partner disclosure/consent.** Requires disclosing all intimate
partners and producing "acceptable proof of informed consent... which
includes disclosure of my offense history in full" before a sexual
relationship. The Judgment's only third-party-notification provision,
Standard Condition 12, was already limited by *Boles*, 914 F.3d 95, 111-12
(2d Cir. 2019) (invalidated a comparable officer-discretion notification
condition) and the district's own Mar. 22, 2019 standing order requiring
the court, not an officer, to make any risk finding. ¶ 2 reinstates and
worsens that discarded structure with no determination by anyone at all.
*Reeves*, 591 F.3d 77, 82 (2d Cir. 2010), vacated a near-identical
partner-notification/offense-disclosure condition, holding it causes
"social isolation" and impairs rehabilitation — binding in-circuit
authority, use this rather than an unverified isolation claim. Also: no
real legal or clinical protocol assigns responsibility for obtaining a
third party's "informed consent" to the patient producing documentary
proof — that work belongs to the clinician in every real protocol (ATSA
2014 ed. 20.05, 22.07; 6th ed. 31.01) — cite at most 1-2 of these, per the
"prescribed dosage" instruction below.

**Consequences ¶ 2(vi) — undefined discharge trigger.** "[O]ther problem
behavior not outlined in this Treatment Contract, as determined in the
sole discretion of the staff of the Practice" triggers immediate discharge.
State the real mechanism plainly: discharge under this clause becomes a
violation report, which can mean a warrant, arrest, and revocation
proceeding, on a standard nobody can read in advance. *Matta*, 777 F.3d
116, 122 (2d Cir. 2015); *Carlineo*, 998 F.3d at 538; *Jimenez*, No.
24-1661, slip op. at 34 (2d Cir. Feb. 25, 2026, amended May 12, 2026) — the
government's own likely authority draws its line exactly here ("giving
[probation officers] the authority to decide whether [the defendant]
participates in treatment at all").

**SC ¶ 9 — minor/vulnerable-population contact.** Requires reporting
"accidental, incidental, or intentional" contact with anyone under 18,
"potential or former victims," "vulnerable populations (as defined by
treatment staff)." The Judgment's Special Condition (c) reaches only
"deliberate contact." Do not argue a private/public saving-construction
theory (research found it doesn't hold up — *Kunz* applied its charitable
construction to a Probation form, not just judicial orders). Instead: the
real test (*Browder*) is whether a term executes or contradicts the
Judgment — "accidental" is the textual opposite of "deliberate," so this
term fails regardless of who wrote it. "Vulnerable populations" is
independently vague/undefined, staff-graded, with no record support for
Bernecky having any issue with that category. Fifth Amendment point:
disclosing contact with a "potential victim" would itself trigger
mandatory reporting and create a link in a chain of evidence — *Von
Behren*, 822 F.3d 1139, 1146-47 (10th Cir. 2016) — cite Federal Rules of
Evidence 413 and 414 (NOT Rule 404 — those rules admit propensity evidence,
a stronger point). *Von Behren*'s own provider contract had a nearly
identical "potential victims" police-reporting clause, given independent
weight by the Tenth Circuit. Note the limit: *Von Behren* fn.9 excludes
questions relevant only to supervised-release status (names
pornography-viewing as its example) — keep this Fifth Amendment point off
SC ¶ 8, apply it here and to the disclosure/relapse clauses below instead.

**Requirements ¶ 1 / Code of Conduct ¶¶ 5-6 — compelled disclosure, no
privilege carve-out.** Requires "full acknowledgement," reports of "any
relapse in problematic behavior" within 24 hours, reports of "criminal
behaviors" beyond the offense of conviction — routed to Probation, with law
enforcement contact permitted. No carve-out anywhere for invoking the
Fifth Amendment. State this as Bernecky's affirmative, unconditional
position: he will invoke the privilege, he will not waive it under any
circumstance regardless of claimed therapeutic benefit, because treatment
providers are mandatory reporters and no immunity — from any
jurisdiction — has been offered. Separately, "problematic behavior" and
"relapse in problematic behavior" are themselves vague and undefined —
argue this independently of the Fifth Amendment point, under *Carlineo*.

**Code of Conduct ¶ 6 — duplicative, no treatment purpose.** Requires
reporting residence/employment changes and a long list of "risk factors"
to treatment staff. Bernecky already owes this reporting to his probation
officer under Standard Conditions 5/7 and Special Conditions (d)/(i) — this
clause duplicates an existing reporting relationship with no stated
treatment purpose and no timeline.

**Consequences ¶ 3(a) — Probation on the Treatment Team.** Nothing in
Special Condition (b) or (h) puts Probation on a body that decides
discharge — those conditions authorize supervising "the details of
participation," which *Matta* calls a minor detail a court may delegate;
sitting on the discharge-deciding body is not a detail, it's
decision-making authority the Judgment never gave away. Relief: Probation
is not a member of any treatment team; provider reporting to Probation is
limited to attendance, participation/progress, and concerns grounded in
actual clinical findings — no session notes, internal records, or raw data
without a court order. One clinical citation available if useful: ATSA's
current guidelines instruct that providers "do not attempt to assume the
roles of supervision officers or law enforcement agents," and require
providers to warn clients in writing that incriminating disclosures can
lead to prosecution — this document never gives that warning. (This point
was previously slated for Part IV rather than Part II — Writer's
discretion on placement.)
