## Notes on Part I.A (client read-through, round 3)

6. **Reframe the *Matta*/delegation paragraph as an *a fortiori* argument.**
   The client doesn't believe expressio unius has ever been squarely
   presented in an SR case, but the canon is applied elsewhere — contracts,
   and (client believes, needs checking) injunctions — and Scalia's
   interpretation-canons book treats it as a tool for evaluating legal
   writings generally, not just statutes. Frame the point as: if the canon
   applies with this force in [contracts/injunctions/elsewhere], it applies
   at least as strongly to a sentence. Worth actually pulling the injunction
   authority (the Hessick & Morley lead already flagged as unverified in
   item 3 above may be the same lead) and citing Scalia's *Reading Law* (or
   equivalent) for the general-applicability point.

7. **Simplify "Neither finding was available to be made."** Client: "a
   little confusing... I think simply saying nobody requested that an
   occupational condition be applied thus no finding was ever made." Current
   sentence: "Neither the government nor the Probation Office asked for an
   occupational restriction at sentencing, and no finding of either kind
   appears in the Judgment, in any Statement of Reasons, or in any filing by
   either party. Neither finding was available to be made." Rewrite along
   the client's simpler line — nobody asked, so nobody made a finding —
   rather than the "available to be made" framing.

8. **Fix the overstated "she told a private company" claim.** Current text:
   "She told a private company that it owed the Probation Office a duty to
   monitor its own employee." The client only knows what the PO told *him*
   (Jeff) — that the company owed such a duty. Client presumes a similar
   statement was made to the company but that is a presumption, not
   personal knowledge. Rewrite to reflect only what Bernecky was told
   directly, with the statement to the employer framed as inferred/
   presumed rather than asserted as fact.

9. **Need a cite for corporate Fourth Amendment rights.** The sentence "it
   holds its own Fourth Amendment rights in its own systems" needs actual
   supporting authority that American companies hold the same Fourth
   Amendment protections as individuals (in their own systems/premises).
   Find and cite one.

10. **Strike the *McDonald* sentence — no application to the computer-
    monitoring argument.** Flagged line: "The same limit governs the other
    private institution in this supervision: a court 'lacks authority to
    tell a private treatment provider how to enforce, or not enforce, their
    rules and regulations.' United States v. McDonald..." Client: "This has
    zero application to the argument regarding computers. so strike it."
    (Note: *McDonald* is presumably still wanted somewhere the treatment
    provider is actually at issue — e.g., Part II — just not here.)

11. **Rewrite the closing sentence of Part I.A's *Mike* paragraph.** Current:
    "That sequence is the one Mr. Bernecky asks this Court to complete —
    and to settle the question generally rather than patch it for one
    company, because [the next employer will ask what this one asked]."
    Client's proposed direction: something like — Given the probation
    officer here "reasonably believe[d] that the computer monitoring
    condition[]" applied to computers owned by third-party employers,
    Bernecky requests clarification by way of an order disclaiming that
    interpretation, and further requests the Court instruct Probation to
    notify the employer in writing [that it owes the Probation Office
    nothing]. Tie the *Mike* quote directly into the relief request rather
    than treating *Mike* as backdrop only.
