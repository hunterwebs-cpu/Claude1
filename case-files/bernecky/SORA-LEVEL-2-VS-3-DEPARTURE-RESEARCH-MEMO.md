## 3. ISSUES AND SUMMARY CONCLUSIONS

**Issue 1: What is the governing legal standard for a downward departure from an automatic RAI override, and who bears the burden of proof?**

*Summary conclusion:* A defendant seeking any downward departure under SORA — including a departure from an automatic override — must, as a matter of law, (1) identify a mitigating factor that tends to establish a lower likelihood of reoffense or danger to the community and that is of a kind or degree not adequately accounted for by the Guidelines, and (2) prove the facts supporting that factor by a preponderance of the evidence (not clear and convincing evidence). *People v. Gillotti*, 23 N.Y.3d 841 (2014), resolved a prior split among the Appellate Division departments and set this preponderance standard; only if that twofold showing is made does the court reach a third, discretionary step of weighing aggravating and mitigating factors. The People, separately, bear the burden of proving the applicability of an override itself — and the facts supporting the Board's/People's recommended risk level generally — by clear and convincing evidence. Once an override applies, the court has no discretion to refuse to apply it; the only avenue for Bernecky is departure.

**Issue 2: Have New York courts granted downward departures specifically from Override 1 on facts resembling Bernecky's?**

*Summary conclusion:* My research located a consistent pattern of New York appellate decisions **affirming** the application of Override 1 and **denying** downward departure requests, including on fact patterns that share real similarities with Bernecky's (a point score that would itself yield Level 1 or Level 2, an offender advancing treatment/family-support/age arguments). I did not locate any case — despite a genuinely diligent search — in which a New York court granted a downward departure from Override 1 specifically on a record resembling Bernecky's. That absence is itself a significant, reportable research finding, not merely a gap: it suggests departures from Override 1 are rare and disfavored, and that Bernecky's showing will need to be unusually strong to succeed. This is discussed fully, with the adverse cases, in Section 5.

**Issue 3: How have courts treated the Board's reliance on a categorical Position Statement versus individualized findings when denying departure?**

*Summary conclusion:* The one Board "Position Statement" that appears repeatedly in the case law I could locate is not the June 1, 2012 statement Bernecky's Case Summary invokes for its Level III recommendation — it is a *different* Board position statement (issued after *People v. Johnson*, 11 N.Y.3d 416 (2008)) addressing whether points should be assessed under RAI Factors 3 (number of victims) and 7 (relationship with victim) in child-pornography cases, not the override analysis at all. *Gillotti* held that this factor-3/7 position statement does *not* bar a SORA court from assessing points under those factors — i.e., the Board's own position statement was held not to control the court's individualized analysis. If the "June 1, 2012" statement Bernecky's Case Summary cites is in fact this same factor-3/7 statement (misapplied here to justify an override-based Level III finding, a subject it does not appear to address), that is a real, arguable weakness in the Board's stated reasoning worth pressing at the hearing. This cannot be confirmed without the actual document, which is not in the client's file. See Sections 5.C and 7.

**Issue 4: Other authority for and against Bernecky's position.**

*Summary conclusion:* The strongest facts for Bernecky are the size of the numerical gap between his score (70) and the override-free Level 2/3 thresholds (75/110), the absence of any violence or forcible-compulsion scoring, the non-contact nature of the instant offense, and his documented, continuous sex-offender-treatment engagement. The strongest facts against him are that his own Case Summary preemptively and specifically rejects the treatment argument ("prior detection, sanction and treatment have not deterred his conduct"), that he committed the instant offense *while actively in phase-two treatment* and under supervision from a prior sex-crime conviction, and that the governing case law treats generic "responded well to treatment" or "family support" showings as insufficient absent something exceptional. On the present record, I assess Bernecky's departure argument as **legally viable but facing a real uphill climb** — not frivolous, but not favored by the pattern of authority I found. See Section 6 for the full assessment and confidence level.

## 5. DISCUSSION / ANALYSIS

### A. Governing standard for downward departure from an automatic override

**Statutory and regulatory framework.** SORA risk-level determinations are governed by Correction Law Article 6-C (§ 168 et seq.). The Board of Examiners develops the Risk Assessment Guidelines and Commentary under Correction Law § 168-l(5); the RAI implements those Guidelines. Correction Law § 168-n(3) governs the hearing itself and the evidence the court may consider. I was not able to obtain and read the Guidelines and Commentary document directly (see Section 2, above, and Section 8) — the override framework described below is reconstructed from the RAI form itself (which I *did* read directly, in the client's transcription) and from consistent secondary-source characterization, not from the Guidelines and Commentary's own text.

**The override mechanism.** The RAI's own printed language — which I read directly in the client's transcription and can quote with confidence, since it is a primary document in this specific case — states that four circumstances, "if any override is circled," make "the offender... presumptively a Level 3": (1) a prior felony conviction for a sex crime, (2) infliction of serious physical injury or death, (3) a recent threat to reoffend, or (4) a clinical assessment of an abnormality decreasing impulse control. Secondary-source characterization consistent across CaseMine's commentary on this exact override and the ACP Guide (read in full, discussed below) confirms that once the People establish an override's applicability by clear and convincing evidence, application of the override is automatic and non-discretionary — the SORA court cannot decline to apply it. The only route to a lower classification once an override is established is a downward *departure*.

**The departure standard and burden of proof: *People v. Gillotti*.** *People v. Gillotti*, 23 N.Y.3d 841, 994 N.Y.S.2d 1, 18 N.E.3d 701, 2014 NY Slip Op 04117 (2014). **Source and verification status: I was unable to read this opinion directly (nycourts.gov, justia.com, and leagle.com all returned 403 errors). The following is reconstructed from FindLaw's and CaseMine's secondary summaries of the opinion, corroborated independently by an Assigned Counsel Program practice guide (Thomas P. Franczyk, "A Few Words on the Sex Offender Registration Act (SORA)," April 1, 2022, at pp. 12–13, which I *did* read in full directly) — all three sources agree on the substance below, but none of the language attributed to the Court is confirmed against the actual opinion text, and none of it should be placed in quotation marks in a filing without that confirmation.**

Per this secondary-source reconstruction, *Gillotti* resolved a split among the Appellate Division departments over what standard of proof governs a defendant's downward-departure application, holding that a defendant must prove the facts supporting a departure by a **preponderance of the evidence** — not the higher clear-and-convincing standard the Fourth Department itself had applied to Gillotti's own case before the Court of Appeals corrected it. This is consistent with the general civil-proceeding principle that a party seeking relief need only meet a preponderance standard, and stands in contrast to the People's own clear-and-convincing burden on establishing the risk level in the first instance (also reflected in the ACP Guide, p. 3, and consistent with Correction Law § 168-n(3)'s clear-and-convincing standard for the Board's/People's recommended classification).

The reconstructed *Gillotti* framework for evaluating any departure request — override-based or points-based — proceeds in three steps: **first**, a legal question of whether the proffered circumstance is "of a kind or to a degree" not adequately accounted for by the Guidelines; **second**, a factual question of whether the movant has proven the circumstance's existence by a preponderance of the evidence; **third**, if both are satisfied, a discretionary weighing of aggravating and mitigating factors under the totality of circumstances. This three-step structure is corroborated by the two-prong articulation that recurs, in materially identical language, across multiple independent secondary sources describing *People v. Wyatt*, 89 A.D.3d 112 (2d Dep't 2011), and *People v. Curry*, 158 A.D.3d 52 (2d Dep't 2017) — both consistently described as requiring the offender to (1) identify, as a matter of law, an appropriate mitigating factor "which tends to establish a lower likelihood of reoffense or danger to the community and is of a kind, or to a degree, that is otherwise not adequately taken into account by the Guidelines," and (2) establish the supporting facts by a preponderance of the evidence — and by the Fourth Department's own articulation of the identical test in *People v. Edwards*, 2021 NY Slip Op 07359 (4th Dep't Dec. 23, 2021), citing *People v. Sanders*, 196 A.D.3d 1066 (4th Dep't 2021), as reported in the ACP Guide (p. 3), which I read directly. Because Monroe County sits within the Fourth Judicial Department for appellate purposes, *Edwards*/*Sanders* is the most directly governing articulation of the test Bernecky must meet, and it matches the framework described above.

**Confirmation this standard applies to override-based departures specifically, not just points-based ones.** This is important because Bernecky's presumptive Level 3 arises from an override, not from his point score (his point score alone would be Level 1). The case most directly confirming the *same* departure standard governs override-based presumptive levels is *People v. Gunter*, discussed in Section 5.B below — a case in which a court expressly applied the *Gillotti*/*Curry*-line preponderance standard to a departure request from an Override 1 presumptive Level 3, and expressly held that "the fact the offender's point score alone would have yielded a lower presumptive level is not itself a valid mitigating factor," because the override "applies irrespective of the points scored on the risk assessment instrument." That holding directly forecloses what would otherwise be Bernecky's single most intuitively appealing argument — that 70 points is far below even the Level 2 threshold of 75 — as a standalone basis for departure. It can still be part of the overall evidentiary picture, but it cannot carry the departure argument by itself.

**Good-law status:** Multiple 2024–2025 secondary-source-reported decisions (*People v. D.W.* (2024), *People v. Wagers* (2025), *People v. Santos* (2025)) continue to cite and apply *Gillotti*'s preponderance standard without qualification, and I found no indication in any source consulted that *Gillotti*, *Wyatt*, *Curry*, *Edwards*, or *Sanders* has been overruled, reversed, or negatively treated. I could not run an actual citator (Shepard's/KeyCite) — this assessment is based on the absence of contrary signals across a reasonably diligent multi-source search, not on citator confirmation, and should be independently Shepardized/KeyCited before filing. See Section 8.

### B. Has any New York court granted a downward departure from Override 1 on facts resembling Bernecky's?

This was the central factual question of this research assignment, and I searched it exhaustively, using varied search terms across multiple sessions (departure grants generally, Fourth Department specifically, treatment-response departures specifically, child-pornography-specific departures, and direct case-name guesses). **I did not find an affirmative example.** What I found instead is a consistent line of adverse authority — courts affirming Override 1's application and denying departure, including on facts that share real similarities to Bernecky's. I report the adverse authority in full, per this assignment's explicit instruction to surface authority that cuts against the client's position rather than let it be discovered later.

**Reliability caveat specific to this section:** The AI-generated search summaries I relied on for these Appellate Division cases proved demonstrably unreliable on at least one occasion during this research — an early search summary asserted that *People v. Adams* (2023 NY Slip Op 02813) *granted* a downward departure to Level 2; a second, independent query on the same case reported the opposite (departure denied, Level 3 affirmed), and a third query confirmed the denial with additional procedural detail. I resolved the conflict in favor of the two consistent, more detailed queries, but this episode is a concrete demonstration of why the Writer must pull actual slip opinions — not search-engine summaries — before citing any of the following in a filed document.

**1. *People v. Gunter*, 2023 NY Slip Op 03247 (App. Div., 2d Dep't, June 14, 2023) — most directly on point.** Per secondary-source reconstruction: Gunter had an override-triggering 1998 conviction for attempted rape in the first degree and sexual abuse in the first degree; the current conviction (upon guilty plea) was sexual abuse in the first degree. His RAI point score was **80** — which, before the override, would itself have yielded a *presumptive Level 2*. The court rejected each of his proffered mitigating factors: (a) the pre-override presumptive Level 2 designation itself — rejected as a matter of law, because the override "applies irrespective of the points scored"; (b) family support and housing — rejected for lack of documentary/testimonial evidentiary support and failure to connect the factor to reduced reoffense risk; (c) treatment response — rejected because the defendant did not establish an "exceptional" response by a preponderance of the evidence; (d) age and health — rejected for failure of proof. The Second Department affirmed the Level 3 designation, describing downward departures as "the exception... not the rule." **This case is squarely adverse and squarely instructive**: it shows a court applying the identical override-based departure framework Bernecky will face, rejecting nearly the identical menu of arguments (a below-override point total, treatment, general good conduct) that Bernecky's papers will likely need to present, and it establishes that "treatment response," standing alone, must be shown to be *exceptional*, not merely present, to succeed.

**2. *People v. Adams*, 2023 NY Slip Op 02813 (App. Div., 2d Dep't, Oct. 25, 2023).** Per two consistent secondary-source queries (after resolving the conflict noted above): the defendant pled guilty to promoting a sexual performance by a child as a sexually motivated felony, stemming from possession of child-pornography images; the Supreme Court (Kings County) denied his downward-departure request and designated him Level 3; the Appellate Division affirmed. The case is reported as articulating the same two-prong *Curry*-line standard. This is a further adverse, and notably *child-pornography-specific*, data point — though I was not able to confirm from secondary sources whether Adams's presumptive Level 3 arose from an override or from points alone, which materially affects how directly comparable it is to Bernecky's situation. Flagged for primary-source confirmation.

**3. *People v. Boone*, decided 2024, New York Court of Appeals (secondary citation only; precise reporter citation not independently confirmed).** Per secondary-source reconstruction, the Court of Appeals affirmed a Level 3 sexually violent offender designation based on both a points assessment *and* the automatic override arising from the defendant's prior felony sex-crime convictions, and affirmed denial of his downward-departure request; the bulk of the opinion concerns an unrelated SOMTA-timing due-process argument, not the substantive departure showing, so it offers limited additional guidance on what showing would have succeeded — but it is a further Court of Appeals-level data point confirming that override-plus-denied-departure outcomes are affirmed at the state's highest court.

**4. *People v. Weber*, 2023 NY Slip Op 03301 (New York Court of Appeals, June 15, 2023) — mixed signal, not override-based.** Per secondary-source reconstruction, Weber's presumptive Level 3 arose from *points* (110, including 10 points for forcible compulsion he disputed), not from an override; the Appellate Division reversed and remitted for reconsideration of the point assessment (and, on remand, whether an upward departure was warranted). This case is not override-based and is of limited direct relevance to Bernecky's Override 1 issue, but it is worth noting because it shows appellate reversal is not unheard of in this area when the underlying point assessment itself is contested — which is not Bernecky's situation, since his RAI scoring itself appears mathematically undisputed on the transcription reviewed.

**Assessment of this line of authority.** Taken together, these cases show New York courts — at both the Appellate Division and Court of Appeals level, across at least two departments — consistently affirming Override 1 (and other override) applications and consistently rejecting departure requests, including ones built on point-score gaps, treatment engagement, and personal-circumstance evidence resembling what is available to Bernecky. I want to be direct about what this means for the client: this is not a favorable body of law for the specific relief being sought, and the Writer should not overstate Bernecky's prospects to the client or to the court based on the record currently available.

### C. The Board's "Position Statement" — categorical reliance versus individualized findings

This is the strongest available *process*-oriented argument I identified, though it is not free of factual uncertainty because the underlying document has not been seen.

There is a well-documented Board "Position Statement," issued after *People v. Johnson*, 11 N.Y.3d 416 (2008), that addresses a *specific and different* problem: whether RAI Factors 3 (number of victims) and 7 (relationship with victim) should be scored against child-pornography offenders, given that scoring every CP offender for "stranger relationship" and multiple "victims" produces (per the Board's own reported language) "an unintended, anomalous result... [not accounting] for differences in individual offenders." *Gillotti* addressed this exact statement and held that it does **not** bar a SORA court from assessing points under Factors 3 and 7 notwithstanding the Board's own stated concern — meaning the Court of Appeals has already treated at least one Board position statement as *not* controlling or displacing individualized judicial analysis.

Bernecky's Case Summary invokes a Board "Position Statement" dated **June 1, 2012** for a materially different proposition — as support for a Level III recommendation, on the theory that Bernecky "has demonstrated the inability to manage his sexual urges and returned to seeking out images of child pornography." On the face of the Case Summary's own description, this looks like it may be a *different* document from the Factor-3/7 statement discussed in *Johnson*/*Gillotti* (which does not, on any description I located, purport to establish a general "inability to manage sexual urges" finding or to speak to Override 1's application at all) — or, alternatively, it may be the same 2012 statement being invoked here for a purpose beyond its actual scope. **I cannot resolve this without the document itself**, which is referenced as "attached" in the Case Summary but is not among the client's photographed pages. This is a concrete, high-value item to chase down before the hearing (see Section 7) — if the Board's own Position Statement does not actually speak to override-based Level III findings or general dangerousness, and the Case Summary has stretched it to do so, that is a legitimate point to press on the adequacy and individualization of the Board's stated reasoning, separate from (and in addition to) the *Gillotti* departure framework itself.

More generally, the recurring theme across the ACP Guide's discussion of *Johnson*, *Gillotti*, and the Board's own Committee-Report-documented self-criticism (the guide cites a February 2022 NYC Bar Association committee report, which I was not able to read directly due to a PDF-extraction failure, but which the ACP Guide quotes and summarizes at length) is that New York courts and the organized bar have repeatedly recognized the RAI's tendency toward reflexive, categorical scoring that does not differentiate among individual offenders — and that this is precisely the kind of concern a well-supported departure motion should invoke. That general critique supports *making* the individualization argument; it does not, on the authority I found, mean courts have been receptive to it in the override context specifically. See Section 5.B.

### D. Other on-point authority, for and against

**For Bernecky:**
- The RAI's own zero-scoring of Factors 1, 2, 3, 6, and 7 is Board-created, contemporaneous, documentary evidence — not argument — that the instant offense involved no violence, no scored sexual contact, and no scored victim relationship. This is a stronger evidentiary predicate than a bare assertion would be, because it comes from the Board's own instrument.
- *People v. Johnson*, 11 N.Y.3d 416 (2008), and the Board's own post-*Johnson* recognition (per the ACP Guide) that categorical, undifferentiated scoring of CP offenders can be "excessive" and is properly checked by discretionary departure, provides some doctrinal support for arguing that Bernecky's individual circumstances (non-contact instant offense, no violence, low remaining point total) deserve individualized consideration — even though, again, that language was developed in the Factor-3/7 scoring context, not the override context, and the Writer should not overstate its transferability.
- The two 2005/2007 historical incidents were, on the Board's own numerical assessment, not scored — meaning the Board itself did not treat them as scorable aggravating conduct notwithstanding their narrative inclusion.

**Against Bernecky:**
- *Gunter*'s holding that a pre-override presumptive Level 2 score is not itself a valid mitigating factor forecloses Bernecky's most numerically intuitive argument as a standalone basis.
- The Case Summary's own language — "no departure is recommended as prior detection, sanction and treatment have not deterred his conduct" — is a preemptive rebuttal of the treatment argument, built on the fact that Bernecky committed the instant offense *while in phase-two sex-offender treatment* following a prior conviction and term of supervision. Under the *Gunter* standard requiring an "exceptional" treatment response, an offender who reoffends during treatment is a difficult fact pattern to characterize as exceptional, absent something more (e.g., an expert opinion specifically addressing why the response should nonetheless be credited, or evidence of what changed since the instant offense).
- The recency scoring (offense committed under two years into supervision from the prior conviction) and the fact that the two-decade documented pattern (2010 admissions, 2012 conviction, 2016–2017 treatment engagement, 2017 reoffense) shows continuity rather than a clean break, are facts a court is likely to weigh heavily in the discretionary third step even if the first two *Gillotti* steps are satisfied.
- I was not able to identify any expert (psychological) evidence specific to Bernecky's *current* risk level beyond what the Board's own Case Summary paraphrases — the Board's own document is likely to be the primary evidentiary record at the hearing unless the defense supplements it, which the case law (*Gunter*'s rejection of undocumented assertions) suggests is important to do affirmatively.

## 6. CONCLUSION

On the record and authority available to me, Bernecky has a **legally viable, but not favored, argument** for a downward departure from Level 3 to Level 2. The legal framework (*Gillotti*'s preponderance standard, the *Curry*/*Edwards* two-prong test) applies to override-based departures as much as to points-based ones, and Bernecky has real, Board-documented facts to build a mitigating-factor showing from — most importantly the complete absence of violence or contact scoring on the instant offense and his sustained treatment engagement. But the pattern of adverse authority I found (*Gunter* especially) shows courts consistently rejecting materially similar showings from Override 1, and Bernecky's own Case Summary anticipates and directly rebuts his single strongest argument (treatment engagement) with the fact that he reoffended while in treatment.

**My confidence level:** Low-to-moderate likelihood of an outright departure grant on the current record, based on the pattern of authority located.
