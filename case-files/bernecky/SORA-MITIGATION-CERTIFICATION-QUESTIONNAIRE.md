# Mitigation Certification — Questions for Jeff

Purpose: Jeff's own answers to these questions will be the raw material for a
sworn certification/declaration to accompany the downward-departure application.
Answer in his own words, as fully as he's willing to — a certification built
from his actual voice and specifics is far more persuasive than one built from
lawyer-drafted generalities, and specifics can be trimmed later more easily than
they can be invented. Where a question touches something already in the Board's
Case Summary, his answer doesn't need to dispute the underlying fact (some of
this is already conceded in the draft memorandum) — the goal is context, not
contradiction, unless something in the Case Summary is actually wrong.

---

## 1. The reoffense itself — what actually happened

1. In his own words, what led him to download and view the material in
   December 2017, after already having been through the 2012 conviction,
   supervision, and treatment? What was going on in his life in the weeks
   before — stress, isolation, a specific trigger or event — that he can point
   to now, looking back?
2. Did he experience it, at the time or since, as a deliberate decision, or as
   something closer to a relapse or a lapse of control despite not wanting to
   do it? This matters directly to how the certification frames the conduct —
   his own honest answer, not the answer he thinks helps most.
3. Was there ever any point — before, during, or after the instant offense —
   where he sought out, arranged, or considered arranging any in-person contact
   with a child, including online communication with a minor, a webcam
   interaction, travel, or any attempt to meet anyone? A direct, specific
   answer here (not just a denial in general terms) is important.
4. What specifically has he done differently since December 2017 — in
   treatment, in his own coping strategies, in his environment — that speaks to
   why this is different from what happened in 2012, or why it's less likely to
   happen again.

## 2. The 2005 and 2007 disclosed incidents

5. In his own words, what happened in each of those two incidents (the 2005 day
   camp bus incident, the 2007 after-school program incident) — the full
   context, not just the moment already described in the Case Summary. What was
   his role, what led up to it, what did he do afterward.
6. Why did he decide to disclose these to his treatment provider and undergo
   the 2017 polygraph re-test, rather than leaving them undisclosed? Was this
   his own decision or prompted by someone else.
7. Has he ever, at any other time in his life, sought out or acted on any
   sexual interest in a child in person — as opposed to online image
   consumption? A direct answer either way, understanding this is a hard
   question to answer candidly but that candor here matters more than a
   reflexive denial.

## 3. Treatment history and insight

8. Describe his treatment at Endeavor — how long he's been engaged, what
   "phase two" involves, what he's actually learned about his own patterns
   (triggers, coping mechanisms, relapse-prevention plan) that he didn't
   understand before.
9. What does his current relapse-prevention plan actually consist of, in
   concrete terms — what would he do differently if he found himself in a
   similar high-stress or high-isolation period again.
10. Has any treatment provider told him, formally or informally, whether they
    believe he presents any risk of contact offending, as opposed to a risk of
    returning to viewing material? If there's any letter, report, or note from
    a treatment provider addressing this distinction specifically, it should be
    identified and obtained.

## 4. Compliance and conduct

11. Regarding voluntary registration during incarceration: what exactly did he
    do, when, and does he have any confirmation, receipt, or record of it
    (a letter, a form, a DCJS confirmation)? This fact needs actual
    documentation before it goes in any filing — see Recommendation in the
    Adjournment memo.
12. Anything else about his conduct during incarceration or at the reentry
    facility beyond what the Case Summary already describes as "satisfactory"
    — program participation, work assignments, any commendations or
    write-ups either way (favorable and unfavorable both matter here; don't
    omit anything unfavorable).

## 5. Release plan and community ties

13. Where does he plan to live upon release, and is that address near a
    school, daycare, or park — relevant both to registration/notification
    practicalities and to any departure argument about actual community risk.
14. What is his employment plan, if any, and does it involve any contact with
    children in any capacity (directly or incidentally)?
15. Who in his life — family, friends, a sponsor, a religious or community
    contact — would be willing to write a letter of support for the departure
    application? Get names and contact information; character letters
    typically carry real weight in a departure application.

## 6. The 2017 phone/employer incident (relevant to the federal CIMP track, not SORA, but worth capturing here while asking)

16. His own account of the December 2017 incident — the phone he told the
    probation officer belonged to a coworker, and what he told his employer
    about not needing to use certain devices for work. This is being developed
    separately for the federal motion, but a consistent, complete account now
    avoids inconsistency later.

## 7. General

17. Anything else he wants the Court to know about him as a person — not
    restated case facts, but anything about who he is now that isn't captured
    anywhere in the Board's file.

---

**Note for whoever conducts this interview**: capture full sentences and
specifics, not just checkmarks — a persuasive certification needs Jeff's actual
voice and detail, not a lawyer's paraphrase of a paraphrase. If any answer
conflicts with something already in the Board's Case Summary or in a prior
memo, flag the conflict rather than silently picking one version.
