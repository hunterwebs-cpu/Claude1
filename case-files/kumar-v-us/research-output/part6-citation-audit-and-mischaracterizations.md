## B. Patterns of overreach/mischaracterization to feature in the opposition (ranked by usefulness)

1. **Dugas v. Coplan** -- a Section 2254 evidentiary-hearing case cited for "clear error" where the district court (per Kumar) took no evidence. Its own text says de novo where no independent fact-finding. (Real and verifiable; strong.)
2. **Tollett** -- quoted for the bar but omits the immediately following carve-out for counsel-advice attacks on the voluntary and intelligent character of the plea (411 U.S. at 267); Maldonado, which the Government cites, repeats the carve-out.
3. **Rule 12(b)(3)(A)(ii)** -- the Government says Kumar "never pursued the motion," but the rule requires a defendant to *raise* the claim by pretrial motion and Kumar did (ECF 58, 75); its authorities (Turner, Brown, Oslund) all involve defendants who filed no motion. Brown and Oslund are out-of-circuit and predate the 2014 amendments to Rule 12.
4. **Handa** -- Sixth Amendment speedy-trial case used for a Fifth Amendment pre-indictment standard of review; the court noted tension and did not decide.
5. **Gaffney** -- the court "decline[d]" to apply Tollett.
6. **Peralta** -- distinguishes the some-issues-granted scenario and reads COA broadly.
7. **Royle** -- not a pre-indictment-delay case.
8. **Fernandez-Santos** -- direct-appeal IAC claim sent to collateral review for factual development.
9. **Robertson, 162 F.4th 209** -- cannot be confirmed; do not accuse, but demand a copy ([UNCONFIRMED]).
10. **"Rodiguez"** -- misspelled name (trivial; mention only in passing, if at all).

**What the Government's list does NOT contain (omissions to flag):** any authority on cause and prejudice (Murray v. Carrier; Massaro; Frady); on Strickland prejudice where the claimed error is a missed evidentiary protection (Hill's "prediction" language); on standard of review in 2255 appeals; on 28 U.S.C. 2255(b) hearings (McGill; David); on Class (despite the Court's Class framework being central to Kumar's brief); on Bui v. DiPaolo (limited COA procedure) or Local Rule 22.0(c); on proffer-agreement law (Apfelbaum, Mezzanatto, Kastigar). The motion is also silent on the Basil affidavit.

## C. Factual overreach in the Government's motion

1. **"Undisputed offense facts in the PSR"** (motion p. 2) and "uncontested evidence" (p. 4). The PSR is not trial evidence; 112 F.4th reports objections at sentencing (cross-reference; loss) and that the Government's loss figure rose from ~$400,000 to ~$3.8 million between the initial and revised PSR (slip op. 5-6). The motion's "millions of illicit pills" is a Government *estimate* ($1/pill; 3,859,772 pills) accepted without clear error; Kumar contested the data on appeal.
2. **"overwhelming and independent evidence of Kumar's guilt [supra 2-4]"** (p. 7): "supra 2-4" is a recital of PSR facts with no assertion of *independence* from the proffer sessions or his consent-given device access, and no citation to any record evidence of independent source.
3. **PSR paragraph cites** (e.g., PSR para 28, 40) and **ECF 130 pages** (3-4, 13-14, 18-21) cannot be tested by us; the district court's order is not attached (FRAP 27(a)(2)(B)(iii)).
4. **"proffer letters typically ..."** is a generalization; it does not describe the actual D.R.I. letter (ECF 122-1) or any D. Mass. letter.
5. **ECF numbers for withdrawal**: Government cites "ECF 58, 75, 80, 82"; Kumar cites ECF 83 for the letter explaining the 15-20 month belief. Pull all five.
6. **COA description**: motion says COA issued from a different judge as ECF 141 on two issues; Kumar's brief says the district court "issued a COA sua sponte" in ECF 130. Must reconcile.
7. **The omission of the Basil affidavit para 8** from the Government's discussion of motive, though it is central to Kumar's brief.
8. **"He expressly withdrew ... on that precise ground"** (p. 7): the motion to dismiss raised double jeopardy, vindictive prosecution and preindictment delay (ECF 58) and successive prosecution (ECF 75). OK as to delay.
9. **Timeline gloss**: the motion says that after Kumar failed to move to expand "this Court set a briefing schedule and Kumar filed a pro se opening brief shortly thereafter." The briefing notice and the opening brief both bear the date 8/24/2026; the notice is the *appellee's* notice issued upon filing of the appellant's brief. Not material, but note when drafting the procedural history.

## D. Reliability of our own earlier materials (IMPORTANT)

**The April 2026 "Research Memorandum: Section 2255 Appeal" (Drive 1QBX1C1VaJ6VMvA8XK1RfKn1hpe1RybF86Z7zY22Lq6w) should NOT be cited or relied upon without independent verification.** Problems I found:
1. It attributes to Irizarry-Colon, 848 F.3d at 70-71, a quotation from United States v. Al-Muqsit (8th Cir. 1999) ("must specifically identify witnesses or documents lost ...") -- not present in Irizarry-Colon.
2. It cites United States v. Goodhue, 486 F.3d 52, 57 (1st Cir. 2007), as a sentencing appeal "involving delays and U.S.S.G. 5G1.3" -- Goodhue (CAP text read) contains no discussion of 5G1.3 or delay; it concerns criminal history recalculation and drug-weight (mixture) calculation.
3. It characterizes United States v. Cardales-Luna, 632 F.3d 731, 737-38 (1st Cir. 2011), as adhering "to strict waiver rules for non-jurisdictional constitutional challenges" -- the CAP text shows an MDLEA statute/jurisdiction case; it is cited in De Vaughn only for whether constitutionality of a criminal statute is jurisdictional.
4. Its "circuit split" section relies on a cert. petition (Herrera v. United States, No. 22-827) and on characterizations of Bowline, Lozano, Thomas, Prewitt that I did not verify; it says "(Verification required)" in several places.
5. Its pinpoint for Concepcion-Guliam is 32; the Government and Kumar use 31.
6. Its description of Irizarry-Colon as rejecting prejudice "categorically" and its assertion that the First Circuit "does not recognize abstract sentencing enhancements ... as cognizable Marion prejudice" is an inference; the actual authority is Stokes/McCoy (speculation), which that memo does not cite.
