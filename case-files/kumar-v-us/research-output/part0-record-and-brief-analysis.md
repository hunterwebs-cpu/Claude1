## 3. Weaknesses in Kumar's own brief that the Government (and the panel) can exploit

1. **Does not engage the district court's two stated prejudice grounds on the proffer claim** (typical proffer letters do not cover false statements; no derivative-use protection). Br. 48-50 argues "suppression rights" but concedes "The question is not whether Kumar's false statements could have been used regardless" -- which is exactly the Government's point as to Count 3.
2. **Concedes the delay claim is not a trial-prejudice claim**: "Kumar does not argue that the delay cost him evidence or witnesses" (Br. 22). First Circuit law requires "substantial prejudice to his right to a fair trial" (Irizarry-Colon, 848 F.3d at 70; Bater, 594 F.3d at 54; Crooks, 766 F.2d at 11). Stokes (124 F.3d at 44) and McCoy (977 F.2d at 711) treat lost-concurrent-sentence prejudice as "pure speculation."
3. **Sentencing numbers appear internally inconsistent.** Br. says offense level 27 with CHC I floor 70 / CHC II floor 87. On the Sentencing Table as I understand it, level 27 is 70-87 (CHC I) and 78-97 (CHC II); 87-108 is level 28 / CHC II (or level 27 / CHC III). Also "CHC I" is used where "CHC II" is meant (Br. 14, 'placing Kumar in Criminal History Category I'). If the true differential is 78 vs. 87 (9 months), and the court's "72 months" is below even the CHC I floor, the claim that CHC II "caused" the 72-vs-87 difference is weaker than briefed. **[VERIFY against the PSR / USSG Sentencing Table; do not repeat Kumar's 70/87 figure in any filing until confirmed.]**
4. **Class for preindictment delay is an admitted "harder question"/"principled extension"** (Br. 21-22); the First Circuit has (per Maldonado, 2026; Turner, 2024; Cardales-Luna, 2011) read Tollett/Class narrowly. This is the weakest leg of Argument I.
5. **Some citations in the brief need checking** (Part 6): Damon v. United States, 732 F.3d 1, 3 for standard of review (CAP text of Damon did not contain a de novo/clear-error statement); United States v. Ciampi, 419 F.3d 20, 24 for liberal construction (the opinion mentions that argument as the petitioner's contention); Chavez-Diaz (9th Cir. 2020) and Riojas (5th Cir. 2025) and Davila-Reyes (1st Cir. en banc 2023) and Cheveres-Morales (1st Cir. 2023) not verified by me.
6. **Brief's certificate of service** is addressed to AUSA Basil (a fact witness) and the date line appears blank in the OCR; the mail envelope image (PDF p.68) should be checked for a postmark (useful evidence of mailing date for the mailbox rule on that filing).
7. **Docket/record cites** use "ECF" numbers throughout; we have none of the underlying documents except a partial CourtListener RECAP excerpt (ECF 56-77; see record-index-district-docket-excerpt.md), which shows that the brief's "pro se with standby counsel" description was accurate for 2/28/2022 but that on 3/15/2022 Kumar elected counsel and Fasoldt was appointed (ECF 65) -- a fact the brief omits and that matters for who advised him in September 2022. State this accurately in any filing. The brief also describes Count 1 as a violation of "21 U.S.C. S 846"; 112 F.4th 30 describes Count 1 as an 18 U.S.C. S 371 conspiracy (to violate 18 U.S.C. S 545, 21 U.S.C. S 331(a), 21 U.S.C. S 841(a)(1)) and Count 2 as the S 846 conspiracy.

## 4. Things in the brief that help us
- Basil affidavit para 8 is the Government's own sworn account of why it charged on 5/12/2021 and that it had already decided to charge.
- The sentencing judge's own remark about 72 months (if accurately quoted) is direct record evidence of Guidelines-sensitivity.
- The unresolved dispute over the RI/MA proffer letter, and the absence of any evidentiary hearing, are record-based points independent of legal theories.
- Kumar's September 2022 letter (ECF 83) states the reason for withdrawal in his own contemporaneous words -- the best "contemporaneous evidence" under Lee v. United States, 582 U.S. 357 (2017).
