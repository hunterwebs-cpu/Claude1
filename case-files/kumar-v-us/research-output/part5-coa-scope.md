# PART 5 -- SCOPE OF THE CERTIFICATE OF APPEALABILITY; EXPANDING IT; WHICH UNCERTIFIED CLAIMS ARE WORTH IT (Track E)
Kumar v. United States, No. 26-1250 (1st Cir.). PRIVILEGED / WORK PRODUCT. 2026-10-05.

## 1. Governing text (verbatim; all CONFIRMED unless marked)

**28 U.S.C. 2253(c)** (LII): "(1) Unless a circuit justice or judge issues a certificate of appealability, an appeal may not be taken to the court of appeals from-- ... (B) the final order in a proceeding under section 2255. (2) A certificate of appealability may issue under paragraph (1) only if the applicant has made a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right. (3) The certificate of appealability under paragraph (1) shall indicate which specific issue or issues satisfy the showing required by paragraph (2)."

**FRAP 22(b)(2)** (Rulebook): "A request addressed to the court of appeals may be considered by a circuit judge or judges, as the court prescribes. If no express request for a certificate is filed, the notice of appeal constitutes a request addressed to the judges of the court of appeals."

**1st Cir. R. 22.0(c)** (Rulebook p. 39; verbatim):
> "(c) Partial Denial by District Court. (1) If the district court grants a certificate of appealability as to one or more issues, the petitioner's appeal shall go forward only as to the issue or issues for which the district court granted the certificate. See Grant-Chase v. Commissioner, 145 F.3d 431 (1st Cir. 1998). (2) If the petitioner wants appellate review of an issue or issues as to which the district court has denied a certificate of appealability, petitioner must apply promptly, within the time set by the clerk of the court of appeals, to the court of appeals for an expanded certificate of appealability. The request for an expanded certificate of appealability: (A) must be explicit as to the additional issues the petitioner wishes the court to consider and (B) should be accompanied by a copy of the district court order and a memorandum giving specific and substantial reasons, and not mere generalizations, why an expanded certificate of appealability should be granted. If the petitioner fails to apply for an expanded certificate of appealability within the time designated by the clerk, the appeal will proceed only with respect to the issues on which the district court has granted a certificate; this court will not treat an inexplicit notice of appeal, without more, as a request for a certificate of appealability with respect to issues on which the district court has denied a certificate."

**The Court's 7/1/2026 order** (as quoted by the Government; we do not have the order itself): permitted a motion to expand by 7/31/2026 and warned that if none were filed, "then the appeal will proceed only with respect to the . . . issues as to which the district court issued a certificate of appealability." This tracks 1st Cir. R. 22.0(c)(2) exactly. **[Obtain the 7/1/2026 order and the clerk's date of mailing/service.]**

**Bui v. DiPaolo, 170 F.3d 232, 237-38 (1st Cir. 1999)** [CONFIRMED text via CAP; pinpoints VERIFY]: "If the district court issues a limited COA ... the petitioner must present promptly to the court of appeals an express request for a complementary COA. ... it is our present practice to notify each habeas petitioner of an outside date by which his request for a COA must be filed in the court of appeals. Failure to file within that interval may be deemed to constitute a waiver." "The request for a complementary COA must be explicit as to the issues which the petitioner wishes us to consider and, in such circumstances, we will not treat an inexplicit notice of appeal, without more, as an invitation to review the district court's denial of certification with regard to the petitioner's remaining claims." And: "when the district court grants a limited COA and the petitioner seasonably seeks to expand it, his appeal on the certified issue(s) should be held in abeyance, and full briefing deferred, until this court determines the appealability of the issues that the district court deemed unworthy of appellate scrutiny."

**Merits showing for expansion:** Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473, 484 (2000), and Miller-El v. Cockrell, 537 U.S. 322, 336-38 (2003) (Part 2): "jurists of reason" could find the district court's assessment "debatable or wrong," or, for procedurally-barred claims, debate both the underlying claim and the procedural ruling. Buck v. Davis, 580 U.S. 100 (2017), is cited in the assignment but I could not retrieve its text from the corpora used (CAP volume 580 not available; CourtListener not queried due to rate limits). **[VERIFY Buck's COA-stage discussion before citing.]**

## 2. The Government's authorities, checked [CONFIRMED by reading text]

- **Vargas-De Jesus v. United States, 813 F.3d 414 (1st Cir. 2016)** (Gov't cites 419 n.6): the court had issued a COA "only as to" ineffective assistance at sentencing. Footnote: "Vargas also argues that his counsel was ineffective at sentencing because he 'failed to argue that [Vargas] should receive a downward departure based on his youth at the time of the individual acts.' But we do not address this argument. It is outside the scope of the COA, and Vargas did not attempt -- below or on appeal -- to obtain a COA on this issue. See Peralta ..." **Accurate; and on point.** (Footnote number: the CAP text shows the footnote but I did not confirm it is n.6; the Government says n.6 at 419.) Note the court there (First Circuit panel) *itself* shaped the COA after reading a pro se petition liberally ("we interpreted Vargas's petition to argue that his counsel was ineffective both at trial and at sentencing"). Pro se context; same disposition.
- **Peralta v. United States, 597 F.3d 74, 83 (1st Cir. 2010) (per curiam)** [CONFIRMED text]: "To the extent his brief raises other issues, they are outside the scope of the COA. ... Where the district court denies a request for a COA as to a particular issue, the proper procedure is to seek a complementary COA with the court of appeals. ... This is not, however, a case where the district court granted a request for a COA as to some issues but not as to others. Instead, Peralta has simply raised new issues in his appellate brief that he never addressed in his request for a COA. Having failed to request a COA as to those issues in either the district court or the court of appeals, Peralta has waived his right to appellate review of those issues." **Helpful nuance for us:** (i) in Peralta "the Court has read [the COA] broadly" -- the panel read a "somewhat awkwardly and inaccurately" framed COA to include "(1) the claim of ineffective assistance of counsel concerning (a) counsel's alleged failure to prosecute the motion to dismiss and (b) counsel's alleged failure to investigate his criminal history ... and (2) the related findings of fact" -- showing the First Circuit will construe a COA broadly to reach related issues; (ii) Peralta expressly distinguished the "granted some, not others" scenario (which is ours) -- Peralta's waiver holding rested on a petitioner who never requested a COA for the new issues at all. The Government cites Peralta for the *general* proposition; the closest authority for our posture is **Bui** (which the Government does not cite), whose waiver language is permissive ("may be deemed"). [Peralta reported date: CourtListener says 2010-03-04; CAP says 2010-03-05; immaterial.]
- **Grant-Chase v. Commissioner, 145 F.3d 431 (1st Cir. 1998)** [CONFIRMED]: holds a litigant who obtains a COA from a district judge need not seek a second COA from the court of appeals "as to these issues"; the appeal proceeds on those issues. Cited in Local Rule 22.0(c)(1) as authority for "only as to the issue or issues for which the district court granted the certificate."

**Conclusion on the Government's cites:** correct, on point, and aligned with the Court's own 7/1/2026 order. **We should not argue that the uncertified claims are properly before the Court without first obtaining an expansion or showing that they fall within the certified issues.**

## 3. Arguments that some additional claims are within or should be added to the certified issues

**A. Interpretive argument: the certified delay claim includes the threshold waiver/cause questions.** The COA text (Gov't mot. 5): "with respect to [Kumar's] claims regarding (1) ineffective assistance of counsel due to failure to obtain proffer protection [and] (2) preindictment delay in violation of the Fifth Amendment." "Claims regarding" is broad. The district court denied the delay claim on two grounds (waived; no merit). The Government's own three waiver grounds are *defenses to the certified claim.* Whether Kumar can overcome the waiver by showing IAC cause (the misadvice that led to withdrawal) is part of whether the certified claim may be adjudicated. Peralta's broad reading of an awkwardly framed COA supports treating "cause" as part of the certified issue. **[Moderate; the Court may see Ground Two as a separate "IAC" claim outside the narrow IAC COA.]**

**B. Slack-based argument:** a COA on a claim the district court rejected as waived necessarily reflects a finding that the procedural ruling is debatable (Part 4 sec. 5).

**C. The COA's origin is murky.** Kumar's brief (Br. 12, 17) says the *district court* "issued a Certificate of Appealability sua sponte, noting the 'unusual facts of this case.' Dkt. No. 130 at 21-22." The Government says COA was granted later by a different judge (Kelly, J.) at ECF 141 on two issues only. If ECF 130 contained a *general* COA and ECF 141 is a *later narrower* order, or if the original COA was general and the narrowing is of unclear provenance, the scope question changes. Under 2253(c)(3) a COA must "indicate which specific issue or issues" satisfy the standard; a general COA may be defective in a way the Court prefers to cure by construing it broadly. **[Must read ECF 130 at 21-22, ECF 132 (NOA), ECF 141 (COA) and the 1st Cir. order of 7/1/2026.]**

**D. Liberal construction of pro se filings.** Erickson v. Pardus, 551 U.S. 89, 94 (2007) (per curiam) [CONFIRMED text]: "a pro se complaint, however inartfully pleaded, must be held to less stringent standards than formal pleadings drafted by lawyers." (Complaint context.) For First Circuit authority that pro se appellate briefs are construed liberally I did not verify a cite; Kumar's brief cites United States v. Ciampi, 419 F.3d 20, 24 (1st Cir. 2005), but my reading shows Ciampi discussing liberal construction only as the petitioner's contention; **do not cite Ciampi for that point without verifying.** Even liberal construction cannot override 1st Cir. R. 22.0(c)(2)'s explicit "will not treat an inexplicit notice of appeal, without more, as a request" (Bui) -- a brief is arguably not an "explicit" request.

## 4. Can Kumar still move to expand the COA after 7/31? On what showing?

**Authority for relief:** FRAP 26(b) (Rulebook): "For good cause, the court may extend the time prescribed by these rules or by its order to perform any act, or may permit an act to be done after that time expires." The 7/31 deadline was set "by its order," so it is extendable (not jurisdictional; the 26(b)(1)-(2) exceptions do not apply). Bui: "Failure to file within that interval **may** be deemed to constitute a waiver" -- permissive. Local Rule 22.0(c)(2): "If the petitioner fails to apply ... within the time designated by the clerk, the appeal will proceed only with respect to the issues on which the district court has granted a certificate" -- mandatory-sounding but self-executing only absent a court order excusing the delay. **No First Circuit case I located addresses extending the time for a late complementary-COA motion; [GAP -- research]. Expect the Government to cite Bui's "seasonably" and 22.0(c)(2).**

**Good-cause facts to look for (do not assert until confirmed):**
1. When did Kumar actually receive the 7/1/2026 order? (Legal-mail log; FCI Fort Dix.) If he received it after 7/31 or too late to prepare, that is the best good-cause fact.
2. Did the order explain the process in terms a lay person would understand? Did the clerk send the Rule 22.0(c)(2) notice?
3. Kumar's pro se status; the fact that his *opening brief of 8/24/2026 expressly argues the uncertified IAC grounds* (Ground Two, Ground Three) and vindictive prosecution -- i.e., he plainly wants them reviewed; Bui requires an "explicit" request but the *substance* is explicit; the brief gave notice and Government had full opportunity to respond; no prejudice to the Government beyond its own election not to address them.
4. Government's choice to file a summary-disposition motion rather than brief the uncertified issues -- there is no prejudice from allowing a COA motion to be decided first.
5. The district court's own statement of "unusual facts of this case" (ECF 130 at 21-22 per Kumar).
6. Release date (projected 8/26/2027) and request for expedited consideration -- timing matters.
7. Immediate diligence once counsel/assistance obtained.

**Substantive showing required (Slack/Miller-El): "specific and substantial reasons, and not mere generalizations" (1st Cir. R. 22.0(c)(2)(B)):** identify each additional issue; show the underlying constitutional claim is debatable among jurists of reason and (for procedural rulings) that the procedural ruling is debatable. Attach ECF 130 and ECF 141.

**Bui timing hook:** If we move to expand, Bui says the certified appeal "should be held in abeyance, and full briefing deferred, until this court determines the appealability of the issues that the district court deemed unworthy." That supports (i) the *stay* the Government wants being at least partially aligned with our interest, but (ii) with our expansion motion decided first. A strategic point: it reframes the dispute as "decide the COA first; do not summarily affirm a narrowed appeal while a complementary COA request is pending."
