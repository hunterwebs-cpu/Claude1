## 1. ECF 58 (pro se Motion to Dismiss, filed 2/28/2022, 10 pp.)

Title/framing (ECF 58 at 1): "Motion to dismiss the indictment on the following grounds. 1) Double jeopardy dual sovereignty doctrine for sham prosecution. 2) Vindictive Prosecution 3) Pre indictment delay in violation of Fifth Amendment." It is handwritten-style prose with heavy string citation. It is signed "Manish Kumar," and the date line reads "2-[?]2/2022" **[OCR-uncertain]**.

### A. Double jeopardy / "sham prosecution" (ECF 58 at 1-4)

Facts alleged:
- Charged in D.R.I. "on august 25 2019." Then-counsel Joan Daley told him to sign a proffer and cooperate (at 1).
- He consented to a search of phones and laptop. By his 9/13/2019 meeting agents "were aware" of the prescription-drug conversations (at 1).
- Agents had him text Robert Polanco to "trap him"; "I decline to cooperate after my last meeting 2-27-2020" and declined the plea deal (at 1). The RI prosecutor "showed his desire to indict me (Exhibit 1)" if he did not cooperate (at 1).
- He pleaded to an information with an indictment waiver, "after the assurance they will not bring no indictment" (at 1). The Boston charges are "a 'sham and cover'" for the RI prosecution (at 1).
- Same agents (Barrios, Broomfield) in both cases; RI file passed to Boston AUSA Kris Bassil "when they saw my sentence got reduced" (at 1).
- Same-conduct argument: PSR para. 13 (RI) and the Boston charges arise from the same customer calls (at 2).
- Sentencing: AUSA said the intended loss was low ($27,000) because "they have been monitoring my activities for past year and they did not find anything else" (at 2).
- Seven "knowledge" points (at 3-4), the government supposedly knowing everything "by end of December 2020 or latest by February 2021" (at 3), or by "the End on 2019" (at 4). They include:
  - the Narang/informant exchanges (Ex. 2);
  - the Richard Ferrier interview;
  - the Shah holding-cell conversation;
  - the 9/3/2019 interview and phone review;
  - the consent search (45-day consent);
  - the 10/3/2019 Polanco texting request, with the information "available to R.I prosecutor and Boston federal investigators after November 7, 2019 (Exhibit 3)";
  - the 2/27/2020 meeting.
- Authorities: Bartkus, Wheeler, Abbate, Palko, In re Nielsen, Belcher, Petite (at 1-4). He asks the court to "follow the petite" (at 4).

Internal date inconsistencies, which the government can exploit: "27th February 2026" (at 2, OCR; plainly a typo for 2020); "February 27, 2021" (at 4, "they searched my devices on February 27, 2021"); and "latest by February 27, 2021" vs "February 2021" vs "end of 2019" (at 3-4). The date of "knowledge" is therefore never stated consistently.

Legal observation (mine, not in the documents): this ground is weak as pleaded. Dual sovereignty/Bartkus has no application to two federal districts. ECF 75 later drops that framing and recasts the argument as Rule 8 joinder/successive prosecution.

### B. Vindictive prosecution (ECF 58 at 5-7)

- Sentenced 2/11/2021 "for 44 Months." He then wrote to Judge McConnell and to AUSA Shah asking to "rectify" the sentence (at 5).
- "in the month of March 2021" then-counsel Melissa Larsson told him Shah said 44 months "is my correct sentence and also told her tell me to be quite and do my time otherwise they will give me more charges" (at 5). Sentence was then reduced by Judge McConnell (at 5).
- Theory: the Boston indictment (indictment per ECF 75 issued 5/17/2021, Doc. 8) is retaliation: "they gave this new case in retaliation of my reduced sentence" (at 5).
- Pre-sentencing-reduction threats are also alleged: AUSAs Shah and Kris Bassil "talking about potential charges to indict me (Exhibit 1 and 5)" (at 6), plus threats when he refused cooperation, declined the plea offer, and wrote to Shah (at 6).
- Cases: Tobin (D. Me. 2009), Groves (9th Cir. 1978), DeMarco (9th Cir. 1977), Blackledge v. Perry, Rosenthal, Cady, Johnson (at 5-7). He argues a presumption of vindictiveness and that the same "factual nucleus" and prior knowledge defeat any justification (at 6-7).
- He also alleges the two prosecutors "agreed upon to charge me in Boston so that they can have a tactical advantage" and avoid RI due-process claims (at 6).

Weaknesses visible on the face of ECF 58:
- The core retaliation trigger (letter to the judge) is a non-appeal, informal request. He concedes it differs from Tobin ("Tobin appealed formally ... I wrote a letter to judge," at 5).
- The only evidence of the March 2021 threat is hearsay from his own former lawyer (Larsson), uncorroborated in the exhibits.
- The "threats" alleged earlier in 2020 pre-date the letter, which cuts against retaliation for the letter and fits the government's story of cooperation negotiations. Exhibit 5 (below) shows the charging discussion tied to plea/cooperation talks.

### C. Pre-indictment delay (ECF 58 at 7-10)

Heading: "Pre indictment delay due process Fifth Amendment violation" (at 7). Specific claims:

Prejudice (at 7-9):
1. Shah did not tell the RI sentencing court of future charges "as done by government prosecutor in Baldwin v. United States, 2019 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 182288," so he faces "two successive federal prosecution[s]" (at 7).
2. Shah waived his indictment while "aware" and "showed his desired to indict me in R.I in an email to my counsel (Exhibit 4)." He came from "I.C.E detention," "spent 10 extra months in pretrial incarceration" (at 7-8).
3. Retaliation for reduced sentence to "make sure I will [get] maximum jail exposure" (at 8).
4. Loss of concurrent sentence: "Government made sure I will not get the opportunity to run my sentence concurrent and I will get maximum jail exposure by bringing this successive [indictment] after I finished my first case sentence" (at 8). Cites Smith v. Hooey via Cabral for lost concurrent sentence, oppressive effect, and impaired defense (at 8).
5. Could not combine both cases and "resolve both cases at once" (at 8).
6. Financial depletion, Irizarry-Colon: wife paid first-case fees, then divorced him; counsel problems (at 8).

Tactical-advantage/actual prejudice (at 9-10):
7. Lost witness Polanco: agents made him text Polanco and "entrap him"; Polanco "in no sense ... will testify anything that will in any way benefits me." "His testimony would have proved my intent" (at 9). He says he has had no contact with Polanco in the 30 months incarcerated (at 9).
8. Lost business partners/shipping-company witnesses and documents: "I have no contacts with any of them during my incarceration ... after my wife divorced me I lost all my financial and legal ability to defend myself" (at 9).
9. Plea-contradiction: having pleaded guilty in RI (PSR para. 13), he cannot defend Boston without risking a perjury charge (at 10).
10. Ramos-Gonzalez, 775 F.3d 483, 491 (1st Cir. 2015): bad-faith/tactical delay standard, plus dictum that egregious delay might suffice "even absent tactical purpose" (at 9-10).

Not alleged: criminal-history increase. ECF 58 never mentions a criminal-history/guidelines increase from the RI conviction; that appears only in ECF 75 (see section 4). Loss of concurrent sentence IS alleged (at 8). Lost witnesses ARE alleged (at 9; Polanco and partners) but with no declarations, no dates of last contact, no description of what the partners would say beyond his own statements, and no showing they are unavailable.

Evidence of prosecutorial motive offered in ECF 58 (all inference or the movant's own account, except the exhibits):
- Shah's alleged "be quiet" message via Larsson (March 2021) (at 5);
- Exhibits 4 and 5 (emails among Shah/Barrett, discussed in section 3);
- prior knowledge theory (the "7 times" the government allegedly knew);
- timing: indictment after sentence reduction and after he finished the RI sentence.

## 2. ECF 72 (Memorandum and Order, Wolf, D.J., 4/26/2022, 2 pp.) (OCR confidence 92-93)

What it says:
- Recites that Kumar filed the pro se motion "on three alleged grounds: (1) double jeopardy; (2) vindictive prosecution; and (3) unconstitutional delay. See Dkt. No. 58" (ECF 72 at 1). It also cites the Petite-policy waiver letter (Dkt. 57).
- Fasoldt was stand-by counsel (Dkt. 53), "since been appointed as Kumar's fourth attorney" (Dkt. 65); "The court has been informed that Kumar still requests a ruling on his pro se Motion. See Order and Final Status Report (Dkt. No. 70)" (at 1).
- "Ordinarily, this court does not consider pro se motions filed by represented defendants" (citing Tracy, 989 F.2d 1279, 1285, and Lopez-Soto, 960 F.3d 1, 15) (at 1). But "the court has discretion and may consider such motions" (at 2). (Case names OCR-garbled: "Lépez-Soto"; "[defendant]"; reporter pages **[OCR-uncertain]**; verify cites before quoting.)
- ORDER: "by May 5, 2022, Kumar shall consult with Mr. Fasoldt and report: (1) whether he wishes to maintain the Motion; and (2) whether Mr. Fasoldt will supplement the Motion. Any supplement shall be filed by May 12, 2022." (at 2).
- Signature block appears to read "UNITED STATES DISTRICT [JUDGE]" (OCR truncated).

What it does NOT say: no findings on the merits; nothing on waiver; no ruling on delay or vindictiveness; no deadline for an election between claims; no warning that unbriefed or unsupplemented claims would be deemed abandoned or waived. The only reference to Kumar's pro se status is the "ordinarily no" / "discretion may consider" passage. It treats all three grounds identically as "alleged."

Cross-check against ECF 75: ECF 75 says counsel was "ordered to inform the Court on two matters ... (Doc. No. 74)" and filed a status report on May 6, 2022 "answering both questions in the affirmative" (ECF 75 at 1). So Kumar did elect to maintain the pro se motion, and counsel elected to supplement. The order is cited as Doc. 74 in ECF 75 but the memorandum in our file is Doc. 72, filed 4/26/2022 (possibly a docket-number discrepancy or a companion order; check the docket). The May 6 status report is not in our file.

## 4. ECF 75 (counsel's Supplemental Memorandum, 5/12/2022) compared with ECF 58

Confirmed by reading ECF 75 in full (5 pp.):
- It expressly "supplements the previously filed pro se Motion to Dismiss (Doc. No. 58)" (at 1). It does not withdraw or abandon ECF 58 or any ground of it.
- Procedural history recites that ECF 58 argued "a double jeopardy violation, vindictive prosecution, and unconstitutional delay" (at 1).
- Arguments: due process/fundamental fairness (Betts, Djokich) (at 2-3); Fed. R. Crim. P. 8 joinder (offenses "would have been eligible for joinder" with RI) (at 3); Blockburger vs. state mandatory-joinder statutes (Me., N.Y.) (at 3); the government "essentially given them two bites at the apple" (at 3); joinder in RI of unrelated pop-up fraud (at 4).
- Prejudice paragraph (at 4): cost "financial and temporal" of two venues; "criminal history is increased by the conviction from the District of Rhode Island"; "likely ... these charges and those in the Rhode Island case would have either grouped or negated each other ... resulting in a significantly lower potential sentence," citing USSG 3D1.1-1.5.
- No heading, authority or argument on vindictiveness. No Ramos-Gonzalez, Marion/Lovasco, or other delay authority. No discussion of delay, lost witnesses, Polanco, or tactical advantage.

So the premise is confirmed with two qualifications that matter:
1. ECF 75 does briefly touch two "prejudice" items from the delay theory without calling them delay: criminal-history increase (not in ECF 58) and sentencing-grouping/lost concurrency-type exposure (overlaps ECF 58 at 8, ground "no concurrent/combined resolution"). It also states the government "was aware of the conduct now charged at the time it proceeded against Mr. Kumar in Rhode Island" (at 3), which is the factual premise of the delay claim. It frames this as successive prosecution, not due-process delay.
2. "decision-making" language (at 4: "In considering the prosecutorial decision-making in this case, particularly the decision to separately and successively try these cases") gestures at motive but is not a vindictive prosecution claim; there is no allegation of retaliation, no mention of the sentence reduction or the Shah/Larsson statements.

## 5. Bearing on the Government's arguments

I have not read ECF 78 or ECF 80 or the transcripts; the following is limited to what ECF 58, 58-1, 72 and 75 show.

### (a) "Withdrawn" / waived (ECF 80: withdrawn "in light of his request for a Rule 11 Hearing (Doc. No. 78)")

Supports Kumar:
- Nothing in ECF 58, 72 or 75 withdraws any claim. ECF 72 required an election about maintaining the motion and supplementing; ECF 75 says counsel's May 6 status report said yes to both, and ECF 75 "supplements" ECF 58 rather than replacing it.
- ECF 72 sets no consequence for claims not repeated in a supplement and makes no waiver finding.
- Any withdrawal, therefore, must come from a later event (the Rule 11 request, Doc. 78, and any docket notation). I could not evaluate that from these files; it needs the ECF 78/80 text and any hearing transcript to see whether the withdrawal was express, limited to the motion as a whole, or with/without prejudice.

Hurts Kumar:
- ECF 75 is the only counsel-prepared statement of the claims and it abandons the vindictiveness and delay theories in substance. The Government can argue counsel made a strategic narrowing, which is the kind of choice that is hard to undo (and, per ECF 72, Kumar had been told to consult counsel on exactly this decision).
- ECF 75 recites that ECF 58 raised all three grounds, so the Government can say everyone knew the claims existed and counsel chose not to brief two of them.
- Whether Kumar knowingly personally concurred in narrowing is not shown by these documents (the May 6 status report is not in the file).

### (b) Was the delay claim ever fully developed?

No, on this record. The pro se ECF 58 states the elements and facts in a lay, discursive way and cites the right First Circuit standard (Ramos-Gonzalez bad faith/tactical purpose and the actual-prejudice requirement), and it does allege concrete prejudice (lost Polanco testimony, lost partners, lost concurrency, 10 extra months of detention, finances). But:
- there is no declaration or evidence supporting lost-witness prejudice, no proffer of what each witness would say, and no showing of when contact was lost;
- there is no developed link between the pre-indictment period and the prejudice. The period he identifies is from alleged government knowledge (end 2019/Feb. 2020) to the 5/17/2021 indictment, yet he was in custody throughout, with his partners cut off by his own 2019 arrest/incarceration and a 2020-21 divorce, not by the government's timing;
- counsel's ECF 75 did not brief it; ECF 72 made no findings; and, as far as these documents show, no hearing or ruling occurred on it before the later withdrawal.
So the claim was raised and partly pleaded, but never briefed by counsel, supported by evidence, or ruled on.

### (c) Government's "improper motive / substantial prejudice" argument

Hurts Kumar (candidly):
- Substantial prejudice: ECF 58's prejudice is largely assertion. Lost concurrency (Smith v. Hooey) concerns detainers/pending charges, not a prosecutor's choice to charge later, and Kumar's own sentence reduction had already lowered the earlier sentence. The Polanco "lost testimony" is speculative: his own account says Polanco explained the texts and "will get back to me," and he concedes Polanco would not now help him. The partners' availability is described as lost because of incarceration and divorce. Criminal-history increase appears only in ECF 75, framed as successive-prosecution prejudice, and is not tied to delay.
- Improper motive: Exhibit 5 (ECF 58-1 pp. 6-7) shows a legitimate, documented, cooperation-based reason for deferring indictment in mid-2020: Shah wanted to extend the indictment deadline because "we are in plea negotiations and that Defendant is considering cooperation," and defense counsel reported Kumar "does not want to be indicted" and would not object to the continuance. Exhibit 4 shows the plea negotiated with counsel; Barrett's reference to "potential charges" from Basil in Sept. 2020 shows Kumar was on notice charges were possible before his plea, which undercuts his claim that he pleaded on an "assurance" of no more charges and that the later indictment was a surprise. The motion itself concedes he refused further cooperation after 2/27/2020 and that the "threats" began then, which fits a charging-leverage story, not delay for tactical advantage against him. His own annotation, "planning on indictment for new charges as of July 30th," is an admission of notice. The date-of-knowledge inconsistency (end 2019 vs. Feb. 2021) weakens any showing of an unjustified gap.
- Many "evidence" items are Kumar's own handwritten gloss, and none establishes bad-faith delay.

Helps Kumar:
- Exhibits 4 and 5 are government-authored (Shah) statements that, as of 7/30/2020, new charges were under consideration ("planning on indictment for new charges as of July 30th" is Kumar's note, not Shah's words; the emails themselves reference "other crimes" and "potential charges"). They corroborate that the government was contemplating more charges while the RI case was pending, and Shah's July 2020 email shows the government chose to wait.
- Timing: Boston indictment 5/17/2021, roughly three months after the 2/11/2021 sentencing and shortly after the March 2021 sentence reduction. The alleged "be quiet or more charges" message (ECF 58 at 5) is direct motive evidence if proved, but it is double hearsay from Kumar and needs Larsson's testimony or a declaration.
- The Government's own prior-knowledge position (ECF 75 at 3) and the same agents in both cases support the inference that the government could have charged earlier.
- Because nothing in ECF 72 or 75 adjudicated these points, there is no finding against Kumar on motive or prejudice.
