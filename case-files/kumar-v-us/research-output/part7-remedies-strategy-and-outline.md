4. **Decide the COA question first.** Per Bui v. Seek expansion limited to **IAC Ground Two (Guidelines misadvice -> withdrawal of motion to dismiss and unconditional plea)** and, if the record supports, Ground Three (5K2.23). R. 46.5 (written request and statement of inability to pay addressed to the court). [Statutory text CONFIRMED via LII; Local Rule 46.5(a): "Every person ... desiring counsel and that the government pay for the expense of appeal ... shall address to this court a request in writing and a statement of the person's inability to pay."] Strong reasons: the case has novel questions (IAC-induced waiver; sentencing prejudice; COA expansion), a record dispute, pro se incarcerated appellant with no access to the district-court record, and a Government that relies on materials he cannot see. ## 3. What to lead with, what to concede, what to hold back

**Lead with** (first 1,200 words): (1) the standard "clearly appear that no substantial question is presented" and the COA's finding of substantiality; (2) the motion's core omissions: cause and prejudice; Tollett's own carve-out; Rule 12's actual text; Dugas's own words. These are verifiable, quoted from the Government's own authorities, and do not require us to prove anything about facts we cannot see.

**Concede candidly** (to preserve credibility; a pro se response that over-claims will lose the panel):
- The COA scope rule is real, the 7/1/2026 order tracks Local Rule 22.0(c)(2), Kumar did not move by 7/31, and Vargas-De Jesus/Peralta/Bui support the Government's *general* point. Ask for leave and expansion; do not argue the uncertified claims are properly before the Court "as of right."
- A proffer agreement would not have barred Count 3 (Apfelbaum). Say Kumar's claim is about Counts 1-2 and the derivative evidence, and about the pre-session advice, not about shielding the false statement.
- The First Circuit's pre-indictment delay test is conjunctive and demanding; Kumar's merits claim is novel (sentencing-based prejudice), and the Court need not decide it now.
- Direct-appeal waiver principles (Concepcion-Guliam, Rodriguez) are correct as stated; our point is that they do not answer cause and prejudice.

**Hold back / do not volunteer:** (i) the Sixth Amendment attachment issue (Cobb/McNeil) for pre-charge proffer sessions; (ii) the question of who advised Kumar on the Guidelines before the withdrawal (the RECAP docket shows Fasoldt was appointed counsel from 3/15/2022 -- ECF 65 -- a fact Kumar's brief omits when it says "pro se with standby counsel"; confirm with ECF 78-93 before asserting that counsel gave the 15-20 estimate; if the estimate came from Kumar's own reading, do not assert IAC for that step and frame Ground Two as pre-plea advice at the plea stage); (iii) Kumar's internal inconsistencies in the Guideline numbers until we have the PSR; (iv) the notice-of-appeal timing issue (Flag A) -- but we must resolve it privately first, because a jurisdictional defect would moot the strategy.
