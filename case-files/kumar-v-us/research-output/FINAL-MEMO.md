### Ranked arguments (strongest to weakest) -- with honest ratings

1. **Rule 27.0(c) is not satisfied: the two certified issues were found by a federal judge to present a "substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right" (28 U.S.C. 2253(c)(2)-(3)), and Miller-El says a COA means the case gets "full consideration."** Rating: moderate-strong, persuasive not controlling. The COA standard is not the same as "no substantial question," and the panel can disagree.
2. **Dugas v. Coplan is a mismatch for the Government's "clear error" proposition.** It is a 2254 case; its text applies clear-error review to fact-findings made after an evidentiary hearing and says "When the district court undertakes no independent factfinding ... the district court's recension of that record will engender de novo review." If ECF 130 decided the proffer-prejudice question on the papers, review is de novo. Rating: strong but narrow (we must confirm there was no hearing).
3. **The Government never addresses cause and prejudice, and Tollett itself preserves a counsel-advice attack on the voluntary and intelligent character of a plea** ("He may only attack the voluntary and intelligent character of the guilty plea by showing that the advice he received from counsel was not within the standards set forth in McMann," 411 U.S. at 267). Concepcion-Guliam and Rodriguez are direct-appeal, no-IAC cases; Maldonado (1st Cir. 2026) repeats the carve-out. Rating: moderate. The docket helps (Attorney Fasoldt was appointed counsel on 3/15/2022, ECF 65), but we do not know who gave the 15-20 month Guidelines estimate.

5. **The COA on the delay claim implies the waiver ruling is debatable (Slack, 529 U.S. at 484), so the Government's waiver defenses are part of the certified issue and cannot support summary affirmance.** Rating: moderate-weak.
6. **The IAC/proffer prejudice ruling rests on an unseen letter and the word "typically"; the signed letter cannot be found; no hearing; Hill requires a fact-based prediction about derived evidence.** Rating: moderate-weak: the burden is Kumar's, Count 3 cannot be protected (Apfelbaum), and the independence of the Government's evidence is the key unknown.

## PART III -- IAC / PROFFER-PROTECTION CLAIM (COA Issue 1)
Full analysis: `part3-iac-proffer-claim.md`.
- **Standard:** Strickland; Hill, 474 U.S. at 59 ("a reasonable probability that, but for counsel's errors, he would not have pleaded guilty and would have insisted on going to trial"); Hill's failure-to-investigate gloss (prejudice depends on a "prediction whether the evidence likely would have changed the outcome of a trial"); Lee, 582 U.S. 357 (focus is the defendant's decision-making, "post hoc assertions" vs. "contemporaneous evidence"). Caramadre, 807 F.3d at 371 is accurately quoted (star page confirmed).
- **Standard of review:** Dugas quotes (verbatim in Part 3 sec. 3). Do not say "clear error is wrong"; say it applies after a hearing and the record shows none.
- **Hearing:** 2255(b) text; McGill and David are unfriendly (abuse of discretion; petitioner's burden). Rating moderate-weak; strongest as an alternative request for a limited hearing on (i) letter terms, (ii) pre-session advice, (iii) derived v. independent evidence.
- **What proffer protection can and cannot do:** Count 3 (18 U.S.C. 1001) is not protectable (Apfelbaum, 445 U.S. 115; Mezzanatto, 513 U.S. 196; Kastigar concerns compelled, statutorily immunized testimony). The claim must be framed around Counts 1-2 and derivative evidence, which depends on facts we lack: how the devices/accounts were accessed (Kumar says he "authorized access to his personal electronic devices and accounts" during the sessions).
- **"Typically":** the district court's own word shows reasoning from generalities; the signed letter cannot be found (ECF 122 at 3 n.2, per Kumar's brief).
- **USSG 3E1.1 n.2 / "overwhelming evidence":** an incentive-to-plead argument, but the plea was open (no bargain), whether Kumar received acceptance credit is unknown (court found his allocution insincere), and the "overwhelming evidence" cited is PSR recitation with no showing of independence. Lee: inquiry "focuses on a defendant's decisionmaking." But if the evidence was in fact independent, Hill prejudice likely fails.
- **Risk not raised by the Government:** Sixth Amendment attachment (Cobb; McNeil).

## PART IV -- PRE-INDICTMENT DELAY (COA Issue 2): THE TEST AND THE THREE "WAIVERS"
Full analysis: `part4-preindictment-delay-and-waiver.md`.
- **Test (BINDING):** conjunctive: "substantial prejudice to his right to a fair trial" AND intentional delay "to gain a tactical advantage" / "bad faith" (Irizarry-Colon, 848 F.3d at 70; Bater at 54; Crooks at 11; Royle: "deliberate misconduct by the prosecutor (or at least something very close to that)"). The Government's "improper motive" paraphrase is fair; the First Circuit does not use a balancing test. Stokes (124 F.3d at 44) and McCoy (977 F.2d at 711) call concurrent-sentence prejudice speculative; Irizarry-Colon and Crooks treat delay to charge co-conspirators together as legitimate.
- **Waiver (i) withdrawal:** Rodriguez and Concepcion-Guliam are accurately quoted but are direct-appeal cases without a cause-and-prejudice argument; waiver requires knowing relinquishment (Zerbst); Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. at 488: "Ineffective assistance of counsel, then, is cause for a procedural default."
- **Waiver (ii) Rule 12(b)(3)(A)(ii):** Kumar raised the claim (ECF 58, 75); every cited case involves no motion at all; 12(c)(3) provides "good cause."
- **Waiver (iii) Tollett/Class:** Tollett's carve-out; Hill ties Strickland's first prong to Tollett/McMann; Maldonado (2026) is adverse on Class/Tollett for grand-jury claims but repeats the carve-out; Gaffney declined to apply Tollett; Class says Blackledge (a collateral vindictive-prosecution case) survived a plea.
- **Procedural default:** not asserted in the motion; Trest and Wood v. Milyard (2254/limitations context) support a forfeiture argument if ECF 122 did not raise it; read ECF 122/130.
- **COA effect:** Slack: COA on a procedurally rejected claim implies the procedural ruling is debatable.
- **Merits:** weak (Part 4 sec. 6); Kumar's own numbers need verification.
