### The concrete dollar exposure comparison

**If Kim's disputed $800,000.00 (and potentially the additional $110,000.00 in trust-related deficiency claims — $80,000.00 and $30,000.00) are found to be §523(a)(15) property-settlement debts** (i.e., the characterization Brad's own schedules currently assume for everything beyond the $200,000.00 he has already conceded as DSO):
- They remain **general unsecured claims**, sharing pro rata in the $1,255,000.00+ general-unsecured pool.
- They **cannot reach any of the $1,116,025.00 in exempt property.** Exempt property — above all the $740,000.00 Vanguard 401(k) and the $350,000.00 Gulfstream ERISA plan, together $1,090,000.00 (97.7% of the exemption) — is off-limits to a property-settlement creditor inside the bankruptcy case. Kim's only recourse for that debt would be nondischargeability under §523(a)(15), meaning she could try to collect post-bankruptcy from Brad's *future*, *non-exempt* earnings/assets — not from the exempt corpus the Chapter 7 estate walls off today.
- Recovery inside the case, if any, would come only from the $1,065,351.00 non-exempt pool, after the $575,000.00 in priority claims are satisfied first — leaving at most **$490,351.00** ($1,065,351.00 − $575,000.00) to be split pro rata among **all** general unsecured creditors (a pool that, including her own $800,000.00–$910,000.00, plus $455,000.00 from other named unsecured creditors, plus B.B.'s and Gulfstream's undetermined "Unknown" claims, likely exceeds $2 million) — i.e., a fractional-cents-on-the-dollar recovery, if the case is administered as an asset case at all with respect to this tier.

**If instead Kim's disputed claims are found to be DSO entitled to §507(a)(1)(A) priority and §522(c)(1) reach-through:**
- They **jump to the very top of the priority waterfall** — ahead of the $375,000.00 in tax priority claims (IRS $300,000.00 + NJ Division of Taxation $75,000.00) and ahead of the entire $1,255,000.00+ general-unsecured pool (including B.B.'s far larger, currently "Unknown"-valued claim, which the record elsewhere pegs at roughly $1,000,000.00 in prejudgment attachment).
- They become able to **reach into the full $1,116,025.00 of exempt property** — property that is otherwise completely shielded from every other creditor in this case, disputed or not. Practically, that means the $740,000.00 401(k) and the $350,000.00 ERISA plan — the two assets that make up nearly all of what Brad has left after the ~$975,000.00 sitting in attorney trust accounts (itself already non-exempt and reachable by everyone) — go from **fully protected** to **fully exposed**.

**Net additional exposure created by a DSO finding, expressed in dollars:**
- **Up to $1,116,025.00** of currently-shielded exempt property becomes reachable that would otherwise never be reachable by Kim (or by anyone) under a property-settlement characterization — this is the direct effect of §522(c)(1).
- On top of that, Kim's $800,000.00 (or, if the trust-related claims also get recharacterized, up to $910,000.00) leapfrogs **$375,000.00** in tax-priority claims and **$1,255,000.00+** in general unsecured claims for first access to whatever is distributable — this is the direct effect of §507(a)(1)(A).
- Put simply: **a DSO finding turns Kim's currently-disputed $800,000.00–$910,000.00 claim from "shares pro rata in a $490,351.00 non-exempt residue with $2+ million of competing claims" into "gets paid first, dollar-for-dollar, out of a $1,116,025.00 exempt pool that every other creditor in the case is legally barred from touching."** That is the entire practical stake of the characterization fight — roughly a swing of well over a million dollars in what Kim can actually collect versus what every other creditor (including B.B., the IRS, NJ Division of Taxation, Courtney Mell, and Diana Mell) will be left fighting over.
