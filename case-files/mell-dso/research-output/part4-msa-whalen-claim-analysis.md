## Step 3 — Itemized MSA Provisions (What the MSA Actually Says)

### MSA-1: Section 3.5(a) — W.H. Mell Capital Account
- **Location:** Article 3 ("Bank, Brokerage and Investment Accounts, Retirement Accounts, and Business Interests"), Section 3.5, subsection (a). Pages ~13–17 of the MSA (Bates KM00036–KM00039).
- **MSA's own label:** "Husband's Business Interest" (falls generally under Article 3's equitable-distribution framework, not Article 6 "Support and Maintenance").
- **Verbatim/near-verbatim quote:** "The Husband maintained a Capital Account at W.H. Mell (equity interest) that he represents had a balance in the approximate amount of $1,625,000.00 as of July 28, 2018, subject to distributions to the Husband and Wife (as set forth below), adjustment for business debt, marital lifestyle expenses and estimated tax payments for 2018... The Husband and the Wife acknowledge that the Capital Account is a marital asset and that repayment of any balance in the Capital Account shall be made by way of installments. **The parties recognize that the first $800,000.00 has already been paid out, with Wife, having received sixty percent (60%) and Husband having received forty percent (40%) of those funds.** Husband represents that his share was utilized to pay legal fees. **Husband also represents that there are currently no remaining funds/balance in the Capital Account.** However, the utilization of proceeds/balance of the Capital Account (i.e., disbursements) after the first $800,000 is currently in dispute in whole or in part. Said disbursements shall be reviewed by [forensic accountant] Mr. Bloomfield to determine if he believes in his opinion they are disputed... Any Disputed Disbursements shall be submitted to mediation... If mediation is not successful, the disputed items shall be submitted to a mutually selected and agreed or court appointed arbitrator for resolution by way of binding arbitration... [T]he parties agree that the remaining W. H. Mell Capital Account, if any, after resolving any Disputed Disbursements under the terms set forth herein, shall be equitably distributed between the parties and Wife shall receive sixty percent (60%) and the Husband shall receive forty percent (40%) of those funds."
- **Amount:** $1,625,000.00 total balance represented as of 7/28/18; **first $800,000 already distributed 60/40 (Wife = $480,000 / Husband = $320,000)**; remaining balance, if any, subject to a defined Bloomfield-review → mediation → binding-arbitration dispute-resolution mechanism before any further 60/40 split applies. Husband represented "no remaining funds/balance" existed as of the MSA's execution.
- **Payment terms:** "By way of installments"; documentation to be provided to Wife "within seven (7) days of receipt by the Husband."
- **Indemnification:** "The Husband shall indemnify and hold the Wife harmless from any liabilities regarding the dissolution of W.H. Mell."

- **Location:** Article 5 ("Personalty and Other Property"), Section 5.3. Pages ~25–26 (Bates KM00047–KM00048).

- **Verbatim/near-verbatim quote:** "The parties recognize and agree that in connection with the purchase of the Regulator 41 Boat in July 2016, the Wife and the Husband borrowed the principal sum of approximately $590,000.00 from the Kimberly Ruggles Mell 2012 Family Trust ('KRMFT') and the Steven Bradley Mell 2012 Family Trust ('SBMFT')... ### MSA-6: Article 7.1 — Trusts Established During the Marriage; the "Reclassified Assets" Clause; Bedminster Property
- **Location:** Article 7, Sections 7.1(a)–(d). Pages ~33–37 (Bates KM00056–KM00059).
- **MSA's own label:** "ARTICLE 7: TRUSTS ESTABLISHED DURING THE MARRIAGE."
- **Trust identification (7.1(a)):** Lists nine trusts, including the "Steven Bradley Mell 2012 Family Trust (est. in 2012)" (beneficiaries: Wife and children) and "Kimberly Ruggles Mell 2012 Family Trust (est. in 2012)" (beneficiaries: children).
- **"Reclassified Assets" clawback (7.1(c)):** "[I]n the event of any potential change in asset status from Trust property to marital property, each of the parties' respective interests in any such asset (a 'Reclassified Asset') is properly identified... the Wife shall be entitled to **50% of such Reclassified Assets for Equitable Distribution, together with an additional 25% to defray the Husband's support obligations**... This claim of 25% (or one-half of Husband's share) shall not exceed the aggregate sum of **$5,000,000.00**." (Example given: on $10M of Reclassified Assets, Wife would receive $5M + up to $2.5M.) This is the only MSA mechanism under which a Trust-held asset that a court later "reclassifies" as marital could generate a Kim recovery beyond the 60/40 splits elsewhere in the MSA — **but it applies only to Trust assets that get judicially reclassified, and it is capped at $5,000,000 in the aggregate for the 25% "support" component.**
- **Bedminster Property (7.1(d)):** Confirms the marital home is owned by "Steven Bradley Mell 2012 Family Trust (30%) and the Kimberly Ruggles Mell 2012 Family Trust (70%)" — i.e., a **Trust asset, not a Mell marital asset**, consistent with Whalen's own Statement of Material Facts (¶¶46-49, 82-96) conceding the same point.
- **Relevance to WNFW/helicopter:** WNFW was never a Trust asset — it was a direct 99%/1% individual LLC membership interest (MSA-3, above), assigned outright to Husband by the MSA itself. The Reclassified-Assets clawback (7.1(c)) is therefore not the correct MSA mechanism for a helicopter/WNFW claim, since there is no Trust-to-marital "reclassification" at issue for an asset the MSA already treated as individually owned and already assigned to Husband as separate property.

### MSA-8: Section 10.2(b) — Husband's Indemnification of Wife for Claims Arising From Husband's Conduct (the B.B.-type indemnification clause)
- **Location:** Article 10 ("Waiver of Property Rights, Releases and Bankruptcy"), Section 10.2(b). Pages ~43–45 (Bates KM00066–KM00067).
- **MSA's own label:** "10.2 Releases" (subsection (b)).
- **Verbatim quote:** "[T]he Wife shall be entitled to assert any and all actions, suits, debts, claims, demands and obligations whatsoever, both in law and in equity she may possess against the Husband, in the event that: (a) litigation, arbitration, or other form of dispute resolution is, or has been, commenced by a third-party asserting any actions, suits, debts and claims, demands and obligations whatsoever, either in law and in equity, against or arising out of conduct, or alleged conduct, by the Husband, and (b) claims are asserted against the Wife and/or any Trusts identified in Article 7 herein in any litigation... in connection with conduct or alleged conduct of the Husband... **The Husband agrees to indemnify, defend and hold the Wife harmless from and against any and all actions, suits, debts, claims, demands, and obligations whatsoever, arising from, relating to, or in connection with the Husband's conduct. This indemnification shall include, without limitation, reasonable counsel fees and costs to the extent adjudicated by a Court.** This provision shall not bar the Husband from defending the third party claims against the Wife."
- **Amount:** No fixed dollar amount — open-ended indemnification for third-party claims and "reasonable counsel fees and costs," but **expressly limited to fees/costs "to the extent adjudicated by a Court."**
- **Notice/consent-to-settle conditions:** No express notice-to-Husband or consent-to-settle condition appears in this clause as drafted (unlike a typical third-party indemnification agreement). The clause does preserve Husband's right to defend the third-party claims himself ("This provision shall not bar the Husband from defending the third party claims against the Wife").
- **This is the MSA provision that corresponds to Whalen's Item W-7 (BB indemnification claim).**

## Step 4 — Cross-Reference and Reconciliation (Narrative)

### A. W.H. Mell Capital Account — is it already paid?

The MSA (MSA-1) shows Kim's 60% share of the *only* funds the MSA identifies as actually distributed from this account — the first $800,000 — was **$480,000**. Whalen's own later-filed Statement of Material Facts (Exhibit 3, ¶64) **confirms Kim did in fact receive exactly $480,000**: "she would only ever receive $480,000." This corroborates our understanding that Brad already paid Kim her Capital Account distribution as calculated under the MSA's own stated mechanism (60% of the $800,000 already paid out).

Whalen's SOF then characterizes the *shortfall* by comparing $480,000 against 60% of the *entire* $1,625,000 represented balance ($975,000), implying an approximately **$495,000** gap. But the MSA does not entitle Kim to 60% of the full $1.625M balance as a matter of course — it expressly limits the *automatic* 60/40 split to the first $800,000 (already paid), and conditions any further recovery on the "Disputed Disbursements" being identified by forensic accountant Mark Bloomfield and then resolved through mediation and, if necessary, **binding arbitration** — a contractual process, not an automatic entitlement. The MSA also records **Husband's own representation, made in April 2019, that "there are currently no remaining funds/balance in the Capital Account."** No Bloomfield report, mediation outcome, or arbitration award appears anywhere in the record reviewed.

Separately, Whalen's earlier-filed Crossclaims (Exhibit 1, ¶11) value the *combined* Gulfstream-plus-Capital-Account shortfall at only **$175,000** — which, netting out the $120,000 Gulfstream figure (MSA-2, which is well-documented and fixed), would leave only **~$55,000** attributable to the Capital Account piece. That is irreconcilable with the ~$495,000 figure in the same law firm's later Statement of Material Facts filed roughly three months afterward. **This is a significant, unresolved internal inconsistency in Whalen's own filings** (see Reconciliation Table, Item 2).

### B. Helicopter / WNFW — the central discrepancy

This is the most significant reconciliation point. The MSA (MSA-3) states plainly, as of April 2019:
1. WNFW LLC — not Brad or Kim individually, and not a Trust — was the entity that "owns a helicopter";
2. "the liabilities exceed the assets of this entity" (i.e., WNFW had *negative* net equity at the time of the MSA); and
3. Kim's 1% membership interest was assigned to Brad, who "shall retain this asset as his **sole and separate property**" — expressly *not* subject to further equitable distribution.

**Whalen's own later-filed Statement of Material Facts (Exhibit 3, ¶¶76–77) concedes exactly this**: "At page 23 of the MSA, the parties deal with a company known as WFNW LLC. Upon information and belief, this company owned and operated a Helicopter... Once again, through the Mell MSA, it would appear that Defendant Brad Mell (the actual Debtor in this case), did quite well. **He was granted 100% of this company asset. Kim got zero.**"

Yet Whalen's earlier-filed Crossclaims (Exhibit 1, ¶¶21-24) assert, "Upon information and belief, the helicopter was a marital asset," and that "all of or the majority of the helicopter sale proceeds... belong to her" — a position **directly contradicted by Whalen's own later Statement of Material Facts** admitting the MSA gave Brad 100% ownership of the WNFW/helicopter asset as his separate property.

Beyond the ownership question, the actual closing statement for the January 5, 2021 sale (`Augusta 109 E sale Jan 5 2021.pdf`) shows the following disbursement from the **$1,810,000.00** gross sale price of N109BM:

| Disbursement | Amount |
|---|---|
| AEROtitle (Seller's ½ Escrow Fee) | $1,175.00 |
| Unibank for Savings (mortgage Payoff) | $1,455,274.16 |
| Evan's Helicopter Services (Commission) | $90,500.00 |
| Diana Donlin Mell (Invoice) | $75,000.00 |
| Pratt & Whitney ESP Program (invoice) | $29,232.36 |
| Sterling Helicopters (invoice) | $42,242.34 |
| Platinum Helicopters LLC (invoice) | $79,867.62 |
| **WNFW LLC (Sales Proceeds)** | **$36,708.52** |
| **Total** | **$1,810,000.00** |

After the outstanding mortgage payoff (~$1.455M) and commission/maintenance/escrow bills (~$317,000 combined), only **$36,708.52** in net proceeds reached WNFW LLC — the entity that, per the MSA, was Brad's sole and separate property. Whalen's crossclaim references "approximately $1.8M" and asks for "all of or the majority of" that gross figure, without accounting for the mortgage payoff or the maintenance/commission bills reflected in the closing statement, and without engaging at all with the MSA's own admission (echoed in Whalen's later SOF) that WNFW belonged 100% to Brad individually, not to the marital estate.

**Whalen's filing does not dispute the WNFW ownership structure, the sale, the mortgage payoff, or the maintenance costs — it simply does not address them.** The Crossclaims' "marital asset"/"majority of proceeds" theory (Item W-6) is unsupported by, and contradicted by, both the MSA itself and Whalen's own later Statement of Material Facts.

### C. Indemnification (B.B. lawsuit) — amount undisclosed

MSA Section 10.2(b) (MSA-8) is a real, broadly-worded indemnification clause covering third-party claims "arising from...the Husband's conduct," including "reasonable counsel fees and costs **to the extent adjudicated by a Court**." Whalen's Crossclaims (Item W-7) track this clause's substance closely but never state a dollar amount. The Stipulation of Dismissal (Exhibit 4) confirms Kim settled with B.B. — "amicably adjusted," dismissed with prejudice, "without costs or fees to any party" — but **discloses no settlement amount**, and nothing in the record shows Brad received notice of, or consented to, that settlement (a gap made more significant because MSA §10.2(b) limits the fee-indemnification component specifically to court-adjudicated fees, not a privately negotiated settlement figure). Kim/Whalen's position (per the Certification, Item W-1) is only that the total value of her claims — indemnification included — is "significantly more than $200,000," without breaking out what portion of that figure is attributable to indemnification specifically.

### D. Kim's personal legal fees

Whalen's filings do not distinguish between fees Kim paid personally and fees potentially paid by a trust of which she is trustee/beneficiary (e.g., the Kimberly Ruggles Mell 2012 Family Trust). No invoice, retainer agreement, or fee statement showing the source of payment for Kim's litigation costs (in either the B.B. action or this bankruptcy) appears anywhere in the documents reviewed. This is a gap in Whalen's own record, not an affirmative MSA-based entitlement — flagged here as an open item for discovery/rebuttal rather than a reconcilable MSA provision, since the MSA's fee provisions (MSA-8, MSA-10, MSA-11) all condition fee-shifting on either court adjudication or a specific default/enforcement scenario, not on a bare assertion that fees were incurred.

### E. Alimony

No specific dollar figure for alimony arrears appears in any Whalen filing reviewed. This is consistent with the MSA (MSA-5), which left alimony "open durational" and unquantified, with lifestyle issues "irrelevant" at present and expressly deferred to a future determination that, on this record, does not appear to have occurred.

## Step 6 — Totaling the Gap

**What Whalen's filings, taken together, appear to assert Brad owes Kim:**
- A stated combined figure of **$175,000** (Crossclaims, Oct. 2025) for the Gulfstream + Capital Account items; **or**
- If the later Statement of Material Facts' Capital Account shortfall figure (~$495,000) is used instead of the Crossclaims' implied ~$55,000 for that same item, the combined Gulfstream + Capital Account total rises to roughly **$615,000** ($120,000 + $495,000);
- **Plus** an express rejection of any "$200,000 cap," with the aggregate value of Kim's claims characterized only as "significantly more" than that, without ever being reduced to a single total figure;
- **Plus** open-ended, unquantified, contingent claims to (i) "all or the majority" of a ~$1.8M gross helicopter sale, (ii) "all or the majority" of the boat sale proceeds, (iii) the W.H. Mell going-concern business value, and (iv) unspecified B.B.-litigation indemnification/legal fees.

No single, internally consistent aggregate dollar figure is ever stated by Whalen across the documents reviewed. Depending on which of Whalen's own inconsistent figures is used for the Capital Account, and whether the helicopter claim is valued at its asserted gross ($1.8M) or "majority" thereof, the implied aggregate exposure narrative ranges from roughly **$175,000** to well over **$2 million**.

**What the MSA, plus the documented record, actually supports as presently quantifiable and outstanding:**
- **$120,000** — Gulfstream Annual Net Profit reimbursement (MSA Art. 4.1(a)(3)(ii)); the one clean, fixed, MSA-grounded, presently quantifiable figure in the record, with no evidence either way in the documents reviewed as to whether it has been paid.
- **$0 additional** on the Capital Account — the $480,000 the MSA's stated mechanism entitled Kim to (60% of the first $800,000) appears, per Whalen's own Statement of Material Facts, to have already been received; any further amount depends on an undocumented Disputed-Disbursements/arbitration process with no evidenced outcome.
- **$0** on the helicopter/WNFW claim — the MSA assigned WNFW 100% to Brad as separate property in 2019 (with liabilities already exceeding assets), and the actual 2021 sale left only $36,708.52 in net proceeds to that entity, not the ~$1.8M (or "majority" thereof) Whalen's crossclaim implies.
- **Unquantifiable on this record** — the boat (formula exists in MSA §5.3, but no sale figures reviewed), the B.B. indemnification (clause exists in MSA §10.2(b), but no amount, adjudication, or notice/consent showing is in the record), and the W.H. Mell business-value claim (no MSA provision addresses it at all, and Whalen directs that theory at Mountainside, not Brad).
- **$0 fixed** on alimony — left open and undetermined by the MSA itself.

**Single largest driver of the discrepancy:** The **helicopter/WNFW claim** (Item W-6 / Reconciliation Table Item 5). It is the largest dollar figure Whalen's filing puts in play (an implied claim to "all or the majority" of a $1.8M gross sale), and it is also the most directly and completely contradicted by both the MSA's own text (100% assignment to Brad as separate property, with liabilities already exceeding assets in 2019) and by Whalen's own later-filed Statement of Material Facts, which expressly concedes "[Brad] was granted 100% of this company asset. Kim got zero." The gap between the ~$1.8M figure invoked in the crossclaim and the documented $36,708.52 in actual net proceeds to Brad's separate-property entity — after the mortgage payoff and maintenance/commission bills — is on the order of **$1.76 million**, dwarfing every other item in this reconciliation.
