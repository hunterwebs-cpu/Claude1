Subject: Whalen's Asserted Claims vs. MSA Terms -- Discrepancies and Questions Before We Respond

Sodono --

I went through the MSA against Whalen's Doc. 23 (Certification and Exhibits 1-4) line by line and want to flag several places where Whalen's numbers don't match the MSA's own terms, plus a few questions I need your read on before we finalize anything.

1. Indemnification vs. contribution on the B.B. claim -- this may matter a lot.
MSA Sec. 10.2(b) is an indemnification clause only -- I indemnify, defend, and hold Kim harmless from third-party claims "arising from... the Husband's conduct," with fee-shifting limited to costs "adjudicated by a Court." The word "contribution" doesn't appear anywhere in that section.

If what Whalen is actually pursuing at trial is contribution -- i.e., Kim paid or settled with B.B. on her own exposure and is now seeking my proportionate share as a co-obligor/joint tortfeasor -- that's a different claim under ordinary tort/contribution law, not one that flows from the MSA at all. If that's the theory, it shouldn't get the benefit of MSA Sec. 10.3's nondischargeability language or a Sec. 523(a)(15) "in connection with a separation agreement" argument, since it isn't a claim "to a spouse" arising from the divorce -- it would exist between any two co-defendants. That would put it on ordinary dischargeable footing in Chapter 7.

Can you tell me how Kim's claim is actually pled for trial -- indemnification under the MSA, or contribution as a co-obligor/joint tortfeasor? That distinction changes the analysis substantially.

Related: the B.B.-Kim Stipulation of Dismissal (Ex. 4) doesn't disclose a settlement amount, and I don't see anything in the record showing I received notice of or consented to that settlement -- which matters because Sec. 10.2(b)'s fee-indemnification is limited to court-adjudicated costs, not a privately negotiated number.

2. Helicopter/WNFW -- this is the largest discrepancy by far.
MSA Art. 4.1(a)(4) assigned WNFW 100% to me as my sole and separate property in 2019, expressly stating "the liabilities exceed the assets of this entity." Whalen's own later-filed Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, paras. 76-77) concedes this outright: "He was granted 100% of this company asset. Kim got zero."

Yet Whalen's earlier Crossclaims (Ex. 1, paras. 21-24) assert the helicopter "was a marital asset" and claim "all of or the majority of" a ~$1.8M gross sale -- directly contradicted by her own later filing. I also have the actual January 2021 closing statement: after the $1,455,274.16 mortgage payoff and roughly $317,000 in commission/maintenance/escrow costs, only $36,708.52 in net proceeds reached WNFW. The gap between the ~$1.8M implied and the $36,708.52 actually realized is about $1.76 million.

3. W.H. Mell Capital Account -- appears already paid, and Whalen's own filings disagree with each other by ~$440,000.
MSA Sec. 3.5(a) shows the first $800,000 already distributed 60/40, giving Kim $480,000. Whalen's Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, para. 64) confirms she received exactly that. My April 2019 representation in the MSA states no further balance remained, with any "Disputed Disbursements" gated on a Bloomfield forensic review, then mediation, then binding arbitration.

Whalen's Crossclaims (Ex. 1, para. 11) value the combined Gulfstream-plus-Capital-Account shortfall at $175,000 total -- which nets out to roughly $55,000 for the Capital Account piece after backing out the $120,000 Gulfstream figure. Her later Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, paras. 63-65) instead implies a ~$495,000 shortfall on the same item. Those two numbers, from the same filings, are off by about $440,000.

Do I have (or can I get) any Bloomfield report or arbitration outcome on this? Without it, I don't think Whalen has any documentary basis for a number beyond what's already been paid.

4. Gulfstream Annual Net Profit -- the one clean, fixed figure.
MSA Art. 4.1(a)(3)(ii) fixes this at $120,000, payable in $30,000 quarterly installments starting 6/1/2020. I don't have anything in my records showing whether I paid this. Can you confirm status?

5. Dischargeability -- I want to make sure we're aligned before I go further with Kim's "it's all support" framing.
I understand Kim is going to try to frame everything -- Capital Account, Gulfstream, WNFW, the boat, all of it -- as child support to lock in the strongest protection. Before I respond to that, I want to check my understanding against what you told me: I thought you said only child support is nondischargeable in Chapter 7. That doesn't match what I'm reading -- Sec. 101(14A) defines "domestic support obligation" to include spousal support by name, and Sec. 523(a)(5) makes any DSO nondischargeable with no ability-to-pay exception. Separately, Sec. 523(a)(15) makes property-settlement debts to a former spouse nondischargeable in Chapter 7 too, post-BAPCPA (the old ability-to-pay defenses under (a)(15) were deleted by the 2005 amendments). Multiple courts, including one in this District (Langman, 465 B.R. at 406, quoting Golio, 393 B.R. at 61), have said the DSO/property-settlement distinction is "of no practical consequence in determining the dischargeability of the debt" in Chapter 7 -- because both categories are nondischargeable anyway.

If that's right, then Kim's "call it all child support" strategy doesn't actually change whether these items are dischargeable -- they're nondischargeable regardless of the label. Where I think the label would matter is (a) Sec. 522(c)(1) -- exempt property is liable for a DSO but shielded from an (a)(15) property-settlement debt, so if I have meaningful exempt property, the label decides what she can reach; and (b) priority under Sec. 507(a)(1)(A) in an asset case.

Can you clarify what you meant by "only child support" -- were you speaking to Chapter 13 specifically (where Sec. 1328(a)(2) discharges (a)(15) debts on plan completion but not (a)(5)), or something else? I want to make sure I'm not working from a wrong premise before this goes further.
