Subject: Whalen's Asserted Claims vs. MSA Terms — Discrepancies and Questions Before We Respond

Sodono —

We went through the MSA against Whalen's Doc. 23 (Certification and Exhibits 1–4) line by line and want to flag several places where Whalen's numbers don't match the MSA's own terms, plus a few questions we need your read on before we finalize anything.

**1. Indemnification vs. contribution on the B.B. claim — this may matter a lot.**
MSA §10.2(b) is an indemnification clause only — Brad indemnifies, defends, and holds Kim harmless from third-party claims "arising from... the Husband's conduct," with fee-shifting limited to costs "adjudicated by a Court." The word "contribution" doesn't appear anywhere in that section.

If what Whalen is actually pursuing at trial is contribution — i.e., Kim paid or settled with B.B. on her own exposure and is now seeking Brad's proportionate share as a co-obligor/joint tortfeasor — that's a different claim under ordinary tort/contribution law, not one that flows from the MSA at all. If that's the theory, it shouldn't get the benefit of MSA §10.3's nondischargeability language or a §523(a)(15) "in connection with a separation agreement" argument, since it isn't a claim "to a spouse" arising from the divorce — it would exist between any two co-defendants. That would put it on ordinary dischargeable footing in Chapter 7.

Can you tell us how Kim's claim is actually pled for trial — indemnification under the MSA, or contribution as a co-obligor/joint tortfeasor? That distinction changes our analysis substantially.

Related: the B.B.–Kim Stipulation of Dismissal (Ex. 4) doesn't disclose a settlement amount, and we see nothing in the record showing Brad received notice of or consented to that settlement — which matters because §10.2(b)'s fee-indemnification is limited to court-adjudicated costs, not a privately negotiated number.

**2. Helicopter/WNFW — this is the largest discrepancy by far.**
MSA Art. 4.1(a)(4) assigned WNFW 100% to Brad as his sole and separate property in 2019, expressly stating "the liabilities exceed the assets of this entity." Whalen's own later-filed Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, ¶¶76–77) concedes this outright: "He was granted 100% of this company asset. Kim got zero."

Yet Whalen's earlier Crossclaims (Ex. 1, ¶¶21–24) assert the helicopter "was a marital asset" and claim "all of or the majority of" a ~$1.8M gross sale — directly contradicted by her own later filing. We also have the actual January 2021 closing statement: after the $1,455,274.16 mortgage payoff and roughly $317,000 in commission/maintenance/escrow costs, only $36,708.52 in net proceeds reached WNFW. The gap between the ~$1.8M implied and the $36,708.52 actually realized is about $1.76 million.

**3. W.H. Mell Capital Account — appears already paid, and Whalen's own filings disagree with each other by ~$440,000.**
MSA §3.5(a) shows the first $800,000 already distributed 60/40, giving Kim $480,000. Whalen's Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, ¶64) confirms she received exactly that. Brad's April 2019 representation in the MSA states no further balance remained, with any "Disputed Disbursements" gated on a Bloomfield forensic review, then mediation, then binding arbitration.

Whalen's Crossclaims (Ex. 1, ¶11) value the combined Gulfstream-plus-Capital-Account shortfall at $175,000 total — which nets out to roughly $55,000 for the Capital Account piece after backing out the $120,000 Gulfstream figure. Her later Statement of Material Facts (Ex. 3, ¶¶63–65) instead implies a ~$495,000 shortfall on the same item. Those two numbers, from the same filings, are off by about $440,000.

Do we have (or can we get) any Bloomfield report or arbitration outcome on this? Without it, we don't think Whalen has any documentary basis for a number beyond what's already been paid.

**4. Gulfstream Annual Net Profit — the one clean, fixed figure.**
MSA Art. 4.1(a)(3)(ii) fixes this at $120,000, payable in $30,000 quarterly installments starting 6/1/2020. We don't have anything in our records showing whether this was paid. Can you confirm status?

**5. Dischargeability question — want to make sure we're aligned.**
Brad mentioned you'd said only child support is nondischargeable in Chapter 7. That doesn't match what we're seeing in the statute and case law — §101(14A) defines "domestic support obligation" to include spousal support by name, and §523(a)(5) makes any DSO nondischargeable with no ability-to-pay exception; separately, §523(a)(15) makes property-settlement debts to a former spouse nondischargeable in Chapter 7 too post-BAPCPA (the old ability-to-pay defenses under (a)(15) were deleted by BAPCPA in 2005). Could you clarify what you meant — were you speaking to Chapter 13 specifically (where §1328(a)(2) does discharge (a)(15) debts but not (a)(5)), or something else? Want to make sure we're not working from different premises before this goes further.
