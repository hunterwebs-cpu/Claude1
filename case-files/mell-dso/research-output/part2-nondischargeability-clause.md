## PART I — BOTTOM LINE

**One-sentence rule statement (for the brief):**

> A prepetition contractual promise that a debt "shall not be dischargeable in bankruptcy" is void as against public policy and is a legal nullity in a Chapter 7 case, because the Bankruptcy Code permits a debtor to waive discharge only by a writing "executed by the debtor **after** the order for relief" and "approve[d]" by the court, 11 U.S.C. § 727(a)(10), and permits waiver of the dischargeability of a *particular* debt only through a reaffirmation agreement satisfying 11 U.S.C. § 524(c).

**Strongest authority available in this forum:** the statute itself (§ 727(a)(10) and § 524(c)), applied by the anticipated forum in **Marra, Gerstein & Richman v. Kroen (In re Kroen), 280 B.R. 347, 352 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002)** (Stern, J.) — "such waivers are void, offending the policy of promoting a fresh start for individual debtors."

**Honest limit:** There is **no** binding Third Circuit or Supreme Court holding squarely on prepetition waiver of discharge. The rule is statutory plus a very deep and uniform body of persuasive authority. The clause is not, however, entirely inert: it may be admissible as *some evidence* of the parties' intent under the § 523(a)(5)/§ 101(14A) "in the nature of support" inquiry governed by **In re Gianakas, 917 F.2d 759 (3d Cir. 1990)** (BINDING), and a *fact-laden* consent judgment (as opposed to a bare nondischargeability recital) can support issue preclusion.

## PART II — THE CORE DOCTRINE: PREPETITION WAIVER OF DISCHARGE IS VOID

### A. The general rule

**Klingman v. Levinson, 831 F.2d 1292 (7th Cir. 1987)** — PERSUASIVE (7th Cir.). This is the most-cited sentence in the field. It appears in **footnote 3**, at 1296 n.3:

> "Generally, all debts are dischargeable in bankruptcy unless specifically excepted by a provision in the Bankruptcy Code. See, e.g., In re Cross, 666 F.2d 873, 879 (5th Cir. Unit B 1982). **For public policy reasons, a debtor may not contract away the right to a discharge in bankruptcy. However, a debtor may stipulate to the underlying facts that the bankruptcy court must examine to determine whether a debt is dischargeable.**"

*Klingman*, 831 F.2d at 1296 n.3 (emphasis added).

**READ CAREFULLY — WHY *KLINGMAN* IS CITED BOTH WAYS.** *Klingman* **affirmed** a judgment of nondischargeability arising out of a state-court consent decree. Opposing counsel will lead with that. But the Seventh Circuit expressly did **not** rest on the parties' nondischargeability recital. Two features of the *Klingman* consent decree, absent from a bare MSA clause, drove the result:

1. **A stipulation to operative facts.** The decree recited "[t]hat malice is the gist of this action, and that the Defendant has stipulated that it is his intention that the obligation to Plaintiff … created by this Agreed Judgment Order not be dischargeable in any bankruptcy or similar proceeding, **and that in any subsequent proceeding all of the allegations of the Complaint and findings of this Court may be taken as true and correct without further proof**." 831 F.2d at 1294 (quoting Agreed Judgment Order) `[VERIFY page — text confirmed verbatim; pinpoint page for the quoted decree not independently confirmed]`. Separately, the decree found that Levinson "through his misappropriation and defalcation, allowed or caused the dissipation and loss of the said trust corpus and income therefrom" — the operative § 523(a)(4) facts.

2. **A manifested intent to be bound in a later proceeding.** The court's reasoning was preclusion, not contract:

> "In the consent decree entered into in this case, the parties specifically provided that the debt owed to Ms. Klingman would 'not be dischargeable in any bankruptcy or similar proceeding and that in any subsequent proceeding all of the allegations of the Complaint and findings of this Court may be taken as true and correct without further proof.' … **In this situation, it is certainly reasonable to conclude that the parties understood the conclusive effect of their stipulation in a future bankruptcy proceeding. Consequently, the consent judgment should be given collateral estoppel effect.**"

*Klingman*, 831 F.2d at 1296.

The Ninth Circuit BAP's characterization of *Klingman* is the one to quote back:

> "Because the stipulated facts in the judgment established all of the elements of the § 523(a)(4) cause of action, the court held that collateral estoppel applied. **The court did not rely on the purported waiver of discharge for its affirmance.**"

*Hayhoe v. Cole (In re Cole)*, 226 B.R. 647, 652 (B.A.P. 9th Cir. 1998) (citing *Klingman*, 831 F.2d at 1295-96, 1296 n.3) `[VERIFY pinpoint 652 — text confirmed verbatim from full opinion; exact reporter page not independently confirmed]`.

### B. The only circuit-level holding: *In re Huang*

**Bank of China v. Huang (In re Huang), 275 F.3d 1173 (9th Cir. 2002)** — PERSUASIVE (9th Cir.). This is the closest thing to appellate law on the point, and it is directly analogous: a settlement agreement recited that the debtor was "familiar with the law concerning the dischargeability of debts in bankruptcy" and that "the Judgment and the Debt … are not dischargeable in any bankruptcy," plus a covenant not to file at all. The Ninth Circuit:

> "**First. It is against public policy for a debtor to waive the prepetition protection of the Bankruptcy Code.** Hayhoe v. Cole, 226 B.R. 647, 651-54 (B.A.P. 1998). **This prohibition of prepetition waiver has to be the law; otherwise, astute creditors would routinely require their debtors to waive.** Accordingly, the district court held that Settlement Agreement's provisions that the judgment and debt are not dischargeable, and that Huang will not enter bankruptcy, are unenforceable."

*Huang*, 275 F.3d at 1177.

**Note the procedural posture honestly.** The Ninth Circuit added: "The Bank did not appeal the decision of the district court on this point. It is the law of the case." *Id.* Opposing counsel may argue the passage is therefore not a holding. The better answer is that it is the Ninth Circuit's express statement of the governing rule, adopted without qualification, and that the *result* in *Huang* — affirmance of dischargeability — is squarely on point. See Part V.C below on the preclusion half of *Huang*, which is the more valuable half.

### C. The leading reasoned decision: *In re Cole*

**Hayhoe v. Cole (In re Cole), 226 B.R. 647 (B.A.P. 9th Cir. 1998)** — PERSUASIVE (9th Cir. BAP). *Cole* is the most thorough treatment and is the case the Bankr. D.N.J. court relied on in *Kroen*. The stipulated judgment there provided "that the Debt was nondischargeable pursuant to § 523(a)(2)(B)" and that "[i]f, for any reason, [Appellee] attempts to discharge this debt through bankruptcy, [Appellee] hereby acknowledges, covenants and agrees that [Appellee] obtained a release of [Appellant's] security for this debt under false pretenses." The panel held:

> "**A. The Bankruptcy Court Did Not Err In Concluding That Appellee's Prepetition Waiver Of Discharge Was Void As Against Public Policy.**"

*Cole*, 226 B.R. at 651 (section heading) `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

And, on the exhaustiveness of the authority:

> "**Appellant has not cited a single case that recognizes the validity of prepetition waivers of discharge resulting from state court litigation.**"

*Id.* at 653 `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

*Cole* collects the trial-court authority, including several decisions from within the Third Circuit and one squarely in the marital context:

- *Giaimo v. Detrano (In re Detrano)*, 222 B.R. 685, 688 (Bankr. E.D.N.Y. 1998) — "As a matter of superseding federal bankruptcy policy …, a prepetition waiver of a discharge of a particular debt or of all debts is against public policy and unenforceable."
- *Chilcoat v. Minor (In re Minor)*, 115 B.R. 690, 694-96 (D. Colo. 1990) — holding a prepetition waiver of discharge of an individual debt invalid unless it complies with § 524(c).
- *Doug Howle's Paces Ferry Dodge, Inc. v. Ethridge (In re Ethridge)*, 80 B.R. 581, 586 (Bankr. M.D. Ga. 1987) — "the provisions of the consent judgment which pertain to the waiver of Defendant's right to a discharge are void."
- *First Ga. Bank v. Halpern (In re Halpern)*, 50 B.R. 260, 262 (Bankr. N.D. Ga. 1985) — "**Policy considerations dictate that dischargeability questions cannot be predetermined either by a state court or by agreement of the parties prior to or in anticipation of the possible filing of a bankruptcy case.**" *aff'd*, 810 F.2d 1061 (11th Cir. 1987).
- ***Bisbach v. Bisbach (In re Bisbach)*, 36 B.R. 350, 352 (Bankr. W.D. Wis. 1984)** — **directly on point for a divorce**: holding "that a prepetition waiver in a divorce agreement that described the debt as maintenance or support, and thus, nondischargeable, was unenforceable" (*Cole*'s parenthetical).
- *Johnson v. Kriger (In re Kriger)*, 2 B.R. 19, 23 (Bankr. D. Or. 1979) — "It is a well settled principle that an advance agreement to waive the benefit of a discharge in bankruptcy is wholly void, as against public policy."
- *In re Weitzen*, 3 F. Supp. 698, 698 (S.D.N.Y. 1933) — "The agreement to waive the benefit of bankruptcy is unenforceable. To sustain a contractual obligation of this character would frustrate the object of the Bankruptcy Act."
- *In re Madison*, 184 B.R. 686, 690 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 1995) — prepetition waiver of the right to file unenforceable as violating public policy. **(Third Circuit district.)**

All quotations in this bullet list are reproduced as they appear in *Cole*, 226 B.R. at 652-53 & nn.6-7 `[VERIFY reporter pinpoints for the string cite]`.

### D. Supreme Court "fresh start" policy

**Local Loan Co. v. Hunt, 292 U.S. 234 (1934)** — BINDING (SCOTUS). Verified against the official U.S. Reports scan (Library of Congress). The passage sits on page 244:

> "One of the primary purposes of the bankruptcy act is to 'relieve the honest debtor from the weight of oppressive indebtedness and permit him to start afresh free from the obligations and responsibilities consequent upon business misfortunes.' Williams v. U.S. Fidelity & G. Co., 236 U.S. 549, 554-555. This purpose of the act has been again and again emphasized by the courts as being of public as well as private interest, in that **it gives to the honest but unfortunate debtor who surrenders for distribution the property which he owns at the time of bankruptcy, a new opportunity in life and a clear field for future effort, unhampered by the pressure and discouragement of preexisting debt.**"

*Local Loan*, 292 U.S. at 244.

And on page 245, the sentence that does the real work against a state-law contract defense:

> "The various provisions of the bankruptcy act were adopted in the light of that view and are to be construed when reasonably possible in harmony with it so as to effectuate the general purpose and policy of the act. **Local rules subversive of that result cannot be accepted as controlling the action of a federal court.**"

*Local Loan*, 292 U.S. at 245.

Also useful, at 244, on the federal character of the question:

> "It is important to bear in mind that the present case is one not within the jurisdiction of a state court, but is a dependent suit brought to vindicate decrees of a federal court of bankruptcy entered in the exercise of a jurisdiction **essentially federal and exclusive in character**."

*Id.* at 244.

**Candid caveat:** *Local Loan* is about a prepetition wage assignment, not a waiver-of-discharge clause. It does **not** hold that discharge is non-waivable. It supplies the policy premise (and the anti-state-law-override principle) on which the lower courts have built the rule. Do not overstate it in a brief.

## PART III — THE STATUTORY BASIS FOR NON-WAIVABILITY

This is the strongest part of the argument, because it does not depend on persuasive case law at all. Congress specified — exhaustively — the two ways a debtor may give up discharge protection, and a prepetition MSA clause satisfies neither.

### A. 11 U.S.C. § 727(a)(10) — global waiver, POSTPETITION and COURT-APPROVED only

Verbatim (uscode.house.gov, current through the prelim edition):

> "**(a) The court shall grant the debtor a discharge, unless—** …
>
> **(10) the court approves a written waiver of discharge executed by the debtor after the order for relief under this chapter;**"

11 U.S.C. § 727(a)(10).

**Three independent conditions, each of which a prepetition MSA clause fails:**

| § 727(a)(10) requires | A prepetition MSA clause |
|---|---|
| a **written waiver of discharge** | is typically a nondischargeability *recital*, not a waiver of discharge at all |
| **executed after the order for relief** | is executed months or years *before* the petition |
| **approved by the court** (i.e., the bankruptcy court) | was never presented to, let alone approved by, a bankruptcy court |

The expressio unius inference is the point. As *Cole* put it:

> "In addition, Congress has only provided two methods for a debtor to waive the discharge of all debts or the dischargeability of specific debts. Section 727(a)(10) permits a debtor to waive the discharge of all debts simply by executing a postbankruptcy written agreement that is approved by the bankruptcy court. … Similarly, a debtor may waive the dischargeability of a specific debt if the waiver satisfies the reaffirmation requirements of § 524(c). … **Here, Congress' failure to authorize prepetition waivers of discharge, while at the same time authorizing certain postpetition waivers of discharge pursuant to §§ 524(c) and 727(a)(1) [sic — 727(a)(10)], must be viewed as intentional.**"

*Cole*, 226 B.R. at 654 (citing *Hohn v. United States*, 524 U.S. 236 (1998), and *Russello v. United States*, 464 U.S. 16, 23 (1983)) `[VERIFY pinpoint 654]`.

**Critical structural point — § 727(a)(10) cannot be repurposed for a single debt.** Quoting Collier as adopted by the Bankr. D.N.J. court in *Kroen*:

> "A distinction must be drawn between a waiver of discharge under section 727(a)(10) and a reaffirmation or exception of a specific debt from discharge. The waiver under section 727(a)(10) is a waiver of any discharge at all. Thus, none of the debtor's debts are discharged. … To waive the discharge of an individual debt, the debtor must reaffirm the debt under section 524. The reaffirmation is subject to specific statutory procedures, standards and safeguards … . **A debtor and creditor should not be permitted to use section 727(a)(10) to avoid the reaffirmation provisions.**"

4 Lawrence King, *Collier on Bankruptcy* ¶ 727.12, at 727-54 (15th ed. rev. 1998), quoted in *In re Kroen*, 280 B.R. 347 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002) `[VERIFY reporter pinpoint within Kroen for this block quote]`.

### B. 11 U.S.C. § 524(c) — reaffirmation, the ONLY route for a single debt

Verbatim (Cornell LII, 11 U.S.C. § 524(c)):

> "**(c)** An agreement between a holder of a claim and the debtor, the consideration for which, in whole or in part, is based on a debt that is dischargeable in a case under this title is enforceable only to any extent enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law, **whether or not discharge of such debt is waived**, only if—
>
> **(1)** such agreement was made before the granting of the discharge under section 727, 1141, 1192, 1228, or 1328 of this title;
>
> **(2)** the debtor received the disclosures described in subsection (k) at or before the time at which the debtor signed the agreement;
>
> **(3)** such agreement has been filed with the court and, if applicable, accompanied by a declaration or an affidavit of the attorney that represented the debtor during the course of negotiating an agreement under this subsection, which states that—
>
>  **(A)** such agreement represents a fully informed and voluntary agreement by the debtor;
>  **(B)** such agreement does not impose an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor; and
>  **(C)** the attorney fully advised the debtor of the legal effect and consequences of—
>   **(i)** an agreement of the kind specified in this subsection; and
>   **(ii)** any default under such an agreement;
>
> **(4)** the debtor has not rescinded such agreement at any time prior to discharge or within sixty days after such agreement is filed with the court, whichever occurs later, by giving notice of rescission to the holder of such claim;
>
> **(5)** the provisions of subsection (d) of this section have been complied with; and
>
> **(6) (A)** in a case concerning an individual who was not represented by an attorney during the course of negotiating an agreement under this subsection, the court approves such agreement as—
>   **(i)** not imposing an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor; and
>   **(ii)** in the best interest of the debtor.
>  **(B)** Subparagraph (A) shall not apply to the extent that such debt is a consumer debt secured by real property."

11 U.S.C. § 524(c).

A prepetition MSA clause fails at least (2), (3), (4) and (5), and typically (1) as a matter of chronology and intent.

**The Bankr. D.N.J. court has said exactly this.** *In re Kroen*:

> "Section 524(a) of the Bankruptcy Code is replete with language that a bankruptcy discharge is effective '**whether or not discharge of such debt is waived**.' The appropriate mechanism for saving a debt from discharge is to comply with the post-petition reaffirmation procedure dictated by the Code in § 524. **Case law makes clear that the reaffirmation procedure is the only mechanism by which a debtor may 'waive' discharge of a specific debt**, and that the strictness and narrowness of the reaffirmation mechanism reflect 'the policy of affording the debtor a broad discharge and an effective fresh start.' In re DiPierro, 69 B.R. at 282."

*In re Kroen*, 280 B.R. 347 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002) `[VERIFY reporter pinpoint — text confirmed verbatim]`.

### C. 11 U.S.C. § 524(a) — the "whether or not … waived" hook

> "**(a)** A discharge in a case under this title—
>
> **(1)** voids any judgment at any time obtained, to the extent that such judgment is a determination of the personal liability of the debtor with respect to any debt discharged under section 727, 944, 1141, 1228, or 1328 of this title, **whether or not discharge of such debt is waived**; …"

11 U.S.C. § 524(a)(1).

*Kroen* quotes the legislative history that makes the point explicit:

> "The language 'whether or not discharge of such debt is waived' is intended to prevent waiver of discharge of a **particular** debt, not waiver of discharge in toto as permitted under section 727(a)(9) [sic, 727(a)(10)]."

H.R. Rep. No. 95-595 / S. Rep. No. 95-989, quoted in *In re Kroen*, 280 B.R. 347 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002) `[VERIFY the specific report and page cited by Kroen]`.

This is the cleanest textual answer to a creditor who says "the parties *agreed*." Congress wrote a provision that operates *even where the debtor purported to waive*.

### D. 11 U.S.C. § 523(a) — the exception categories are fixed by Congress

Verbatim introductory language:

> "A discharge under section 727, 1141, 1192, 1228(a), 1228(b), or 1328(b) of this title **does not discharge an individual debtor from any debt—**"

11 U.S.C. § 523(a). What follows is a closed, enumerated list. There is no paragraph excepting "any debt the parties have agreed is nondischargeable."

**Exceptions are construed narrowly in the debtor's favor — BINDING Third Circuit language:**

> "The overriding purpose of the Bankruptcy Code is to relieve debtors from the weight of oppressive indebtedness and provide them with a fresh start. **Exceptions to discharge are strictly construed against creditors and liberally construed in favor of debtors.**"

*In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108, 1113 (3d Cir. 1995) — **BINDING**. (Pinpoint 1113 confirmed by the Bankr. D.N.J. court's own citation in *Kroen*.)

**And BINDING SCOTUS language, verified against the official U.S. Reports scan:**

> "A construction so broad would be incompatible with the '**well-known**' guide that **exceptions to discharge 'should be confined to those plainly expressed.**' Gleason v. Thaw, 236 U.S. 558, 562 (1915)."

*Kawaauhau v. Geiger*, 523 U.S. 57, 62 (1998) — **BINDING**.

A private contract is the antithesis of an exception "plainly expressed" by Congress.

### E. Dischargeability is a federal question; the bankruptcy court has exclusive jurisdiction over § 523(a)(2), (4), (6)

> "Except as provided in subsection (a)(3)(B) of this section, the debtor **shall be discharged** from a debt of a kind specified in paragraph (2), (4), or (6) of subsection (a) of this section, **unless, on request of the creditor to whom such debt is owed, and after notice and a hearing, the court determines** such debt to be excepted from discharge …"

11 U.S.C. § 523(c)(1).

*Cole* draws the structural conclusion:

> "**Thus, the bankruptcy court must make a determination regarding the dischargeability of a § 523(a)(2) claim notwithstanding a state court stipulated judgment or prepetition agreement that purports to determine the dischargeability of a debt.**"

*Cole*, 226 B.R. at 653 `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

For a **divorce** obligation, the Third Circuit has said the same in binding terms:

> "Moreover, although the decree or settlement establishing the obligation almost invariably arises in the context of a state court proceeding, **whether the obligation is in the nature of alimony, maintenance or support for the purposes of the Bankruptcy Code is a question of federal, not state, law.**"

*In re Gianakas*, 917 F.2d 759, 762 (3d Cir. 1990) — **BINDING**.

And a state court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate dischargeability before a case is filed:

> "The District Court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to enter judgment on the issue of nondischargeability where no bankruptcy case had been filed."

*Cheripka v. Republic Ins. Co. (In re Cheripka)*, 122 B.R. 33, 36 (Bankr. W.D. Pa. 1990) (parenthetical as rendered in the Bankr. N.D. Ga. decision discussed at Part V.A) — PERSUASIVE (Bankr. W.D. Pa., Third Circuit district) `[VERIFY — quoted secondhand, not opened in full text]`.

### F. § 541 / general federal-supremacy framing — a candid note

The prompt asks about § 541. **I found no authority holding that § 541 itself invalidates a prepetition nondischargeability clause, and I will not manufacture one.** § 541 defines property of the estate; it is not the operative provision here. The correct supremacy framing is (i) *Local Loan*, 292 U.S. at 245 ("Local rules subversive of that result cannot be accepted as controlling the action of a federal court"); (ii) *Gianakas*, 917 F.2d at 762 (federal, not state, law governs the § 523(a)(5) characterization); and (iii) § 523(c)(1) plus *Cole* (exclusive federal adjudication of dischargeability). Use those, not § 541.

## PART IV — THIRD CIRCUIT AND BANKR. D.N.J. AUTHORITY

This was the highest-value search target. Findings, stated candidly:

### A. There is NO binding Third Circuit holding on prepetition waiver of discharge

Extensive searching (CourtListener full-text search across ca3 and njb; law.resource.org F.2d/F.3d; casemine) located **no** published Third Circuit decision holding that a prepetition contractual nondischargeability clause is (or is not) enforceable. **Say so in the brief rather than implying otherwise.** The Third Circuit authority that *is* binding goes to the adjacent propositions: narrow construction of § 523(a) exceptions (*Cohn*), the federal-law character of the § 523(a)(5) inquiry and the duty to look past labels (*Gianakas*), and issue preclusion in dischargeability litigation (*Docteroff*).

### B. Bankr. D.N.J. HAS decided the question — and squarely

***Marra, Gerstein & Richman v. Kroen (In re Kroen)*, 280 B.R. 347 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002)** (Stern, J.) — **PERSUASIVE, but from the anticipated forum, which makes it the single most useful non-binding citation available.**

Facts: A matrimonial attorney sued his former client under § 523(a)(2)(A), alleging that the debtor had orally promised the attorney's fees would not be discharged and that the attorney relied on that promise. The court held the alleged promise was worthless as a matter of law, so reliance on it could not be justifiable.

The holding sentence, with the pinpoint confirmed:

> "The fundamental point here is that **such waivers are void, offending the policy of promoting a fresh start for individual debtors.**"

*In re Kroen*, 280 B.R. 347, **352** (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002).

The court then walked through the authority:

> "Consider, in particular, In re DiPierro, 69 B.R. 279 (Bankr. W.D. Pa. 1987) ('[a] debtor cannot contract away the right to a bankruptcy discharge in advance of the bankruptcy filing'). See the often quoted dicta in Klingman v. Levinson, 831 F.2d 1292, 1296 n. 3 (7th Cir. 1987) ('[f]or public policy reasons, a debtor may not contract away the right to a discharge in bankruptcy')."

*Id.* `[VERIFY reporter pinpoint — text confirmed verbatim]`. **(Note: the published *Kroen* opinion contains a typographical error, citing *Klingman* as "831 F.3d 1292." The correct reporter is 831 F.2d 1292. Do not replicate the error.)**

And, importantly for an MSA-clause fact pattern, the court closed the "backdoor":

> "And, it would be no small rupture in the policy voiding waivers if an attorney were permitted to assert the waiver as a misrepresentation qualifying the fee-based debt for a § 523(a)(2)(A) exception to discharge. **The backdoor to circumventing fresh start policy through 'pre-printed' retainer agreement discharge waivers, would be wide open.**"

*Id.* `[VERIFY pinpoint]`. This is the answer to a creditor who repackages the broken promise as fraud: the promise is void, so reliance on it cannot be justifiable, and § 523(a)(2)(A) fails.

Finally, *Kroen* supplies the Third Circuit narrow-construction quote with its pinpoint:

> "'The overriding purpose of the Bankruptcy Code is to relieve debtors from the weight of oppressive indebtedness and provide them with a fresh start.' In re Cohn, 54 F.3d 1108, 1113 (3d Cir. 1995). For this reason, exceptions to discharge are strictly construed against creditors and liberally interpreted in favor of debtors."

*Id.* `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

### C. Other authority within the Third Circuit

- ***Levy v. DiPierro (In re DiPierro)*, 69 B.R. 279, 282 (Bankr. W.D. Pa. 1987)** — PERSUASIVE (Bankr. W.D. Pa.). "[A] debtor cannot contract away the right to a bankruptcy discharge in advance of the bankruptcy filing"; the reaffirmation mechanism's "strictness and narrowness … reflect the policy of affording the debtor a broad discharge and an effective fresh start." (Quoted through *Kroen*.) `[VERIFY — not opened in full text]`
- ***In re Madison*, 184 B.R. 686, 690 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 1995)** — PERSUASIVE. Prepetition waiver of the right to file unenforceable as against public policy. `[VERIFY — quoted through Cole]`

- ***Saler v. Saler (In re Saler)*, 205 B.R. 737 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 1997), aff'd, 217 B.R. 166 (E.D. Pa. 1998)** — PERSUASIVE, and **adverse**. See Part VI.

## PART V — THE IMPORTANT NUANCE: WHERE THE CLAUSE *DOES* HAVE EFFECT

Be rigorous here. Overclaiming will cost credibility, because the honest answer is that the clause is not entirely inert.

### A. The label is EVIDENCE of intent; it is not BINDING

This is the distinction that matters in an MSA case, and the Third Circuit's binding rule cuts in the debtor's favor on the "binding" half while leaving the "evidence" half open.

**BINDING — *In re Gianakas*, 917 F.2d 759 (3d Cir. 1990):**

> "The statute explicitly provides that a debt 'to a spouse, former spouse, or child of the debtor' is not exempted from discharge merely because it is 'designated as alimony, maintenance or support, unless such liability is actually in the nature of alimony, maintenance, or support.' 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(5)(B). **Thus, the court must look beyond the label attached to an obligation by a settlement agreement to examine its true nature.**"

*Gianakas*, 917 F.2d at 762.

> "We believe that whether an obligation is in the nature of alimony, maintenance or support, as distinguished from a property settlement, **depends on a finding as to the intent of the parties at the time of the settlement agreement**. … **That intent can best be found by examining three principal indicators.** The many other factors referred to by various courts are merely elements of these indicators."

*Id.* at 762.

The three indicators, verbatim:

> **First:** "First, the court must examine the language and substance of the agreement in the context of surrounding circumstances, using extrinsic evidence if necessary. … However, it is likely that 'neither the parties nor the divorce court contemplated the effect of a subsequent bankruptcy when the obligation arose.' … Therefore, the parties and the state courts may not have focused on whether a particular obligation was to serve as support or as a property settlement unrelated to support."
>
> **Second:** "Because the language of the agreement alone may not provide a sufficiently conclusive answer as to the nature of an obligation, the second indicator to which we must look to assist in ascertaining the parties' intent is the parties' financial circumstances at the time of the settlement."
>
> **Third:** "Third, the court should examine the function served by the obligation at the time of the divorce or settlement. An obligation that serves to maintain daily necessities such as food, housing and transportation is indicative of a debt intended to be in the nature of support."

*Gianakas*, 917 F.2d at 762-63.

**The "evidence, not binding" distinction, applied to a nondischargeability clause in a divorce decree.** The best decision found on this precise point is a 2017 opinion of the Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Georgia (Drake, J.), Adv. Proc. No. 16-01028-whd (Bankr. N.D. Ga. Mar. 8, 2017) — PERSUASIVE. The decree provided: "The property division payments to the wife shall not be dischargeable in bankruptcy should the husband file for bankruptcy." The court held:

> "Provisions of this type, seeking to 'bankruptcy proof' contracts and agreements, are not enforceable as against public policy. See Hester v. Daniel (In re Daniel), 290 B.R. 914, 919-20 (Bankr. M.D. Ga. 2003) …; Freeman v. Freeman (In re Freeman), 165 B.R. 307, 312 (Bankr. S.D. Fla. 1994) ('Provisions in a property settlement agreement that obligations thereunder are non-dischargeable in bankruptcy are not specifically enforceable and in themselves are not binding. An agreement to waive the benefit of a discharge in bankruptcy is void, as against public policy.') … . **The fact that the waiver is included in an order of the Superior Court does not change this analysis.** As the Debtor did not file his petition until after the decree was entered, the Superior Court had no jurisdiction to decide the issue of dischargeability."

And then, the honest concession:

> "**Thus, the nondischargeability provision of the final decree does not control this Court's decision. However, the waiver may be some evidence of whether the parties intended the [obligation] to be a domestic support obligation.** See generally Reynolds v. Reynolds (In re Reynolds), 546 B.R. 232, 234 n.7 (Bankr. M.D. Fla. 2016) (noting that 'labels in a settlement agreement are perhaps instructive, but they are not determinations of whether an obligation is a domestic support obligation.')."

And, decisively, how the court then *weighed* that evidence:

> "As mentioned above, the nondischargeability provision in the decree is not effective to render the debt nondischargeable, but it could be some evidence of the parties' intent in making their agreement. **However, this provision was not inserted to represent an agreement that the debt is a domestic support obligation. Rather, the Plaintiff appears to have been afraid that the Debtor would immediately file for bankruptcy after the divorce, and the parties inserted this provision as an attempt to assuage those fears. Thus, the Court finds that the nondischargeability provision was simply an attempt to bar the Debtor from receiving a discharge, and does not represent any intent by the parties to treat the debt as support.**"

Bankr. N.D. Ga., Adv. No. 16-01028-whd, slip op. (Mar. 8, 2017) `[VERIFY — full text obtained from govinfo (USCOURTS-ganb-3_16-ap-01028); the reported case NAME and any B.R. citation could not be confirmed. Do not cite by name until verified.]`

**This is the template for the fallback argument.** See Part VII.B.

### B. Stipulated and consent judgments CAN support issue preclusion — but only on facts

**BINDING Third Circuit statement of the elements — *In re Docteroff*, 133 F.3d 210 (3d Cir. 1997):**

> "Collateral estoppel prohibits the relitigation of issues that have been adjudicated in a prior lawsuit. The principles of collateral estoppel apply in discharge proceedings in bankruptcy court. … For a party to be estopped from relitigating an issue, the following elements must be present: **(1) the issue sought to be precluded must be the same as the one involved in the prior action; (2) the issue must have been actually litigated; (3) the issue must have been determined by a valid and final judgment; and (4) the determination must have been essential to the prior judgment.**"

*Docteroff*, 133 F.3d at 214 (citing *In re Ross*, 602 F.2d 604, 608 (3d Cir. 1979), and Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 27 (1982)).

*Docteroff* also confirms the correct scope of preclusion — facts, not the ultimate dischargeability conclusion:

> "That is not controlling, however, because the plaintiffs only seek to estop Docteroff from asserting certain facts … . **Collateral estoppel is applicable if the facts established by the previous judgment … meet the requirements of nondischargeability listed in 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A), (a)(4), and (a)(6)** … . See In re Halpern, 810 F.2d 1061, 1064 (11th Cir. 1987) (bankruptcy court makes ultimate determination regarding debt's dischargeability; **collateral estoppel permits court to accept facts established by previous judgment as evidence of nondischargeability**)."

*Id.* at 214-15 `[VERIFY pinpoint 214-15]`.

**IMPORTANT — WHICH PRECLUSION LAW APPLIES.** *Docteroff* applied **federal** preclusion law because "the prior judgment was rendered by a federal court." 133 F.3d at 214. A New Jersey MSA incorporated into a Superior Court dual judgment of divorce is a **state** judgment, so 28 U.S.C. § 1738 requires the bankruptcy court to apply **New Jersey** preclusion law. This is the single most important framing point in this section, and the anticipated forum has said so:

> "In a nondischargeability proceeding under section 523(a), a bankruptcy court must, pursuant to the full faith and credit principles of 28 U.S.C. § 1738, **give the same issue preclusion effect to a state court judgment as the judgment would be given under that state's law.** 28 U.S.C. § 1738; Marrese v. American Acad. of Orthopaedic Surgeons, 470 U.S. 373, 380 (1985)."

*In re Azeglio*, 422 B.R. 490 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2010) (Wizmur, J.) `[VERIFY reporter pinpoint — text confirmed verbatim]`.

**New Jersey preclusion law, as canvassed by the anticipated forum in *Azeglio*:**

> "As their federal counterparts do, New Jersey courts also agree that **collateral estoppel may not be applied in the case of a default judgment.** See Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 27 comment e. See, e.g., Slowinski v. Valley National Bank, 264 N.J. Super. 172, 183 (App. Div. 1993). See also In re Hawkins, 231 B.R. 222, 231 (D.N.J. 1999) ('Pursuant to New Jersey law, collateral estoppel does not apply to default judgments because such judgments are not "actually litigated."')."

*Azeglio*, 422 B.R. 490 `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

> "The canvassing of New Jersey case law governing collateral estoppel reveals that **the requirement that a matter be 'actually litigated' means just that: the party against whom the doctrine is sought to be applied must have had a full and fair opportunity to litigate the issue to be precluded, so that a judgment on the merits, reached after an adversarial hearing, is entered.**"

*Id.* `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

Note *Azeglio*'s outcome: preclusion was **denied** even though the debtor "participated actively in pretrial proceedings" — filing responsive pleadings and sitting for depositions — because he did not participate at trial. That is a demanding standard, and an uncontested MSA falls far below it. *Azeglio* also cites *Gallo v. Tooley*, No. 06-02523, 2007 WL 1071945 (Bankr. D.N.J. Apr. 3, 2007) (Steckroth, J.), where preclusion was denied even after a contested proof hearing `[VERIFY]`.

### C. Can a BARE RECITAL — no factual findings — ever support preclusion?

**Researched answer: No. Two courts have addressed this squarely and both said no.**

**(1) *In re Cole* (B.A.P. 9th Cir.) — a stipulation that a *future* filing will be *deemed* fraud is a nullity:**

> "We have already concluded that the portion of the Stipulated Judgment that purported to waive Appellee's right to obtain a discharge of the Debt was unenforceable as against public policy. **However, if the parties stipulated to the underlying facts that support a finding of nondischargeability, the Stipulated Judgment would then be entitled to collateral estoppel application.** See Levinson, 831 F.2d at 1296 n.3."

> "Here, the Complaint was based entirely on the facts asserted in the Stipulated Judgment. It stated that the occurrence of a future act … would be deemed an admission that the release … was obtained under false pretenses … . **This stipulated fact was just another attempt by Appellant to have Appellee prospectively waive his right to discharge the Debt in bankruptcy, and thus, was unenforceable.**"

> "**The stipulated facts relate to a possible future cause of action in a possible future bankruptcy case.** Thus, the stipulated facts have nothing to do with the merits of Appellant's state court lawsuit … . **Therefore, the stipulated facts could not have been necessary to the Judgment. In addition, since not relevant to it, the stipulated facts could not have been actually litigated.** … **Thus, the Stipulated Judgment as to deemed fraud had absolutely no evidentiary effect in the § 523(a)(2) adversary proceeding.**"

*Cole*, 226 B.R. at 656-58 `[VERIFY pinpoints — text confirmed verbatim from the full opinion]`.

**(2) *In re Huang* (9th Cir.) — silence on the operative facts is fatal, and drafting sophistication does not cure it:**

> "**Fraud, or facts showing fraud, are not mentioned in the Settlement Agreement or in the judgment enforcing it.** … The Bank argues that Huang's potential liability for fraud was part of the two months of negotiations that preceded settlement. That argument, in the end, cuts against the Bank. … **But, with apparent deliberateness, neither fraud, nor facts supporting the charge of fraud, are recited in the Settlement Agreement.**"

> "The omission of any mention of fraud is all the more striking when it is observed that the Bank was advised by experienced counsel, who must have been aware of what was required to constitute collateral estoppel. … **The Bank … was unable to secure the one provision that would have led to collateral estoppel, that is, Huang's admission of fraud.**"

> "We judge upon the basis of the Settlement Agreement as it is written, **dancing around any admission of fraud and constituting no collateral estoppel upon which the Bank can now rely.** AFFIRMED."

*Huang*, 275 F.3d at 1178-79 `[VERIFY pinpoints — text confirmed verbatim]`.

**(3) A district court within the Seventh Circuit reached the same result on an arbitration award with no findings:**

> "Consistent with established law, the bankruptcy court found the provision unenforceable as a matter of bankruptcy law. … Appellant does not challenge this holding on appeal, **nor could she**, but this does not mean that she was without any ability to protect the enforceability of the arbitration award by advance agreement, **either by entering into detailed stipulations of fact describing the dissipation of assets … or … requiring the arbitrators to set forth in detail the factual basis for the award and adoption by the state court.**"

> "**Because there were no findings of fact or conclusion[s] of law in the arbitration award, it is not possible to determine whether the elements for any claim under section 523 were present and satisfied by the arbitration award.**"

*Hebl v. Windeshausen*, 590 B.R. 871 (W.D. Wis. 2018) (quoting the bankruptcy court) — PERSUASIVE `[VERIFY reporter pinpoints — text confirmed verbatim]`.

**Synthesis — the line:** A clause is preclusive only if it (i) recites **operative facts** matching the elements of a specific § 523(a) exception, (ii) those facts were **material to the state-court judgment actually entered**, and (iii) the parties **manifested an intent** to be conclusively bound. A recital that says only "this debt is nondischargeable" satisfies none of the three: it recites a legal conclusion, not facts; the conclusion was immaterial to (indeed, outside the jurisdiction of) the state court; and no amount of drafted "intent" can cure a missing factual predicate.

## PART VI — CONTRARY AUTHORITY, STEEL-MANNED

**Is there a circuit split? No — not on the core proposition.** No court of appeals has held that a *prepetition* contractual nondischargeability clause is enforceable as a waiver. *Huang* (9th Cir.) and *Klingman* n.3 (7th Cir.) point the same direction. What exists is a body of decisions that reach *creditor-favorable results* on adjacent theories. These are what opposing counsel will actually use.

### A. *In re Halpern*, 810 F.2d 1061 (11th Cir. 1987) — the strongest creditor case

PERSUASIVE (11th Cir.). The consent judgment recited both a nondischargeability-type promise **and** detailed factual admissions. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed nondischargeability.

**The steel-man:**

> "The requirements that issues be actually litigated and necessary to the judgment in order for issue preclusion to apply **are altered somewhat in the context of consent decrees.** … '[t]he very purpose of [consent] decrees is to avoid litigation, so the requirement of actual litigation necessary to preclusion always will be missing.' **Instead, the central inquiry in determining the preclusive effect of a consent judgment is the intention of the parties as manifested in the judgment or other evidence.**"

*Halpern*, 810 F.2d at 1064 (quoting *Barber v. Int'l Bhd. of Boilermakers*, 778 F.2d 750, 757 (11th Cir. 1985)) `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

> "We agree … that here the parties intended that the consent judgment operate as a final adjudication of the factual issues contained therein. **The factual findings in the consent judgment are sufficiently detailed to leave little doubt as to their meaning.** … Any contrary construction is refuted by the fact that Halpern expressly consented in the judgment that 'these Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law will collaterally estop [Halpern] from denying any of the facts or law established herein.' … **A chilling effect on consent judgments and settlements might result if the court were to ignore such a clear and unambiguous expression of the parties' intent.**"

*Id.* `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.

**The limits — four, all strong:**

1. **The bankruptcy court in *Halpern* itself rejected the waiver theory.** *First Ga. Bank v. Halpern (In re Halpern)*, 50 B.R. 260, 262 (Bankr. N.D. Ga. 1985): "Policy considerations dictate that dischargeability questions cannot be predetermined either by a state court or by agreement of the parties prior to or in anticipation of the possible filing of a bankruptcy case." The Eleventh Circuit affirmed on **preclusion**, not waiver.
2. **The record contained detailed factual admissions**: "that Halpern made material misrepresentations of fact to First Georgia; that Halpern knew the statements were false at the time they were made; and that Halpern made the misrepresentations with the intent to induce reliance," conduct Halpern admitted was "wilful, malicious, and intentional." 810 F.2d at 1062-63 `[VERIFY pinpoint]`. A bare MSA recital has nothing comparable.
3. **The court itself drew the line at bare judgments.** Distinguishing *Balbirer v. Austin*, 790 F.2d 1524 (11th Cir. 1986): "the consent judgment in this case contains **detailed factual findings** that are directly relevant to the legal issues presented. … **In contrast, the judgment in *Balbirer* did not contain explicit factual findings nor any clause indicating that the judgment was intended to be a final adjudication of certain issues.**" 810 F.2d at 1065 `[VERIFY pinpoint]`.
4. **The "consent decrees relax actual litigation" rule is Eleventh Circuit law about a federal judgment.** In Bankr. D.N.J., § 1738 requires **New Jersey** preclusion law for a New Jersey state judgment, and New Jersey requires "a judgment on the merits, reached after an adversarial hearing." *Azeglio*, 422 B.R. 490. *Halpern*'s relaxation is therefore not available on a New Jersey MSA.

### B. *In re Saler*, 205 B.R. 737 (Bankr. E.D. Pa. 1997), aff'd, 217 B.R. 166 (E.D. Pa. 1998) — the in-circuit adverse case

PERSUASIVE (Bankr./D. E.D. Pa. — **within the Third Circuit**, which gives it more weight than its holding deserves). Held that a debtor may settle **bankruptcy nondischargeability litigation** by court-approved stipulation without complying with § 524(c).

**The steel-man:** § 524(c) by its terms applies to agreements based on "a debt that is dischargeable"; where the parties have stipulated the debt is nondischargeable, § 524(c) is not triggered.

**The limit — this is the distinction the whole area turns on, and *Cole* states it best:**

> "However, these cases are distinguishable from the situation here. **In all three cases, the settlement occurred in nondischargeability litigation in the bankruptcy court and not litigation in state court.** As stated in Saler, '**the settlement of bankruptcy discharge litigation materially differs from agreements reached in prebankruptcy state court litigation. In prebankruptcy litigation, the question of the dischargeability of the debt is not in issue.**' Saler, 205 B.R. at 745-46 … . However, dischargeability is the 'central issue in bankruptcy dischargeability litigation,' and bankruptcy courts have exclusive jurisdiction … . **Consequently, a state court stipulated judgment where the debtor waives his right to discharge is unenforceable as against public policy. However, a stipulation in a related bankruptcy case that a debt is nondischargeable is enforceable and res judicata.**"

*Cole*, 226 B.R. at 653-54 (quoting *Saler*, 205 B.R. at 745-46, 749) `[VERIFY pinpoints]`.

*Saler* is therefore **not contrary** to the position advanced here. It is the *postpetition* half of the same rule. Same for *Martinelli v. Valley Bank of Nev. (In re Martinelli)*, 96 B.R. 1011, 1014 (B.A.P. 9th Cir. 1988), and *Laing v. Johnson (In re Laing)*, 1993 WL 732230, at \*4 (N.D. Okla. Oct. 25, 1993), aff'd, 31 F.3d 1050 (10th Cir. 1994) `[VERIFY — cited through Cole, not opened]`. Accord *FDIC v. Daily (In re Daily)*, 47 F.3d 365, 369 (9th Cir. 1995) (a debtor's **postpetition** stipulation of nondischargeability is binding).

### C. *In re Wald*, 211 B.R. 359, 361 (Bankr. D.N.D. 1997)

PERSUASIVE, and weak. *Cole* cites it as the lone "But see": "stating that a showing of special circumstances is required to relieve a party from a prepetition stipulation that permitted the creditor to commence foreclosure upon default." *Cole*, 226 B.R. at 653 n.7 `[VERIFY]`. It concerns a **stay-relief** stipulation, not discharge. Distinguishable on its face.

### D. The practitioner literature agrees there is no split on the core rule

- "**An agreement providing that the debt will be non-dischargeable is not enforceable and therefore will not work.** … The settlement must be reduced to a stipulated judgment which contains detailed findings of fact that will serve as a basis for non-dischargeability and manifests an intent to be conclusively bound." Bloomberg Law, *Is a Pre-Petition Settlement Agreement Providing That a Debt Will Be Non-Dischargeable in Bankruptcy Enforceable?* (Apr. 27, 2016) `[VERIFY — read via search summary; article itself behind a paywall]`.
- "Courts generally do not favor pre-petition agreements in which a debtor waives the rights granted under the Bankruptcy Code due to public-policy considerations." Dentons, *Enforceability of Pre-Petition Agreements in Bankruptcy* `[VERIFY — PDF partially extracted]`.
- See also Marshall E. Tracht, *Contractual Bankruptcy Waivers: Reconciling Theory, Practice, and Law*, 82 Cornell L. Rev. 301, 307-08 (1997) (cited with approval in *Kroen*); Laura B. Bartell, *Waiver of Discharge: Is It Ever Really Voluntary?*, 96 `[VERIFY full cite — journal and page not confirmed]`.

## PART VII — PRACTICAL FRAMING FOR A BRIEF

### A. The primary argument, in one sentence with the best citation available

> **Rule statement:** "A debtor's prepetition contractual promise that a debt will not be discharged is void as against public policy and cannot except the debt from discharge, because Congress specified the *only* two mechanisms for waiving discharge protection — a court-approved written waiver 'executed by the debtor after the order for relief,' 11 U.S.C. § 727(a)(10), and a reaffirmation agreement complying with 11 U.S.C. § 524(c) — and a prepetition clause satisfies neither."
>
> **Best citation chain:** 11 U.S.C. §§ 727(a)(10), 524(a)(1), 524(c); *Marra, Gerstein & Richman v. Kroen (In re Kroen)*, 280 B.R. 347, 352 (Bankr. D.N.J. 2002) ("such waivers are void, offending the policy of promoting a fresh start for individual debtors"); *accord Bank of China v. Huang (In re Huang)*, 275 F.3d 1173, 1177 (9th Cir. 2002); *Klingman v. Levinson*, 831 F.2d 1292, 1296 n.3 (7th Cir. 1987); *Hayhoe v. Cole (In re Cole)*, 226 B.R. 647, 651-54 (B.A.P. 9th Cir. 1998); *see also In re Cohn*, 54 F.3d 1108, 1113 (3d Cir. 1995) (exceptions to discharge "strictly construed against creditors and liberally construed in favor of debtors"); *Kawaauhau v. Geiger*, 523 U.S. 57, 62 (1998) (exceptions "should be confined to those plainly expressed").

**Order of presentation matters.** Lead with the **statute**, not with the public-policy cases. The statutory argument is unanswerable and does not require the court to adopt a persuasive holding; the public-policy cases are corroboration. A brief that opens with "courts have held it is against public policy" invites the response "which binding court?" A brief that opens with § 727(a)(10)'s three conditions does not.

### B. The fallback: if the court gives the clause evidentiary weight

Concede the point cleanly and win on the weighing. The concession is required by *Gianakas* and costs nothing.

1. **Concede the correct proposition:** the clause "does not control this Court's decision," but "may be some evidence of whether the parties intended" the obligation to be a domestic support obligation. (Bankr. N.D. Ga. formulation, Part V.A.)
2. **Then characterize what the clause is evidence *of*.** A nondischargeability clause is evidence of an intent to **defeat a bankruptcy discharge** — not evidence of an intent to create **support**. The two are distinct, and only the latter is relevant under § 523(a)(5)/§ 101(14A). The Bankr. N.D. Ga. court made exactly this move: the provision "was simply an attempt to bar the Debtor from receiving a discharge, and does not represent any intent by the parties to treat the debt as support."
3. **Reframe under *Gianakas*.** The clause is at most one input into indicator one ("the language and substance of the agreement in the context of surrounding circumstances"). Indicators two (financial circumstances at the time) and three (function served by the obligation) are untouched by it. *Gianakas*, 917 F.2d at 762-63.
4. **Invoke the anti-boilerplate premise.** If a recital sufficed, "astute creditors would routinely require their debtors to waive," *Huang*, 275 F.3d at 1177 — and every MSA in New Jersey would contain one, rendering § 523(a)(5)'s "actually in the nature of" language and *Gianakas*'s three-indicator test dead letters. Courts do not read statutes to be nullified by boilerplate. *Cf. Geiger*, 523 U.S. at 62.
5. **Preempt the § 523(a)(2)(A) repackaging.** If the creditor argues the debtor *fraudulently promised* not to discharge, *Kroen* forecloses it: the promise was void, so reliance on it cannot be justifiable, and permitting the theory would open "[t]he backdoor to circumventing fresh start policy." *In re Kroen*, 280 B.R. 347.
6. **Preempt the preclusion argument.** Insist on § 1738 and New Jersey law (*Azeglio*), which requires "a judgment on the merits, reached after an adversarial hearing." A consensual MSA entered without a contested hearing is not "actually litigated" under New Jersey law, and *Halpern*'s Eleventh Circuit relaxation for consent decrees is not New Jersey law.

### C. The one way opposing counsel can still get real mileage

**By abandoning "waiver" and arguing "characterization."** In a *divorce* case the creditor does not need the clause to be enforceable — she needs the obligation to be "in the nature of support" under § 523(a)(5)/§ 101(14A), which is nondischargeable regardless of any clause and requires no reaffirmation. If the MSA's surrounding text ties the payments to the former spouse's living expenses, housing, or the children's needs, the nondischargeability clause becomes a supporting datum in a *Gianakas* argument the creditor would win anyway. **Anticipate this.** Also note that after BAPCPA, § 523(a)(15) makes most *non-support* divorce-related obligations nondischargeable in Chapter 7 as well — so in a Chapter 7 the § 523(a)(5)/(a)(15) line may not be outcome-determinative even if the clause is void. **This is a significant practical caveat and should be confirmed against the actual obligation before the argument is built.** `[VERIFY — § 523(a)(15) analysis not developed in this Track; see Track covering § 523(a)(5)/(15).]`

**A second, narrower avenue:** if the MSA or the judgment of divorce contains *factual* recitations (e.g., admissions of concealment of assets, fraudulent transfer, or intentional misconduct) beyond the bare nondischargeability sentence, the creditor can attempt issue preclusion under *Klingman* and *Halpern*. That argument rises or falls on whether those facts were material to the state-court judgment and whether New Jersey law treats them as "actually litigated." Under *Azeglio*, it will usually fail — but it is the only preclusion theory with any life in it.

**Negative findings stated affirmatively (no authority found — do not imply otherwise):**

1. **No binding Third Circuit or Supreme Court decision holds that a prepetition contractual waiver of discharge or nondischargeability clause is void.** The rule rests on the statute plus uniform persuasive authority.
2. **No court of appeals has held such a clause enforceable as a waiver.** There is no circuit split on the core proposition.
3. **No authority was found holding that 11 U.S.C. § 541 invalidates such a clause.** Do not make that argument.
4. **No authority was found holding that a bare nondischargeability recital, unaccompanied by factual findings, can support issue preclusion.** Every decision located that addressed the question held the opposite (*Cole*, *Huang*, *Hebl*).
