## TASK 1 — The "No Further Discovery/Full Disclosure" Stipulation(s)

The MSA contains **multiple, mutually reinforcing** provisions on this point — a recital in the preamble, a dedicated Article (Article 9, "Discovery"), a related waiver/release Article (Article 10), and general-provisions acknowledgments (Article 12) about the adequacy of each party's information and representation. All are quoted verbatim below. **Note the important qualifier inside Article 9.1 itself, flagged after the quotes — it partially cuts the other way and must be dealt with head-on in any brief.**

### A. Recital in the Preamble (page 3, Bates KM00026)

> "WHEREAS, both the Husband and the Wife have each been represented by various independent legal counsel and have made a determination after discussion with his and her respective legal counsel and financial professionals that it is unnecessary to engage in further litigation or discovery (by depositions, interrogatories, forensic analysis, valuation or otherwise), and that it is unnecessary to retain further experts to resolve all economic disputes arising from the marital relationship in this Agreement. Both parties accept this Agreement as fair, just and reasonable to their respective individual best interests, considering all of the relevant circumstances;"

Immediately following (same page, Bates KM00026), a related recital on non-transfer of assets:

> "WHEREAS, the parties warrant and represent to each other that, except for the payments noted in section 4.1 a) 3) ii) herein, during the prior three (3) years neither has transferred directly or indirectly any marital asset or earnings to a third party which would defeat one party's equitable distribution right, except to pay bills or effectuate a transaction in the ordinary course."

And the recital that elevates all of the above from mere preamble to substantive, operative text (page 3, Bates KM00026):

> "WHEREAS, the recitals to this Agreement are incorporated herein as integral parts hereof and shall be considered as substantive and not precatory language and;"

**Significance:** This last recital is important in its own right — it forecloses any argument that the "no further discovery necessary" language is throat-clearing preamble rather than a binding term of the contract.

### B. Article 9: Discovery (pages 42–43, Bates KM00065–66) — the core clause

**Section 9.1, Discovery Waiver** (page 42, Bates KM00065):

> "9.1 Discovery Waiver. Both parties knowingly and willingly waive their respective rights to take any further discovery in this matter by way of interrogatories, depositions, examinations of the books and records of the other party as well as all rights to any further independent, separate evaluation of the assets possessed or controlled by the other, and such other tools of discovery as may be available pursuant to the Court Rules. The parties represent that the property contained in this MSA represents the entire marital estate. Notwithstanding, in the event an asset of the marriage is not disclosed herein, whether intentionally or inadvertently, such asset shall be subject to equitable distribution. The parties have had the opportunity to consult with accountants, financial and/or tax advisors prior to entering into this Agreement and have waived the right to further consult with forensic accounting experts, notwithstanding for the limited scope of determining equitable distribution."

**Section 9.2** (pages 42–43, Bates KM00065–66):

> "9.2 Each party further warrants and represents that he or she has been advised of the right to hire additional experts, to take further depositions, or to use such other tools of discovery as may be available, and each party has agreed to waive the right to complete further formal discovery proceedings. Both parties further acknowledge that they have been advised of the risks inherent in such a waiver, but they deem the terms and provisions of the Agreement and the aspects thereof to each party to be fair and equitable, and that the expenses associated with the completion of formal discovery are unwarranted, and that the disclosure provided by each party to the other has been adequate to permit both parties to make informed and voluntary judgment as to the fairness of this Agreement. Each party understands that such waiver shall not be grounds for a future attempt by either party to attack the validity of, or set aside, this Agreement."

**IMPORTANT — this is the single sentence in the MSA most likely to cut against the debtor's position and must be addressed, not ignored:** Section 9.1 contains a carve-out — "*Notwithstanding, in the event an asset of the marriage is not disclosed herein, whether intentionally or inadvertently, such asset shall be subject to equitable distribution.*" On its face, this sentence preserves a route for a party (or, derivatively, Whalen on Kim's behalf) to argue that an undisclosed asset — e.g., a "going-concern" value of W.H. Mell Associates beyond the Capital Account already addressed in Article 3.5 — remains subject to distribution notwithstanding the discovery waiver. Any brief invoking Article 9.1 for finality should affirmatively address this sentence, e.g., by arguing (a) W.H. Mell's business/goodwill value *was* disclosed and addressed (Article 3.5, discussed below, expressly covers "Husband's Business Interest," represents the entity had "no assets" beyond the Capital Account, and gave Wife's own forensic accountant express subpoena/investigation rights over it), so the carve-out's factual predicate (non-disclosure) is not met; and (b) the recital and 9.2's non-merger/no-attack language independently bar collateral re-litigation of value on an already-disclosed asset even where the 9.1 carve-out might apply to something entirely un-mentioned.

### C. Article 10: Waiver of Property Rights, Releases and Bankruptcy (pages 43–45, Bates KM00066–68)

**Section 10.1** (pages 43–44, Bates KM00066):

> "10.1 Waiver of Property Rights. Except as herein to the contrary provided, each party may dispose of his or her property in any way, and each hereby waives and relinquishes any and all right he or she may now have or hereafter acquire, under the present or future laws of any jurisdiction to share in the property or the estate of the other party as a result of the marital relationship including, but not limited to, equitable distribution of property pursuant to N.J.S.A. 2A:34-23 or other similar statutory rights, dower, curtesy, statutory allowances, widow allowance, homestead rights, or rights under N.J.S.A. 3A:35-5, right to take against or under any Last Will and Testament of the other even if named as beneficiary therein and the right to act as administrator or executor of the other's estate even if named in such capacities therein unless such last will and testament is executed subsequent to the execution of this Agreement. Both parties further acknowledge that they have been advised of their right to seek independent tax consultation from a Certified Public Accountant, tax attorney or other tax expert, to explain to them the potential impact and tax liabilities created by the within Marital Settlement Agreement."

**Section 10.2, Releases** (page 44, Bates KM00067) — the mutual general release. Husband's release of Wife (broad, unconditional):

> "10.2 Releases. a) Subject to the terms and conditions of the within Agreement and except as herein to the contrary provided, the Husband remises, releases and forever discharges the Wife from any and all actions, suits, debts and claims, demands and obligations whatsoever, both in law and in equity, which he ever had, now has, or may hereafter have against the Wife upon or by reason of any matter, cause or thing up to the date of the execution of this Agreement, it being the intention of the Husband that henceforth there shall be only such rights and obligations as are specifically provided in this Agreement."

Wife's release of Husband as to Equitable Distribution and her Tevis claim (conditional/limited — subject to a carve-back for third-party litigation arising from Husband's conduct, e.g., the B.B. litigation):

> "b) Subject to the terms and conditions of the within Agreement and except as herein to the contrary provided, the Wife remises, releases and forever discharges the Husband from the claims for Equitable Distribution and her Tevis claim set forth in the Complaint for Divorce filed on her behalf on March 9, 2018 that is incorporated by reference herein and made a part hereof. Notwithstanding the foregoing, this release, shall be void nunc pro tunc, and the Wife shall be entitled to assert any and all actions, suits, debts, claims, demands and obligations whatsoever, both in law and in equity she may possess against the Husband, in the event that: (a) litigation, arbitration, or other form of dispute resolution is, or has been, commenced by a third-party asserting any actions, suits, debts and claims, demands and obligations whatsoever, either in law and in equity, against or arising out of conduct, or alleged conduct, by the Husband, and (b) claims are asserted against the Wife and/or any Trusts identified in Article 7 herein in any litigation, arbitration, or other form of dispute resolution or in supplementary proceedings in connection with conduct or alleged conduct of the Husband."

**Note:** Unlike the discovery-waiver language, the Wife's release of the *Equitable Distribution* claim is expressly *not* absolute — it is voided "nunc pro tunc" if third-party litigation (like B.B.'s suit) is commenced asserting claims arising from Husband's conduct against Wife or the Trusts. This is a double-edged provision: it is the contractual mechanism by which Kim's cross-claims in the B.B. litigation survive, but it is narrowly limited to claims connected to litigation over Husband's conduct — it does not by its terms revive or reopen the separate, already-closed question of what was in the marital estate for equitable-distribution purposes (that remains governed by Article 9's discovery waiver and full-disclosure representation, not by 10.2(b)).

**Section 10.3, Bankruptcy** (page 45, Bates KM00068):

> "10.3 Bankruptcy. All of the provisions in this Agreement for the payment of money by either party, as well as all other financial obligations of the parties as set forth in the within Agreement are not dischargeable in bankruptcy. The parties hereby acknowledge that the debts incurred by either of them under this Agreement, other than as expressly set forth herein, are of such a nature that they are not dischargeable in a bankruptcy proceeding, whether voluntary or involuntary."

### D. Article 11: Judicial Proceedings — Non-Merger (page 46, Bates KM00069)

> "11.3 Non-merger. Notwithstanding its incorporation in conformity with Section 11.2 into a judgment terminating the marriage, entered by a court of competent jurisdiction, the provisions of this agreement shall not merge with, but shall survive such judgment in its entirety, in full force and effect, except as may be invalidated by a court of competent jurisdiction in accordance with Section 11.4 hereof."

## TASK 2 — Search for Documentation of a ~$1,000,000 Total Payment to Kim from the W.H. Mell Capital Account

### What was searched

Beyond the MSA itself, the following were searched across the user's entire Google Drive (not limited to the "Whalen Response" folder): full-text queries for `"Capital Account" + "Kimberly"`, `"Bloomfield"`, `"1,000,000" + "Kimberly"`, `"wire transfer" + "Mell"`, title searches for `"distribution"` / `"Capital Account"`, and title/full-text searches for the two known depositions of Steven Bradley Mell (2021 and 2023) taken in the related B.B. v. Mell civil action, ESX-L-7200-19, since the Capital Account was examined at length in both. No bank statements, wire-transfer records, canceled checks, a distribution schedule, or a Mark Bloomfield forensic-accounting report/output were located anywhere in the Drive.

### What the MSA itself says (baseline, for reference — Article 3.5(a), pages 13–16, Bates KM00036–39)

- Capital Account balance as of July 28, 2018: **$1,625,000.00** (represented by Husband, subject to adjustments).
- "The parties recognize that the first $800,000.00 has already been paid out, with Wife, having received sixty percent (60%) and Husband having received forty percent (40%) of those funds" — i.e., **$480,000 to Wife**, $320,000 to Husband, as of the MSA's execution (April 2019).
- The balance/disposition of the Capital Account **after** the first $800,000 (i.e., up to ~$825,000 more) was, as of the MSA, "currently in dispute in whole or in part," to be reviewed by Wife's forensic accountant, Mark D. Bloomfield, CPA/ABV/CFF, with any "Disputed Disbursements" subject to mediation/arbitration.
- Any *remaining* Capital Account balance after resolving disputed disbursements was to be split 60% Wife / 40% Husband going forward.

This is the $480,000 figure already identified in prior research and is **not** the ~$1,000,000 the debtor recalls.

### What was found: Mell's own sworn deposition testimony (no independent financial records located)

No bank records, wires, or an actual Bloomfield report/reconciliation were found. However, **Steven Bradley Mell's own deposition testimony**, given on two occasions in the related B.B. v. Mell civil action, directly and repeatedly asserts that Kim ultimately received **60% of the entire ~$1.625 million Capital Account balance — not just 60% of the first $800,000** — which computes to roughly **$975,000**, closely matching the debtor's recollection of "~$1,000,000 total."

**1. Deposition of Steven Bradley Mell, September 22, 2021** (Drive file ID `1zD9rS_ozykmJHZlmuVf9CuePtOTCa25M`, "Brad Mell Depo Sept 22, 2021_compressed.pdf") — testimony on how the Capital Account was distributed as W.H. Mell wound down:

> "A Right. So as the firm was winding down, I believe — if memory serves, she got 60 percent, I got 40 percent. So as — as the business started to wind down, whatever the balances were moving forward, she'd get 60 percent and I'd get 40 percent. It was payout over a — you know, a year and a half or so, a year, I guess. I don't know."

Same deposition, confirming the $1,625,000 total balance figure and admitting no accounting was ever created of what was actually disbursed:

> "Q If I were to tell you that there's a reference to a document, the MSA, that has it being 1,625,000, would that sound approximately correct to you? A Yes. That's what that references."
>
> "Q Was there an accounting provided or made of your expenditures from this equity balance? A I don't believe so. … Q Do you know if anybody created an accounting of that on your behalf? A No, they wouldn't have. Q Do you know if Ms. Ruggles Mell had anybody that created an accounting of that? A I — I don't think so, no."

**2. Deposition of Steven Bradley Mell, July 27, 2023** (Drive file ID `1dMlmR-IsR7FHtGDwTVbVGpY3XRPNj7vj`, "Brad Mell Depo July 27, 2023.pdf") — this is the most direct and quotable admission. Questioned by Attorney A.J. Borrelli (counsel for Mountainside Securities) at transcript pages 168–169:

> [p.168:15–21] "A … When we did a snapshot of what the capital account was worth, Kim got 60 percent of that. So I didn't deduct anything to pay my lawyer's fees that would have impacted her. Do you see what I'm saying? So she got her 60 percent and I don't know where the accounting is missing or who is missing what. But something clearly is missing."
>
> [p.168:22–25, Q] "Well, yeah. And are you saying that you believe that she got 60 percent of the 800 that was not in dispute or are you saying you believe she got 60 percent of the 1.625?"
>
> [p.169:1–4, A] "I'm telling you she got 60 percent of the capital account, which is roughly one-six, one-seven. Whatever that number was, she got her 60 percent. I'm 100 percent on that."

60% of "one-six, one-seven" (i.e., roughly $1.6–1.7 million) is **approximately $960,000–$1,020,000** — this is the clearest evidentiary anchor in the Drive for the debtor's recollection that Kim was told/is aware she received approximately $1,000,000 total from the Capital Account.

**Important caveat — this testimony is internally inconsistent and was walked back minutes later in the same deposition**, when Mell was confronted with a documentary exhibit (Exhibit 3, described as reflecting "distributions under equity") showing only $800,000 in recorded distributions:

> [p.170:9–16, Q] "And the only distribution amount reflected there is $800,000. Do you see that, Mr. Mell? A Correct. Q So wouldn't we expect to see the — if the capital account was distributed in full, the entire 1.6 million, wouldn't we see it there somewhere on Exhibit 3?"
>
> [p.170:17–20, A] "Well, it wasn't distributed 100 percent in full at this time. Because the broker-dealer was still — even though it had withdrawn, it was still winding down."
>
> [p.171:11–13, A] "Okay. The 800 was paid out. But all I know is there was a balance after December 31st, 2018 because the company was still winding down."
>
> [p.171:23–25, A] "Million dollar question. I don't know where the actual books and records are…"

So within the same 2023 deposition, Mell (a) confidently and unequivocally swears Kim received 60% of the full ~$1.6–1.7 million balance ("I'm 100 percent on that"), and then (b) when shown a document reflecting only $800,000 in recorded distributions, retreats to "I don't know" and cannot reconcile the two positions. No balance sheet, distribution schedule, or accounting for 2019 (or any later year) was produced or located to resolve the discrepancy — Mell testified he does not know where the W.H. Mell books/records are, and no such records or a Bloomfield report/output appear anywhere in the Drive search results.

### Bottom line on Task 2

- **No independent documentary evidence** (bank/wire records, account statements, a distribution schedule, or Mark Bloomfield's forensic report/output) evidencing a ~$1,000,000 total payment to Kim was located anywhere in Google Drive, despite a broad search across all folders using the requested search terms and related terms (Capital Account, distribution, Bloomfield, wire transfer, 1,000,000/1 million, Kimberly Mell payment).
- **What was found instead is testimonial, not documentary**: Steven Bradley Mell's own sworn deposition testimony, given twice (2021 and 2023) in the related B.B. v. Mell civil action, states that Kim received 60% of the *entire* Capital Account (~$1.625M as of July 2018), not merely 60% of the $800,000 tranche disclosed in the MSA — arithmetically consistent with the debtor's recollection of ~$1,000,000 total. The clearest, most quotable statement is Mell's July 27, 2023 deposition at 169:1–4: "I'm telling you she got 60 percent of the capital account, which is roughly one-six, one-seven. Whatever that number was, she got her 60 percent. I'm 100 percent on that."
- **That same testimony is contradicted moments later** in the identical deposition when Mell is shown documentary Exhibit 3 reflecting only $800,000 in recorded distributions, and he cannot explain or locate records for the remaining ~$825,000. No balance sheets, books, or an accounting for 2019 forward were produced or located.
- **Recommendation:** This should be characterized in any filing as Mell's own prior sworn admission (subject to impeachment/adoptive-admission use), not as independently verified accounting — the number should not be presented as a proven, documented fact of an actual $1,000,000 wire/disbursement. If corroboration is needed, the logical next targets (not found in this Drive) would be: (a) the Mark Bloomfield forensic report/output referenced in MSA §3.5(a) and §12.5(b), (b) W.H. Mell's/Bank of New York records for the Capital Account for 2018–2019, and (c) Deposition Exhibit 3 from the July 27, 2023 Mell deposition itself (the balance sheet/equity-distribution document referenced at pp.170–171), none of which currently appear in Drive as a standalone exhibit.
