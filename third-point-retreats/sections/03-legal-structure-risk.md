## 3.1 The structure at a glance

The family has settled on a four-layer stack. Described from the top down, as the planned approach pending final attorney documentation:

| Layer | Entity | What it holds | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Family Trust** | The beneficial interest in the Land Trust | Ultimate ownership; estate-planning and succession vehicle for the family. Holds no operating role. |
| 2 | **Land Trust** (in Massachusetts practice, a **nominee realty trust**) | Fee title to 173 Polpis Road | Record owner of the real estate. Provides title privacy and clean transferability. Landlord under the lease. |
| 3 | **Third Point Retreats, LLC** (Wyoming) | A leasehold interest in the property; the operating business, brand, bookings, bank accounts | Leases the house from the Land Trust and runs the short-term rental business: markets, contracts with guests, collects rent, pays operating expenses, employs/engages the house manager and vendors. **This is the entity that faces the public and absorbs the litigation risk.** As lessee it is also the statutory "operator" for both DOR and Town registration purposes — see § 3.6. |
| 3a | **Sister LLC — Jeep** | The beach-permitted Jeep | Holds the highest-severity single asset separately, with its own commercial auto policy. Third Point Retreats, LLC sub-rents or brokers it to guests under a separate signed agreement. **Recommend forming in Massachusetts** — see the cost note below. |

### Why each layer exists, in plain business language

**The Family Trust** is the estate-planning layer: continuity of ownership across generations and control of the asset — not liability protection in itself. Its job in this structure is to be **as far from the guest as possible**. Every layer below it exists so that a plaintiff who slips on the pool deck sues an entity, not the family.

**The Land Trust** is the title layer. In Massachusetts the customary vehicle is a **nominee realty trust**: the trustee appears as owner in the Registry of Deeds, the schedule of beneficiaries is *not* recorded, and a trustee's certificate under **M.G.L. c. 184, § 35** is what a title examiner relies on. The statute is real and current: a certificate signed by a person appearing to be a trustee, certifying the identity of the trustees or beneficiaries, the trustees' authority as to trust-owned real estate, and the existence of conditions precedent, **"shall be binding on all trustees and the trust estate in favor of a purchaser or other person relying in good faith on the certificate."** The Real Estate Bar Association's title standards treat a conforming § 35 certificate as sufficient for non-testamentary trusts, nominee trusts included, and the trust instrument itself stays unrecorded — which is where the privacy comes from. ([M.G.L. c. 184 § 35](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-ii/title-i/chapter-184/section-35/); [REBA Form 35](https://www.reba.net/Userfiles/files/pdfs/form35.pdf))

Two cautions, both now stated as settled rather than hedged: a Massachusetts nominee trust is *not* a liability shield the way an LLC is — the trustee acts only at the direction of the beneficiaries, and the beneficiaries are generally treated as principals — and **Massachusetts has no Illinois-style land-trust statute**, so "land trust" in the family's notes is shorthand for the MA-law vehicle counsel selects. *(Remaining for counsel — and genuinely so: whether the family's existing instrument is drafted as a nominee trust or as a true trust with independent trustee powers, and whether a § 35 certificate is on record at the Nantucket Registry, are facts about a private, unrecorded document. No amount of public research reaches them. See § 3.9 item 2.)*

**Third Point Retreats, LLC** is the liability layer and the business. Everything that touches a guest happens here: booking terms, the rental agreement, the security deposit, vendor contracts, payroll, the bank account. If a guest is injured and sues, the intended defendant is this LLC and its insurance — not the Land Trust, not the Family Trust, not any individual family member. This is the wall the family is paying to build.

**The separate Jeep LLC** isolates the single asset with the worst risk-to-revenue ratio in the program. A Jeep driven on Great Point sand by a renter who has never driven on sand is a plausible six- or seven-figure auto-liability event; the vehicle generates perhaps $12–15K a season. In its own entity with its own commercial auto policy, a catastrophic auto claim attaches to a company whose only asset is a Jeep — it does not reach the house business, the rental income, or the real estate. Brad's instinct is correct and should be preserved even at the cost of a second filing fee and registered agent.

**The "WY or MA?" question on the Jeep entity is now answered on the numbers.** A Wyoming LLC that owns a vehicle garaged, registered, insured and rented in Massachusetts is doing business in Massachusetts and must foreign-qualify — **$500 to register plus $500 every year thereafter** (M.G.L. c. 156C § 48; see § 3.3), on top of Wyoming's $100 formation and $60 annual license tax and a Wyoming registered agent. A Massachusetts LLC costs **$500 to form and $500 annually** and needs no second registration or second agent. Wyoming's headline benefit — charging-order exclusivity — protects the *member's* interest from the member's own creditors, which is not the Jeep's risk profile; the Jeep's risk is outside-in auto liability, against which a Massachusetts LLC plus a commercial auto policy performs identically. **Recommendation: form the Jeep entity in Massachusetts and save roughly $600–700 a year and one registered-agent relationship.** *(For counsel: the choice is a recommendation, not a filing instruction — if the family wants a single-domicile story across both entities for administrative simplicity, that is a legitimate reason to overrule the cost math.)*

## 3.2 Why Wyoming — and the honest limits of what Wyoming buys

Wyoming is the most-used domicile for asset-protection LLCs for four reasons that are real and one benefit that is frequently oversold.

**1. Charging-order protection as the exclusive remedy — verified text, including the single-member point.** Under **Wyo. Stat. § 17-29-503(g)** (current through the 2025 statutes), the charging-order section *"provides the exclusive remedy by which a person seeking to enforce a judgment against a judgment debtor, **including any judgment debtor who may be the sole member**, dissociated member or transferee, may, in the capacity of the judgment creditor, satisfy the judgment from the judgment debtor's transferable interest or from the assets of the limited liability company."* The statute also disallows foreclosure and forced sale of the interest and bars any order conferring management rights. The **express inclusion of the sole member** is the operative advantage over states that read a single-member exception into their charging-order provisions. ([Wyo. Stat. § 17-29-503, 2025](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/title-17/chapter-29/article-5/section-17-29-503/))

**2. No state income tax, trivial compliance burden — current fees.** Wyoming imposes no individual or corporate income tax. Formation is **$100** for Articles of Organization. The annual report license tax is the **greater of $60 or $0.0002 per dollar of assets located and employed in Wyoming**; with no Wyoming-situs assets the LLC pays the **$60 floor**. It is due **on or before the first day of the LLC's anniversary month** each year. ([Wyoming SOS fee schedule](https://sos.wyo.gov/business/docs/businessfees.pdf))

**3. Privacy at the state level.** Wyoming's public filings require no disclosure of members or managers — neither the articles of organization nor the annual report puts the family's names in a searchable state database. Combined with a nominee realty trust on title in Massachusetts, this meaningfully raises the cost of a casual search connecting "Third Point" to the family.

**4. A mature, business-friendly LLC statute** with an established registered-agent industry. A **Wyoming registered agent with a physical Wyoming street address is mandatory** — an ongoing cost of roughly $50–200/year.

### What Wyoming does *not* buy — read this carefully

- **Charging-order protection is the wrong shield for our actual risk.** It is *inside-out* protection: it stops a creditor of the *member* (the trust) from reaching the LLC's assets. Our realistic exposure is *outside-in*: a guest injured at the house sues the operating LLC. Against that, the protection is **ordinary limited liability plus insurance** — the same protection a Massachusetts LLC would provide. Wyoming's famous statute is a genuine benefit against a *different* risk than the one that keeps us up at night. Any advisor who pitches Wyoming primarily as a defense against slip-and-fall claims is confusing the two.
- **A Massachusetts court applies Massachusetts law to Massachusetts events.** Litigation over an injury at 173 Polpis Road will be brought in Massachusetts. MA courts generally look to Wyoming law for internal-affairs questions (who owns the LLC, what the operating agreement means) but apply their own law to tort and premises-liability claims, and veil-piercing analysis is fact- and forum-driven. Forming in Wyoming does not import Wyoming's protective posture into a Massachusetts courtroom. *(This one stays with counsel, correctly: choice-of-law and veil-piercing outcomes are applied judgment on facts not yet in existence, not a lookup.)*
- **Single-member LLCs draw more veil-piercing scrutiny.** Wyoming's own Supreme Court pierced a single-member Wyoming LLC in *GreenHunter Energy, Inc. v. Western Ecosystems Technology, Inc.*, 2014 WY 144, on facts including deliberate undercapitalization, disregarded-entity tax treatment, and the member's manipulation of the entity to take reward without risk. Wyoming later amended its LLC Act to tighten the standard, but the lesson stands: **the structure protects you only if you run it like a real company.** See § 3.4.
- **Privacy has hard federal edges — and the CTA question is now answerable.** See the box below.

### Corporate Transparency Act / FinCEN beneficial ownership — status as of August 2026

This was the item most likely to be stale, and it has been re-verified end-to-end. **Current status: Third Point Retreats, LLC, as a domestically formed entity, has no beneficial-ownership reporting obligation.**

- On **March 21, 2025**, FinCEN issued an **interim final rule** (published in the Federal Register **March 26, 2025**) that **redefined "reporting company" to mean only entities formed under the law of a foreign country that register to do business in a U.S. State or Tribal jurisdiction.** All entities created in the United States — the former "domestic reporting companies" — **and their beneficial owners are exempt** from BOI reporting. Foreign reporting companies additionally need not report BOI for any beneficial owner who is a **U.S. person**. ([FinCEN BOI](https://www.fincen.gov/boi); [FinCEN news release](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-removes-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirements-us-companies-and-us); [90 Fed. Reg., Mar. 26, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/26/2025-05199/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision-and-deadline-extension))
- **FinCEN's own BOI landing page still carries the "Updated March 26, 2025" alert as of August 2026** — i.e., the interim final rule is still the operative guidance, not a superseded one.
- A **final rule was received by OMB's Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs on June 5, 2026** and had not been published as of this writing; FinCEN had originally promised a final rule by the end of 2025, and slipped on it in part because of appropriations lapses. ([Holland & Knight, June 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/what-happened-to-fincens-corporate-transparency-act))
- In **May 2026 the GAO** published a report on the interim final rule, noting the exemptions eliminate **more than 99% of entities** that previously had to report. Congressional bills to repeal or reform the CTA have been introduced but none had been enacted as of early 2026.

**What to do with this.** Do **not** file a BOI report for the new LLC — there is nothing to file. Do **put the final rule on a watch calendar**: the pending OMB rule is the live risk, and if it restores domestic reporting the family will need names, dates of birth, addresses and identifying documents for the beneficial owners of both LLCs on short notice. Assemble that file now and leave it unfiled. And regardless of the CTA: the IRS knows who the owners are, and banks collect beneficial-ownership data under their own KYC rules. **Do not sell anyone in the family on "anonymous."**

- **Nothing here is offshore-grade.** This is a well-built domestic structure that raises the cost and friction of litigation and defeats casual, opportunistic claims. It does not make the family judgment-proof and should never be described that way, internally or externally.

**Net assessment:** Wyoming is a defensible, low-cost, low-friction choice with genuine privacy and creditor-remedy advantages; no reason to revisit the decision for the operating entity. But roughly **80% of the real-world protection in this plan comes from (a) respecting entity separateness and (b) the insurance stack** — not from the choice of formation state.

## 3.3 Foreign qualification — operating a Wyoming LLC in Massachusetts

**The compliance follows the property, not the formation state.** A Wyoming LLC that leases and commercially operates a house in Nantucket is doing business in Massachusetts. This does not weaken the Wyoming strategy — Third Point Retreats, LLC remains a Wyoming entity governed by Wyoming's LLC Act, and the charging-order and privacy advantages survive. It simply adds a Massachusetts registration overlay.

**Is c. 156C still the right chapter?** Yes — verified. Massachusetts recodified its **business corporation** law into **c. 156D** in 2004, but **LLCs were not swept into that recodification**: the Massachusetts Limited Liability Company Act remains **M.G.L. c. 156C**, and foreign LLC registration remains **§ 48**. The Secretary of the Commonwealth's current Corporations Division form for this filing is still captioned to c. 156C § 48. ([M.G.L. c. 156C § 48](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section48); [SOC filing form](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/corporations/download/c156cs48fllcregistration.pdf))

**What that means practically (planned approach, to be executed by MA counsel):**

1. **Foreign LLC registration with the Secretary of the Commonwealth** under **M.G.L. c. 156C § 48**, filed **within ten days after the LLC commences doing business** in Massachusetts. Filing fee **$500**. The application must be accompanied by a **certificate of legal existence / good standing** issued by the Wyoming Secretary of State, and must name a **Massachusetts resident agent** with written consent.
2. **Annual report** to the Secretary of the Commonwealth, due **on or before the anniversary date of the registration filing**, fee **$500** — the same statute fixes both: *"the fee for the filing of the application of registration and each annual report shall be five hundred dollars."* This is not a "budget ~$500, confirm later" item; $500 is the statutory number. ([SOC Foreign LLC annual report](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/corporations/filing-by-subject/limited-liability/corporations-foreign-limited-liability-company.htm))
3. **A Massachusetts resident agent**, distinct from the Wyoming one. Two agents, two annual fees — budget for both.
4. **The penalty for skipping this is the sharp edge of the whole plan.** Under **M.G.L. c. 156C § 54**, a foreign LLC doing business here that fails to register *"shall, for each year that such failure shall continue, be fined not more than five hundred dollars,"* and — the part that matters — *"**No action shall be maintained or recovery had by the foreign limited liability company in any of the courts of the commonwealth as long as such failure continues.**"* The statute also expressly preserves the LLC's right to **defend** any action, keeps its **contracts valid**, and provides that failure to register does **not** make members personally liable for the company's obligations; and it makes the **Secretary of the Commonwealth the agent for service of process** on an unregistered foreign LLC. Net: an unregistered Third Point Retreats, LLC could be sued but could not sue — it could not pursue a guest who trashed the house, enforce a damage claim, or chase a defaulting vendor. **Registration is a precondition to enforcing our own rental agreement.** ([M.G.L. c. 156C § 54](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section54))
5. **State and local short-term-rental registrations must be in the operating entity's name** — see § 3.6, which confirms the LLC is the correct registrant as statutory "operator."
6. **Massachusetts tax filings.** Massachusetts taxes MA-source income regardless of formation state. Depending on classification: a multi-member LLC generally files **Form 3** and nonresident members file **Form 1-NR/PY** (a composite return is available for qualifying electing nonresidents); a single-member LLC may be disregarded, pushing reporting up to the trust. The **MA 4% surtax** threshold is indexed annually and is **$1,107,750 for tax year 2026** (it was $1,083,150 for 2025) — relevant in a sale year or an unusually strong season. ([Mass.gov, 4% surtax](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-4-surtax-on-taxable-income)) *(For the CPA — genuinely: which classification the family should elect depends on the trust's own tax posture, the beneficiaries' residency, and the family's broader return, none of which are public facts.)*

## 3.4 Lease mechanics between the Land Trust and Third Point Retreats, LLC

This is the joint that fails. The entire structure depends on a court accepting that the Land Trust and the LLC are genuinely separate parties transacting at arm's length. If a plaintiff's lawyer can characterize the LLC as a shell that exists only on paper — no real lease, no real rent, one commingled bank account, no capital — the alter-ego argument becomes available, the wall comes down, and the family has paid formation fees for nothing.

**This section is deliberately still framed as work for counsel, and that is correct rather than lazy: everything below is drafting and execution, not research.** No public source can tell us what this lease should say.

- **A real written commercial lease**, signed by the trustee for the Land Trust and by the manager for the LLC, with a defined term, renewal mechanics, rent, allocation of maintenance and capital obligations, insurance and indemnity provisions, and default remedies. Not a one-page memo.
- **Market rent, documented.** Set rent at a commercially reasonable figure and keep the reasoning in the file — ideally a broker's opinion or written analysis benchmarked against the property's economics (peak rate $32,500/wk; annual carry ≈ $200,000). Below-market rent risks recharacterization as a disguised gift or distribution; above-market rent strips the operating entity of the capitalization it needs. Non-arm's-length related-party leasing can also raise **gift-tax and imputed-income** questions, and IRC **§ 469 self-rental** rules can recharacterize the income. **The CPA and counsel should set the number together.**
- **Rent actually paid, on schedule, from the LLC's own account.** A lease that exists on paper but whose rent is never transferred is worse than no lease — it is documentary evidence of a sham.
- **Separate bank accounts and books.** LLC revenue goes into the LLC's account. Owner draws are documented distributions, not ad-hoc transfers. No family personal expenses through the LLC; no LLC expenses on a family card without reimbursement.
- **Adequate capitalization** — enough working capital to plausibly run a business carrying $200K/year, plus the insurance stack in § 3.5. Deliberate undercapitalization was an explicit factor in *GreenHunter*.
- **An operating agreement**, even for a single-member LLC, with named management, signature authority, and distribution mechanics.
- **Correct signature blocks everywhere.** Guest agreements, vendor contracts, website terms, STR registrations and insurance policies should all name **Third Point Retreats, LLC** — not the family, not the trustee, not "Brad."
- **Timing matters for fraudulent-transfer purposes.** Massachusetts' Uniform Voidable Transactions Act, **M.G.L. c. 109A**, scrutinizes transfers made *after* a claim arises. The family's position is unusually clean: the property has **never been rented**, so there are no existing rental-related claimants. **Do the restructuring now, before the first 2027 booking is accepted** — that timing is itself a defense, and it will never be this easy again.

## 3.5 The insurance stack

**Entity structure is not a substitute for insurance. Insurance is the first line of defense; the entities are the backstop.** In a premises-liability claim the LLC is the defendant, and what pays the claim — and, just as importantly, the *defense costs* — is the policy.

| Coverage | Sits with | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Property / hazard (dwelling, contents, loss of rents)** | Land Trust (owner of the asset) | Must be written knowing the property is **commercially short-term rented** — a standard homeowner's or seasonal-dwelling policy can be voided by undisclosed commercial use. **M.G.L. c. 175 § 4F affirmatively requires an operator to notify any insurer writing a homeowners or renters policy on the property of the intent to operate a short-term rental.** Coastal wind/flood deductibles and any FEMA flood-zone requirement need separate attention on a Polpis Road property. |
| **Commercial general liability / STR-specific liability** | Third Point Retreats, LLC | **Statutory floor confirmed: M.G.L. c. 175 § 4F requires an STR operator to maintain liability insurance of not less than $1,000,000 to cover each short-term rental**, unless the rental is offered through a hosting platform maintaining equal or greater coverage — which a direct-booking house does **not** have, so the obligation sits squarely on us. The coverage must defend and indemnify the operator and any tenants or owners in the building for bodily injury and property damage arising from the short-term rental. Nantucket independently requires a certificate evidencing **at least $1,000,000 per claim** at registration (Town Regulations Ch. 338). $1M is the legal floor, not the right number for a $32,500/week house. ([M.G.L. c. 175 § 4F](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter175/Section4F); [Mass.gov STR insurance](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/short-term-rental-insurance)) |
| **Umbrella / excess liability** | Over both the LLC and the Land Trust | At this asset and rate tier, $5M is a conversation starter, not a ceiling. Ask the broker to price $5M / $10M and let the family choose knowingly. |
| **Land Trust named as additional insured on the LLC's liability policy; LLC indemnifies the Land Trust under the lease** | Both | **Do not skip this.** A plaintiff will name the record owner as well as the operator. The Land Trust is not automatically insulated because it is "just the landlord." |
| **Commercial auto — the Jeep** | Jeep LLC | Full commercial coverage as already decided, written for rental/loan-to-guest use, with the oversand-permit and beach-driving exposure disclosed. Personal auto policies exclude rental-for-fee use. |
| **Workers' compensation** | Third Point Retreats, LLC | **Threshold confirmed at the statute:** M.G.L. c. 152 § 1 defines a *part-time domestic servant* as one working **"less than sixteen hours per week,"** and coverage is elective only as to seasonal, casual or part-time domestic servants — i.e. **coverage is mandatory at 16+ hours/week.** The Domestic Workers' Bill of Rights (M.G.L. c. 149 § 190) adds a written agreement, notice of rights, and timekeeping obligations. A full-season house manager will cross the threshold. ([M.G.L. c. 152 § 1](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter152/Section1); [M.G.L. c. 149 § 190](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section190)) |
| **Cyber / privacy liability** | Third Point Retreats, LLC | See the 201 CMR 17.00 note in § 3.7 risk 13 — the trigger is narrower than Revision 1 assumed, but the Jeep program almost certainly trips it. |
| **Vendor certificates of insurance** | Collected by the LLC | Pool/spa tech, housekeeping, landscaper, handyman, chefs, charter operators, bike vendor — every vendor under contract, insured, naming the LLC as additional insured where practical. The cheapest risk transfer available. |

Two structural notes for the broker conversation: **(a)** make sure there is no coverage gap between the Land Trust's property policy and the LLC's liability policy — the classic failure is a claim that each carrier says belongs to the other; **(b)** the umbrella must sit above *both* entities and the auto policy, or the Jeep's separateness creates an uncovered seam. *(Limits and named insureds remain a broker/counsel judgment — that is placement advice on this family's balance sheet, not a researchable fact.)*

## 3.6 Short-term-rental regulatory compliance

### State level (Massachusetts) — the tax question, closed

- **Registration with DOR before accepting reservations.** The **operator** registers through **MassTaxConnect** and receives a **certificate of registration per property**; registered operators appear in the state's Public Registry of Lodging Operators. Registration is required regardless of how few days the property is rented. A short-term rental is a rental of **31 consecutive days or fewer**. ([Mass.gov, room occupancy excise](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/room-occupancy-excise-tax); [830 CMR 64G.1.1](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/830-CMR-64g11-massachusetts-room-occupancy-excise))
- **Room occupancy excise — state: 5.7%** (5% statutory plus a 0.7% uncodified surtax), collected from the guest and remitted by the operator.
- **Local option excise — up to 6%; Nantucket is at 6%.**
- **✅ RESOLVED — the Cape Cod & Islands Water Protection Fund 2.75% excise does NOT apply to Nantucket. The correct all-in rate is 11.7%, not 14.45%.** The CCIWPF excise under M.G.L. c. 64G § 3C is imposed only on occupancies in a municipality that **is a member** of the Fund. Membership was automatic for the Barnstable County towns; the Dukes County and Nantucket County towns (Aquinnah, Chilmark, Edgartown, Gosnold, **Nantucket**, Oak Bluffs, Tisbury, West Tisbury) were given the *option* to join by town-meeting vote. **Nantucket has not joined.** The Cape Cod Commission, which administers the Fund, lists exactly **15 member communities — Barnstable, Bourne, Brewster, Chatham, Dennis, Eastham, Falmouth, Harwich, Mashpee, Orleans, Provincetown, Sandwich, Truro, Wellfleet and Yarmouth** — and identifies the Nantucket Town Manager as an **ex-officio, non-voting** member of the management board, i.e. explicitly not a member town. The Town of Nantucket's own STR FAQ lists the taxes an operator must collect as **5.70% state + 6.00% local + 3.00% community impact fee**, with **no CCIWPF line**. **The 11.7% figure already in the marketing materials and rate table is correct. Publish it.** ([Cape Cod Commission — CCIWPF](https://www.capecodcommission.org/our-work/cape-cod-and-islands-water-protection-fund/); [Town of Nantucket STR FAQ](https://nantucket-ma.gov/3337/FAQs-for-Short-term-Rentals))
  - *Standing watch item, narrow and specific:* Nantucket **can** join by a majority vote of Town Meeting at any time, which would add 2.75% (≈ **$894/week** at the peak rate, **$9–11K across a peak season**). The CCIWPF management board has already amended its regulations to clarify when new members become eligible for subsidies — i.e. the door is deliberately propped open. **Check each Annual and Special Town Meeting warrant for a CCIWPF acceptance article, and build the booking engine so the tax rate is a configurable field, not a hard-coded 11.7%.**
- **✅ RESOLVED — the 3% community impact fee does not apply to us.** Nantucket has accepted **M.G.L. c. 64G § 3D(a)**, imposing a 3% community impact fee on the transfer of occupancy of a short-term rental in a **"professionally-managed unit,"** statutorily defined as *"1 of 2 or more short-term rental units that are located in the same city or town, operated by the same operator and are not located within a single-family, two-family, or three-family dwelling that includes the operator's primary residence."* **Third Point is a single unit with a single operator in Nantucket, so the fee is not triggered.** The trigger is **two or more units in the same town under the same operator** — so this flips the moment the family adds a second Nantucket rental under Third Point Retreats, LLC. That is a real strategic constraint on expansion and belongs in the Section 2 growth discussion: a second island property adds 3% to the tax on *both* properties. ### Town of Nantucket — registration, and a newly surfaced eligibility question

**The governing instruments are two, and it matters which is which.** The **bylaw** is **Chapter 123** of the Town Code (Short-Term Rentals), effective January 1, 2023, amended repeatedly through December 2024. The **operational detail** — occupancy, fee, insurance evidence — lives in **Chapter 338** of the Town Regulations (Short-Term Rental Property Regulations), jointly adopted by the Board of Health and Select Board on **September 21, 2022** and **most recently amended March 19, 2026**, promulgated under the rulemaking delegation in § 123-4. Revision 1 attributed the occupancy rule to Chapter 123; it is in Chapter 338. ([Town Code Ch. 123](https://ecode360.com/41522694); [Town Regulations Ch. 338](https://ecode360.com/39942148))

- **Certificate of registration from the Board of Health is mandatory** — no operation without one since January 1, 2023 (§ 123-3(A)). The application must carry the **DOR identification number or proof of exemption under G.L. c. 64G**, an attestation of compliance with federal, state and local law and of being current on taxes and fees, operator contact information, and a **certificate of insurance**.
- **The certificate is issued to the OPERATOR, and a transfer requires a new application** (§ 123-3(G)). Chapter 123-2 defines **OPERATOR** as *"a person operating a short-term rental including, but not limited to, the owner or proprietor of such premises, the **lessee**, sublessee, mortgagee in possession, licensee or any other person otherwise operating such short-term rental,"* and states the intent that it carry the same meaning as **G.L. c. 64G § 1**. **This confirms the plan: Third Point Retreats, LLC, as lessee under the Land Trust lease, is the correct registrant at both DOR and the Board of Health.** It also confirms the urgency — because the operator is changing, this is a **new application, not a renewal**, and § 123-3(G) says so expressly.
- **Fee: $250 per unit, per year**, submitted with the application (Ch. 338).
- **Insurance evidence: liability limits of at least $1,000,000 per claim**, unless offered through a hosting platform maintaining equal or greater coverage. Direct booking means the platform exception is unavailable to us (Ch. 338).
- **✅ Calendar confirmed.** Certificates are valid one year and **expire October 31**. The Town's renewal window runs **September 1 – October 31**; **new registrations must be completed by October 31**; § 123-3(F) requires renewal applications, with a compliance attestation, **no later than November 1**. The Town's own registration page states the window and the requirements — updated insurance evidence with **$1,000,000** liability coverage, an attestation that the prior year's rentals complied with Town regulations, and the fee. **This is a hard date on the 2027-season timeline and it lands inside the Aug–Jan Nantucket booking window.** ([Town of Nantucket — Short-Term Rentals](https://nantucket-ma.gov/2673/Short-Term-Rentals))
- **Named contact:** the Health Department's **Short-Term Rental Community Liaison, Lauri Elliott** — lelliott@nantucket-ma.gov, 508-221-6998. She is the right first call on both the certificate transfer and the eligibility question immediately below.
- **STR certificate C0570541970** is on file and currently used in the website footer. **It must be re-applied for in the name of Third Point Retreats, LLC before the 2027 season.** Do not take a 2027 booking on a certificate registered to the wrong party — § 123-3(G) makes the certificate operator-specific and non-transferable.

#### ⚠ NEW AND MATERIAL — § 123-3(H), the entity-ownership restriction

**This was not in Revision 1 and it is the most important thing in this section.** Nantucket adopted a restriction on entity-owned short-term rentals at the **2024 Annual Town Meeting (Article 60, May 8, 2024)**, tightened at the **September 2024 Special Town Meeting (Article 5)**. As codified, § 123-3(H) makes a dwelling unit **ineligible for a certificate of registration** if it is *"owned by a corporation, partnership, real estate investment trust, or any similar entity which pools funds from investors."* Two definitional qualifiers do most of the work:

- **"CORPORATION"** is defined in § 123-2 as *"All businesses and charitable entities required to file Articles of Incorporation and Annual Reports with the Massachusetts Secretary of State or an equivalent agency of another state, pursuant to G.L. c. 156D, § 2 or G.L. c. 180, § 4, respectively."* That is a **narrow** definition: it reaches business corporations and charitable corporations, not LLCs (which file a certificate of organization under c. 156C) and not trusts (which file nothing).
- **The carve-out:** an **LLC or an S corporation is not treated as a corporation "when every shareholder, partner, and member is a natural person,"** established **by documentation the applicant provides at the time of registration** and from time to time thereafter under regulations the Board of Health may adopt. An **LLC that is taxed as a corporation is** treated as a corporation.
- **Grandfathering does not help us.** § 123-3(K) exempts entities (other than pure corporations) that **paid rooms excise on the unit in a calendar quarter before January 1, 2024 and owned the property before May 7, 2024**. **173 Polpis Road has never been rented**, so no rooms excise was ever paid and the legacy exemption is unavailable by its own terms.

**Why this matters here, specifically.** The chain is Family Trust → Land Trust → fee title, with a Wyoming LLC as operator. On the better reading, nothing in this stack is caught: a nominee realty trust is not a c. 156D corporation, is not a partnership or REIT, and does **not pool funds from investors**; and Third Point Retreats, LLC will not be taxed as a corporation. But the natural-person documentation test is where the friction is, because **the LLC's sole member will be a trust, and the Land Trust's beneficiary will be a trust — not, on their face, "natural persons."** The Board of Health has reportedly rejected some entity applications without publishing its criteria, while sophisticated operators have registered successfully by holding properties in LLCs whose members are individuals. The administrative practice, not the text, is the variable.

**What to do — and this is the one item worth spending attorney time on first:**

1. **Call the Community Liaison before filing anything.** Ask directly how the Health Department applies the natural-person test where the member or beneficiary is a family trust whose beneficiaries are individuals, and what documentation it wants. Get the answer in writing.
2. **Have MA counsel pull the certified current text of Chapter 123** from the Town Clerk. The chapter has been amended at least three times since 2023 and the online code aggregator blocks automated retrieval; the operative wording of § 123-3(H) — particularly whether "member" is read through to trust beneficiaries — should be read off the certified text, not a summary.
3. **Build the documentation package now:** trustee certificate, schedule of beneficiaries (for the Town's confidential review, not for recording), the LLC operating agreement, and a short memo tracing every interest to a named individual.
4. **Sequence the restructuring around this.** If the Health Department signals that a trust-of-a-trust member fails the test, the family has time to restructure — hold the LLC membership interest, or the Land Trust beneficial interest, directly in individual names — **before** the September 1 window opens. That option disappears once a certificate is denied.

*(This is a legitimate counsel item and it stays one. But it has been narrowed from "confirm STR rules" to a single question with a named decision-maker, a known statutory test, a known documentation requirement, and a fallback plan.)*

#### One thing that is **not** current law — occupancy-churn caps

Nantucket has repeatedly considered, and repeatedly declined to adopt, limits on the **number of changes of occupancy** in July and August (proposals capped existing operators at **9** changes, and post-sale owners at **4** changes for five years). Those provisions appear in warrant articles that were **defeated** — most visibly at the November 2023 Special Town Meeting — and **no such limit appears in the codified Chapter 123.** A weekly-turnover July–August calendar is lawful today. **But this is the restriction the anti-STR constituency keeps returning to, and it is the one that would most directly damage this business model** — a 4-turnover cap would remove roughly six peak weeks of inventory. Read every warrant.

### Zoning — the genuinely live risk, and the good news

- **March 14, 2024** — Land Court (Judge Michael Vhay), in *Ward v. Nantucket Zoning Board of Appeals*, ruled that the Town's zoning bylaw did not permit short-term rental as a **principal use** of a primary dwelling. On **March 29, 2024** the Select Board voted **unanimously not to appeal**, choosing to pursue zoning amendments instead.
- **June 6, 2025** — after remand and a further ZBA decision, Judge Vhay again ruled for the plaintiff, holding that the then-current bylaw **did not permit rentals shorter than 31 days of a primary dwelling in the Residential Old Historic (ROH) district**, except for the rental of rooms within an owner-occupied dwelling, and vacating the ZBA's September 2024 decision. **This time the Town did appeal**, and announced enforcement forbearance in the interim.
- **November 4, 2025** — a Special Town Meeting passed **Article 1 by 1,045–421 (≈71%, clearing the two-thirds threshold)**, restoring short-term rental to the zoning bylaw as an **allowed use by right in every zoning district except the commercial-industrial districts**.
- **The Attorney General approved Article 1**, rejecting a challenge that sought to nullify the vote: *"We approve Article 1 because, based on our standard of review, we cannot conclude that Article 1 conflicts with state law."*
- **May 4–5, 2026 Annual Town Meeting — for the first time since 2021, there was no short-term-rental article on the warrant at all.** (10,364 registered voters; 1,332 attending; 13% turnout.) That is the single best indicator available that the islandwide question is, for now, settled.

**Practical read for Third Point.** **173 Polpis Road is zoned LUG-1** (Limited Use General 1) on a **0.92-acre** lot, per the property's marketed listing data — it is **not** in the Residential Old Historic district, which is the downtown historic core. The *Ward* holding was therefore never directly on point for this property, and Article 1 plus the AG's approval put islandwide STR use on firm footing for 2027. **Treat this as favorable and close to settled — but not immutable.** Appellate proceedings on the June 2025 judgment, further challenges, and future Town Meeting articles are all live on an island with an organized anti-STR constituency.

## 3.7 Occupancy, bedrooms, and Title 5 — the correction

**Occupancy capacity on Nantucket is a function of BEDROOMS, not BATHROOMS.** This corrects a mistaken belief in the source materials. Two separate regimes, both bedroom-based, both verified:

**1. Town of Nantucket — the occupancy cap.** Chapter 338 of the Town Regulations provides: **"Occupancy of a short-term rental shall be limited to two people per bedroom plus two additional people in the unit."** ([Town Regulations Ch. 338](https://ecode360.com/39942148))

> **173 Polpis Road: 4 bedrooms × 2 = 8, + 2 = 10.**
> **Maximum lawful occupancy is 10 — which is exactly what the marketing says. "Sleeps 10" is correct and defensible.**

**2. Commonwealth of Massachusetts — Title 5 septic design flow.** Under **310 CMR 15.203**, residential sewage design flow is calculated at **110 gallons per day per bedroom**. The Title 5 consequence follows directly: **adding a bathroom is not an increase in design flow and does not by itself require a septic upgrade; adding a bedroom — or any space meeting Title 5's definition of a bedroom — is an increase in flow and does.** ([310 CMR 15.000](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/310-CMR-15000-septic-systems-title-5); [310 CMR 15.203 via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-15-203); [Mass.gov Title 5 FAQ](https://www.mass.gov/guides/repairs-and-new-construction-for-title-5-frequently-asked-questions))

**Consequences for the plan, stated plainly:**

- **The 4.5th bathroom, or a sixth bathroom, buys the family nothing in occupancy or in septic capacity.** It is a guest-comfort and rate-defensibility investment only. Do not budget it as a capacity play.
- **Only a permitted bedroom raises the cap — and it raises it to 12, not 14.** A fifth bedroom yields **5 × 2 + 2 = 12**. The "sleeps 14" figure that appears in the source documents is **not achievable at five bedrooms** under Chapter 338; reaching 14 would require **six** permitted bedrooms. Marketing must not use 14.
- **A fifth bedroom requires the septic system to support 5 × 110 = 550 gpd** rather than 440 gpd. That is a Board of Health / Title 5 review that must happen **before framing**, not after. Title 5 also defines "bedroom" functionally, so a finished basement room with a closet and egress window can count as a bedroom whether or not it is labelled one — which cuts both ways: it can trigger an unplanned septic upgrade, and it can create an unpermitted-bedroom problem on a future sale.
- **The STR registration's stated occupancy must be amended after any bedroom addition is signed off** — not before, and not in reliance on the renovation being finished on schedule.
