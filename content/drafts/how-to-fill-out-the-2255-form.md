# How to Fill Out the 2255 Form

**By Bilal Khan**

The form for a 2255 motion is 13 pages. It looks like paperwork. It is not paperwork. It is the motion. Whatever you write on it is what the judge and the prosecutor will read, and it is what a court will hold you to if you ever try to file again.

Most people who fill it out make the same mistake. They treat it like a place to argue. It is a place to state facts. Do you know the difference?

## Before you start

The form is called AO 243, "Motion to Vacate, Set Aside, or Correct a Sentence by a Person in Federal Custody." It is issued by the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts. The version I worked from is dated 09/17.[^1] Many district courts post their own copy on their website, and some add local requirements. Get the form from the clerk of the court that sentenced you, or from that court's website, and use that court's version if it has one.

The form's own instructions matter. These are the ones people ignore:[^1]

- You file the form in the **U.S. district court that entered the judgment you are challenging.** Not where you are locked up.
- You can challenge **only one court's judgment** on a single form. If you have judgments from different courts, that is a separate motion for each.
- Type it or write it neatly. If you cannot be read, you will be asked to redo it.
- **You sign it under penalty of perjury.** A false statement of a material fact can get you prosecuted.
- Answer every question. You do not need to cite law. Use extra pages if you need them.
- **Legal arguments do not go on the form.** They go in a separate memorandum, and your court's local rules may limit its length.[^1]

And this one, which the form prints in bold:

**YOU MUST INCLUDE IN THIS MOTION ALL THE GROUNDS FOR RELIEF FROM THE CONVICTION OR SENTENCE THAT YOU CHALLENGE.** If you leave a ground out, you may be barred from raising it later.[^1] That is the most expensive sentence on the form. Combine it with the second-or-successive rule and you understand why you get one real shot. I wrote a separate piece on the deadline, "The 2255 Deadline: How Long Do You Have to File?," and you should read it before you write a word.

## The header

Fill in the court's name and district, your name exactly as you were convicted, your docket or case number, your place of confinement, and your prisoner number. On the caption line, "United States of America" is the movant's opponent, and you are the movant.

## Questions 1 through 7: the basics of your case

These ask for facts you can verify from your judgment and your docket. Do not guess.[^1]

1. The court that entered the judgment, and the criminal docket number.
2. The date of the judgment and the date of sentencing. They are often different days. Use your docket.
3. The length of your sentence.
4. The nature of the crime, every count.
5. Your plea: not guilty, guilty, or no contest. If you pleaded to some counts and not others, say which.
6. If you went to trial: a jury or a judge only.
7. Whether you testified at any pretrial hearing, trial, or post-trial hearing.

## Questions 8 and 9: your direct appeal

Did you appeal? If yes, give the court, the case number, the result, the date, the citation if you have it, and the grounds you raised. If you asked the Supreme Court to review it, the form wants the same details.[^1] This matters because a court can decide that a claim you could have raised on appeal, and did not, is procedurally lost. The form asks the follow-up in Question 12(b), so your answers here and there must match.

## Questions 10 and 11: everything else you have filed

Have you filed any other motion, petition, or application about this same conviction, in any court? If yes, list each one: the court, the number, the date filed, what it was, what you argued, whether you got a hearing, and the result. The form also asks whether you appealed the result of each.[^1] Include the ones that went nowhere. The court will find them on its own, and an incomplete list looks like hiding something.

## Question 12: your grounds

This is the heart of it. The form has room for four grounds and tells you to attach more pages if you have more.[^1]

For each ground you are asked to:

- **(a) State the supporting facts.** The form says: "Do not argue or cite law. Just state the specific facts that support your claim."[^1] Who did what, when, and how you know. If the claim is that your lawyer failed to do something, say what he or she did not do, on what date, and what it cost you. Facts, dates, names, and documents.
- **(b) Say whether you raised it on direct appeal,** and if you did not, explain why.
- **(c) Say whether you raised it in any other post-conviction motion,** and give the details if you did.

Write each ground as its own compartment. One claim per ground. Don't blend them. If your lawyer gave bad advice on the plea and also failed to file a motion, that is two grounds, not one.

I'm not going to list which claims are worth bringing here. That depends on your case. What I can tell you is that the ground has to be a constitutional or legal violation, and the form is not the place to complain about how you feel about the outcome. The most common ground, ineffective assistance of counsel, is a claim about your lawyer's performance and its effect on your case, and that is what the facts have to show.

## Question 13: grounds you have not raised before

Is there any ground in the motion that you have not presented in some federal court? If so, which, and why? The form is asking for an explanation of why this is the first time.[^1] Be honest and specific.

## Question 14: anything still pending

Do you have any motion, petition, or appeal now pending in any court about the conviction you are challenging? If yes, say where, the number, the type, and the issues.[^1]

## Question 15: your lawyers

The form asks for the name and address, if you know it, of each attorney who represented you at each stage: preliminary hearing, arraignment and plea, trial, sentencing, appeal, any post-conviction proceeding, and any appeal from a post-conviction ruling.[^1] If you are claiming ineffective assistance, this page is your map of who did what. Fill it in completely.

## Questions 16 and 17: other sentences

Were you sentenced on more than one count or indictment at the same time? Do you have another sentence to serve after this one? If yes, the form asks for the court, the date, the length, and whether you plan to challenge it.[^1]

## Question 18: timeliness

This is the one that ends motions. If your judgment became final more than a year ago, you must explain why the one-year statute of limitations does not bar your motion.[^1] The form reproduces the four possible start dates from the statute. You need to say which one applies to you and why. That is the entire reason I wrote the deadline piece. If you cannot explain in plain facts why your motion is timely, the government will say it is not, and the court will read your explanation first.

## The relief you are asking for

At the bottom of the last pages there is a line that starts "Therefore, movant asks that the Court grant the following relief." State what you want in plain words, such as vacating the conviction, correcting or reducing the sentence, or another remedy that fits your claim. The form adds "or any other relief to which movant may be entitled."[^1]

## The signature and the date

You sign under penalty of perjury that everything is true and correct, and you state the date the motion was placed in the prison mailing system.[^1] That date matters. An inmate's motion is generally treated as filed when it is put in the institution's mail system, and you will want to be able to prove the date.[^2] Keep a copy.

## Sending it

The form says to send the original and copies to the Clerk of the U.S. District Court at the court's address. If you want a file-stamped copy back, enclose an extra copy and ask the clerk to stamp it and return it.[^1] Make copies of everything before you mail it. Send it in a way that gives you a receipt, and log the date.

If you cannot pay the costs of the motion, such as for an attorney or transcripts, the form has a last page for asking to proceed *in forma pauperis*, and you must attach a certificate signed by a prison officer showing the amount of money the institution holds for you.[^1]

## What gets a motion sent back

- Missing answers. Every question, every time.
- Arguments where the facts belong.
- Missing the deadline, or not explaining why you did not.
- Leaving out a ground you meant to raise.
- No signature, or no date in the prison mail system.
- Filing in the wrong court.

## What to do right now

1. Get the form from your sentencing court's website or its clerk.
2. Pull your docket and copy every date and number you need for Questions 1 through 11.
3. Write your grounds on a separate sheet first, one fact at a time, with dates.
4. Calculate your deadline before anything else.
5. Fill in the form in pencil first if you have to. Then type or print it neatly.
6. Have someone you trust read it against the form's instructions before you mail it. A second pair of eyes catches missing answers.
