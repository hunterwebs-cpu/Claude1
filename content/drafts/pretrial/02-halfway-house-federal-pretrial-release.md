# Halfway House Before Trial: What It Really Means

> [EPIGRAPH PLACEHOLDER. A verified quotation from the federal bench, to be supplied by Bilal. Do not publish without one.]

**By Bilal Khan**

---

<!-- SRC: book p.24 -->
In *Surviving Pretrial*, "pretrial" means the whole time from arrest to the day judgment is entered. Physically, it means one of three places: in custody at a jail, in a halfway house, or out on bail. Most people think the halfway house is a soft landing between jail and freedom. Is it?

## How you end up there

<!-- SRC: book p.140-142 -->
At a detention hearing the judge decides whether you stay in jail while the case is pending or go home. When the judge lets you out, it comes with conditions. The federal system relies less on money and more on conditions than most state courts do. Two of the harshest conditions a judge can pick are house arrest and a halfway house.

<!-- SRC: book p.334 -->
Which one you get often turns on one thing: whether anyone will take responsibility for you. If you have a person who can serve as your third-party custodian, the judge is more likely to send you home. If you don't, the needle moves toward a halfway house.

## What the rules are

<!-- SRC: book p.144 -->
In a halfway house you are given rules just like jail. The difference is that you will probably be allowed to go to a job every day. You have to be back by a set time. Any movement has to be requested. And if you mess up, even slightly, you can be taken straight to jail and have your bail revoked by the judge.

## No credit. None.

<!-- SRC: book p.142, p.340, p.408 -->
This is the part most defendants learn too late. If you sit in jail, every day counts against the sentence you later receive. If you spend that time in a halfway house or on house arrest, you get no credit at all. You can feel like the restrictions are so heavy that they should count. They don't.[^1] The Bureau of Prisons looks at the "Release Status" in your presentence report to work out your jail credit, and only days held in jail (and not in a halfway house or house arrest) count.

## The choice is yours

<!-- SRC: book p.144, p.332 -->
Many defendants who have tried both say jail is the better option. The book is clear that there is no single right answer. What matters is that you decide before the hearing, not during it. If you don't want a halfway house, tell your lawyer right away. Your lawyer can then argue harder for you to go home. The judge will be "really pissed" if you sit through a whole bail hearing in which the judge grants you a halfway house and you then announce that you don't want it.

<!-- SRC: book p.144 -->
And if you do not want to put up with any of it, you can tell your lawyer you want to stay in jail, and he will arrange for you to waive the hearing. Most people would rather have the hearing and whatever freedom it gets them.

## What gets written down about you

<!-- SRC: book p.413 -->
Whatever happens at the halfway house goes into a report. If you were released and placed in a halfway house or on house arrest, the pretrial services information is placed in your presentence report. The probation officer can write about literally anything from your time there. If you caused problems, refused to follow rules, or went to therapy, it will be noted. The prosecutor will use it against you. If something in that section is wrong, tell your lawyer so he can try to get it removed or get your version added.

## If the judge detains you instead

<!-- SRC: book p.144-145 -->
A detention order can be appealed right away. If a magistrate judge held the hearing, you are entitled to review by a district judge, and if that is denied, you can appeal to the Court of Appeals. These appeals take work, so lawyers tend not to mention them. If you went through the hearing and lost, you can demand that your lawyer appeal.

## What to do right now

<!-- SRC: derived from the sections above -->
1. Decide before the hearing whether you would accept a halfway house.
2. Tell your lawyer that decision as early as you can.
3. If someone could take responsibility for you at home, get their name, address and phone number to your lawyer. The third-party custodian guide explains who qualifies.
4. Remember that every day in a halfway house or on house arrest is a day that will not count.
5. If you are placed in one, treat every rule as if a judge will read the report. One may.
