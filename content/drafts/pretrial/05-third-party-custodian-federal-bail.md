# Third-Party Custodian in a Federal Case

> [EPIGRAPH PLACEHOLDER. A verified quotation from the federal bench, to be supplied by Bilal. Do not publish without one.]

**By Bilal Khan**

---

<!-- SRC: book p.333, p.334 -->
If a family member is reading this, you may be the most important person in the courtroom. Whether a defendant goes home or goes to a halfway house can turn on whether one person is willing to stand up and take responsibility. That person is called a third-party custodian. Are you the right one?

## What it is

<!-- SRC: book p.333 -->
The judge may ask a defendant whether he or she would agree to live with a specific person who takes responsibility for them. That person is the third-party custodian.

<!-- SRC: primary source: 18 U.S.C. 3142(c)(1)(B) -->
The Bail Reform Act lists this as one of the conditions a judge can impose: that the defendant "remain in the custody of a designated person, who agrees to assume supervision."[^1]

## Who the court wants

<!-- SRC: book p.333-334 -->
The more closely related the custodian is to the defendant, the better. The reasoning is that a defendant is less likely to let down a family member than a friend. The book lists what a judge and probation look for in a custodian:

1. Someone with no felony convictions.
2. An upstanding member of the community.
3. Someone who is willing to remove items from the home when probation asks, for example firearms, or computers in computer cases.
4. Someone with significant ties to the community.

<!-- SRC: book p.334 -->
If you fit, the defendant's lawyer needs your name, address and telephone number.

## What probation will do

<!-- SRC: book p.142 -->
Expect a call. It is probable that the probation officer will want to interview the person the defendant proposes as responsible for them while they are out of custody.

## What happens if there is no custodian

<!-- SRC: book p.334 -->
If no one can take responsibility for the defendant, the needle ticks closer to a halfway house than to home. That does not mean you cannot argue for home without one. It is just harder.

## Why the defendant should care about this list

<!-- SRC: book p.144, p.340 -->
A halfway house comes with jail-like rules, a curfew, and no credit toward the sentence. House arrest also earns no credit. Because of that, the custodian's role is not a formality. It can change where the defendant spends the next year or more, and how hard those days are.

## What the custodian takes on

<!-- BILAL: the book does not cover what a custodian signs, what they promise to report, or what happens to them if the defendant violates a condition. I have not guessed. See the open questions at the bottom. -->
The statute describes a custodian as someone who "agrees to assume supervision."[^1] The details of what that means in practice, including reporting duties, signing paperwork, and what happens to a custodian when a defendant breaks a condition, vary. A defendant's lawyer or the probation officer can explain what is required in a particular district.

## What to do right now

<!-- SRC: derived -->
1. If you might be the custodian, ask yourself the four questions above honestly: felony record, standing in the community, willingness to remove items from the home, ties to the area.
2. Give the defendant's lawyer your full name, address and telephone number.
3. Be ready for probation to call you and to visit.
4. Do not promise anything to the judge or probation that you are not certain you can do.
5. Ask the lawyer to tell you in writing exactly what the custodian must do.
