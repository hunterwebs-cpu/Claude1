# How to Send a Book to Someone in Federal Prison

You order a book. You type in the address. A week later it comes back, or it never arrives, or the person inside is told it "was not approved" and nobody tells them why. Families lose money and weeks doing exactly this, and the person inside loses the one thing they were waiting for.

The rules are not complicated once you know who makes them. The problem is that there are two different sets, and most families are dealing with the wrong one. Which one applies to your person?

## The two places a federal defendant can be

**In a Bureau of Prisons facility.** That means a federal prison, a federal correctional institution, or a federal detention center run by the BOP. The BOP has one national policy for books and other publications, and it applies the same way in every one of its institutions. This is the part I can give you in detail.

**In a county jail under a U.S. Marshals contract.** Most people waiting on a federal case are held here, not in a BOP facility. The BOP's book rules do not govern these jails. Each jail writes its own. Some accept paperbacks from an approved vendor. Some accept nothing, not even from Amazon.<!-- VERIFY: Bilal to confirm from experience that some county jails refuse books entirely or have replaced them with tablets. --> I was held in a string of them, and the rules were never the same from one jail to the next.

For a county jail, the only answer that counts is the jail's. Call the jail, ask for the mail room or the inmate services desk, and ask three questions: Do you accept books? Who is allowed to send them? Is there a limit on how many at one time? Get the answer in writing if you can. Many jails post an inmate handbook on their website.

## What the Bureau of Prisons requires

The national rule is Program Statement 5266.11, "Incoming Publications," which follows the federal regulation at 28 C.F.R. § 540.71.[^1] Here is what it says in plain English.

**Hardcover books.** In every BOP institution, an inmate may receive a hardcover book only from the publisher, a book club, or a bookstore.[^1]

**Softcover (paperback) books.** In medium security, high security, and administrative institutions, the same rule applies: paperbacks only from the publisher, a book club, or a bookstore. In minimum and low security institutions, an inmate may receive softcover publications, other than newspapers, from any source.[^1]

**The sender's address.** It must be clearly identified on the outside of the package.[^1]

**The warden can reject it, but only for specific reasons.** The warden may reject a publication only if it is detrimental to the security, good order, or discipline of the institution, or if it might facilitate criminal activity. The policy says the warden may NOT reject a book solely because its content is religious, philosophical, political, social, or sexual, or because it is unpopular or repugnant.[^1] The reasons the policy does list include material describing how to build weapons or explosives, material that encourages or instructs in criminal activity, and sexually explicit material that poses a threat.[^1] Only the warden, or the acting warden, can make that call.

What this means for you: **DO NOT HAND A PAPERBACK TO A FRIEND TO MAIL, AND DO NOT SEND IT FROM YOUR OWN HOUSE.** At most facilities the book has to come from a publisher or a real bookstore. If you buy through a store that ships directly to the facility, you have solved the problem the rules are worried about, which is contraband.

## Amazon, and the trap in the fine print

Amazon is the route almost everyone uses, because it ships to an address you type in and it is a bookstore. When you order:

1. Sign in to your own Amazon account and add the facility as a shipping address. Use the **mailing address the facility publishes**, not the street address of the building. Each BOP institution's page on bop.gov lists it.
2. Put the person's **full name and register number** exactly as the facility requires. A missing register number is the most common reason a package sits in the mail room.
3. Buy the **paperback**, and make sure it is sold and shipped by Amazon itself. A copy from an outside seller who ships from their own address may not count as coming from a bookstore, and the facility can send it back.[^2]
4. Don't send hardcovers unless the facility says yes, and only from the publisher or a bookstore.
5. Keep the order confirmation. If the book is rejected, you will need it to appeal or to get a refund.

The Kindle edition of a book costs much less, and it is useful for you as the supporter, because you can read it first and know what you are sending. But you cannot ship a Kindle file to a cell. The person inside needs the paperback.

## If the book is rejected

You have rights here, and most families never use them.

- **The warden has to tell the inmate in writing, with reasons.** The notice must point to the specific material considered objectionable.[^1]
- **You, as the sender, get a copy of the rejection letter.** You are also told you may ask for an independent review by writing to the BOP Regional Director within 20 days of receiving that letter.[^1] Do not let that 20 days pass.
- **The inmate can appeal through the Administrative Remedy Program.** The institution holds the rejected book for 20 days from the date the inmate is notified, so that an appeal can be filed.[^1]
- Keep your Amazon order confirmation. It shows who sold and shipped the book.

Books also take time to move through a mail room. Ask the facility how long inspection usually takes before you decide the package is lost.

## Printed pages

You can also print and mail pages. Every article on this site is built to be printed and mailed. Letters and printed pages travel through the regular mail, which follows different rules from a package of books. Ask the facility what it accepts.

## What to do right now

1. Find out which kind of facility your person is in: a BOP institution or a county jail.
2. For a BOP institution, look it up on bop.gov, copy the exact mailing address, and note the security level.
3. For a county jail, call and ask the three questions above. Ask for the rule in writing.
4. Order the paperback through Amazon, shipped and sold by Amazon, to the correct address with the register number.
5. Keep the order number, and call after two weeks if it has not arrived.
