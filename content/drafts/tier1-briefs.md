## 1. What happens after a federal indictment

- **Slug:** `what-happens-after-a-federal-indictment`
- **Search phrases (autocomplete, multiple engines):** what is a federal indictment · federal indictment process · what happens after federal indictment · how long does a federal indictment take
- **Reader:** family member or the defendant, in the first days or weeks. High urgency, high information hunger.
- **Working `seo_title`:** What Happens After a Federal Indictment
- **Working `description`:** What an indictment is, what happens next, and how a federal case moves from arraignment to trial or plea. A plain-English timeline for defendants and families.
- **Type of opening (VOICE §2):** A, procedural. Correct the assumption that an indictment means guilt or means a trial is coming, then pull the rug.
- **Outline (H2s as questions):**
  1. What is an indictment, and what is it not? (a charge, decided by a grand jury on the prosecutor's evidence; not proof)
  2. Indictment vs. criminal complaint vs. information (how the case starts)
  3. What happens in the first hearing (initial appearance, arraignment, plea entry, detention request)
  4. What happens between arraignment and trial (discovery, motions, plea talks, superseding indictments)
  5. How long does it take? (real ranges, with the Speedy Trial Act clock and why it rarely runs on schedule)
  6. What families can do this week (lawyer, docket, calls, what not to say on recorded lines)
- **Personal hooks available:** grand jury "ham sandwich"; charged by complaint and never received a target letter; "I was never told I could schedule a plea hearing at any time"; the unauthorized-plea-hearing story.
- **Verify from primary sources:** 18 U.S.C. § 3161 (Speedy Trial Act deadlines and exclusions); Fed. R. Crim. P. 5, 10, 11 and 7 (initial appearance, arraignment, plea, indictment/information); Fed. R. Crim. P. 6 (grand jury secrecy); Bureau of Justice Statistics figures for case-processing time (bjs.ojp.gov felony case processing) for a "how long" range.
- **Internal links (spelled out in prose):** the first-48-hours article, the PACER article, the lookup guide, the plea-deal article.
- **Length:** 4–8 pages. **Overlap with book:** heavy in parts. Keep it a timeline and point to the book for depth.
- **Risks:** do not state timelines as promises. Sealed indictments exist.

- **Outline:**

## 3. Federal detention hearing and bail

- **Slug:** `federal-detention-hearing-and-bail`
- **Search phrases:** what is a federal detention hearing · what happens at a detention hearing · is there bail for federal crimes · how does federal bail work · federal pretrial release
- **Reader:** family in the first week; the defendant if they can be reached.
- **Working `seo_title`:** Federal Detention Hearings and Bail Explained
- **Working `description`:** How a federal detention hearing works, how federal bail differs from state bail, and what a family can do to help. A plain-English guide.
- **Opening type:** B or C. VOICE.md's own model line fits: "I have yet to meet a defendant who isn't concerned about the detention hearing." Then the ALL CAPS line: approach bail as if the presumption applies against you.
- **Outline:**
  1. What a detention hearing is, and why it is not a "bail hearing" the way state court works
  2. The presumption cases (what triggers a presumption of detention)
  3. What the judge weighs: risk of flight, danger to the community, the nature of the charge, the evidence, history
  4. Conditions of release (third-party custodian, property bond, curfew, monitoring) and what a family member must do to qualify
  5. Pretrial Services: the interview, and why to be careful with it
  6. If detention is ordered: options to reopen, and appeal
  7. Where the person will be held (Marshals, contract county jails) and how to reach them
- **Personal hooks:** the "detention hearing is where the case's trajectory quietly bends" point already in the site's first-48-hours piece; "prepare as if you are going to trial"; the sister who found the U.S. Marshal in charge of prisoner housing (a family-reach story).
- **Verify:** Bail Reform Act, 18 U.S.C. § 3142 (release and detention factors, presumptions, conditions), § 3145 (review and appeal of release or detention orders); the Federal Judiciary's pretrial services pages; the local district's practices, since they vary.
- **Cross-check:** the site already has "Your Rights in Pretrial Detention" (conditions of confinement). This page is about getting a person out or arguing to; link the two both ways and avoid duplicating.
- **Length:** 6–9 pages. **Overlap with book:** detention hearing is covered in the book. Give a solid explainer, and leave the strategy detail for the book.
- **Risks:** bail practice differs a lot by district. Say so. Do not promise release.
