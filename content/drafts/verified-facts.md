### 28 U.S.C. § 2255 (Cornell LII page)
- (a): a prisoner in custody under sentence of a federal court, claiming the right to be released because the sentence was imposed in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States, or the court lacked jurisdiction, or the sentence exceeded the maximum authorized by law, or is otherwise subject to collateral attack, "may move the court which imposed the sentence to vacate, set aside or correct the sentence."
- (b): notice to the U.S. attorney, and a prompt hearing "unless the motion and the files and records of the case conclusively show that the prisoner is entitled to no relief."
- (e): a habeas petition will not be entertained if the applicant has failed to apply to the sentencing court, "unless it also appears that the remedy by motion is inadequate or ineffective to test the legality of his detention."
- (f): "A 1-year period of limitation shall apply to a motion under this section. The limitation period shall run from the latest of— (1) the date on which the judgment of conviction becomes final; (2) the date on which the impediment to making a motion created by governmental action in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States is removed, if the movant was prevented from making a motion by such governmental action; (3) the date on which the right asserted was initially recognized by the Supreme Court, if that right has been newly recognized by the Supreme Court and made retroactively applicable to cases on collateral review; or (4) the date on which the facts supporting the claim or claims presented could have been discovered through the exercise of due diligence."
- (g): the court may appoint counsel, governed by 18 U.S.C. § 3006A.
- (h): a second or successive motion must be certified by a panel of the appropriate court of appeals to contain (1) newly discovered evidence that, if proven and viewed in light of the evidence as a whole, would be sufficient to establish by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder would have found the movant guilty; or (2) a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court, that was previously unavailable.

### Form AO 243 (Rev. 09/17), uscourts.gov, 13 pages
- Instructions: (1) for a person serving a federal sentence; (2) file in the district court that entered the judgment; (3) typed or neatly written; (4) tell the truth and sign, false statement of material fact may be prosecuted for perjury; (5) answer all questions, no need to cite law, extra pages allowed, legal arguments go in a separate memorandum which may be subject to local page limits; (6) if you cannot pay costs, you may ask to proceed in forma pauperis and must submit a certificate signed by an officer at the institution showing the amount of money held for you; (7) challenge the judgment of only one court per motion; (8) send the original and copies to the Clerk, and enclose an extra copy to get a file-stamped copy back; (9) CAUTION: include all grounds and the facts supporting each, or you may be barred from presenting additional grounds later; (10) capital cases entitled to counsel.
- Questions: 1 court and docket number; 2 date of judgment and sentencing; 3 length of sentence; 4 nature of crime, all counts; 5 plea; 6 trial type; 7 whether you testified; 8–9 direct appeal details and certiorari; 10–11 prior motions, petitions or applications concerning this judgment; 12 grounds one through four, each with (a) supporting facts ("Do not argue or cite law. Just state the specific facts that support your claim."), (b) direct appeal of the ground, (c) post-conviction proceedings on the ground; 13 any ground not previously presented in a federal court and why; 14 pending motions or appeals; 15 name and address of each attorney at each stage (preliminary hearing, arraignment and plea, trial, sentencing, appeal, post-conviction, appeal from post-conviction ruling); 16 sentenced on more than one count or indictment at the same time; 17 any future sentence to serve; 18 timeliness, with the § 2255(f) text reproduced; then "Therefore, movant asks that the Court grant the following relief"; then a declaration under penalty of perjury and the date the motion "was placed in the prison mailing system."

### BOP Program Statement 5266.11, Incoming Publications (bop.gov/policy/progstat/5266_011.pdf, dated 11/9/2011)
- § 540.71(a)(1): at all Bureau institutions, an inmate may receive hardcover publications and newspapers only from the publisher, a book club, or a bookstore; the sender's address must be clearly identified on the outside of the package.
- (a)(2): at medium security, high security, and administrative institutions, softcover publications only from the publisher, a book club, or a bookstore.
- (a)(3): at minimum and low security institutions, softcover publications (other than newspapers) from any source.
- (a)(4): the Unit Manager may make an exception if the publication is no longer available from those sources.
- (b): the Warden may reject a publication only if determined detrimental to the security, good order, or discipline of the institution or if it might facilitate criminal activity; may not reject solely because its content is religious, philosophical, political, social or sexual, or unpopular or repugnant. Listed grounds include depicting or describing construction or use of weapons, ammunition, or bombs; encouraging or instructing in the commission of criminal activity; and sexually explicit material which by its nature or content poses a threat to security, good order, or discipline or facilitates criminal activity.
- Notice: the Warden must promptly advise the inmate in writing of the decision and reasons, with reference to the specific material; the inmate may appeal under the Administrative Remedy Program. The publisher or sender is given a copy of the rejection letter and advised they may obtain an independent review by writing to the Regional Director within 20 days of receipt. The rejected publication is retained for 20 days from the date the inmate is notified.

### BOP inmate locator (bop.gov/inmateloc)
- Covers federal inmates "from 1982 to the present." Search by number (BOP register, DCDC, FBI, or INS) or by name. The page states a person shown as released or not in BOP custody "may still be in the custody of some other correctional/criminal justice system/law enforcement entity, or on parole or supervised release," and that release dates may be inaccurate because of ongoing sentence recalculations under the First Step Act.

### PACER fees (uscourts.gov Electronic Public Access Fee Schedule, via search result summary)
- $0.10 per page; $3.00 cap per document (30 pages); the cap does not apply to name-search results, non-case-specific reports, or transcripts; fees waived if a user's charges are $30 or less in a quarter. Registration is free.
